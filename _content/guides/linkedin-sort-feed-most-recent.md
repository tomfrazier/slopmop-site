On desktop, click the **Sort by** menu just above your feed and choose **Recent**. That lasts for the current session only. To make it permanent, go to **Me**, then **Settings & Privacy**, then **Preferred Feed View** under **General preferences**, and choose **Most recent posts**.

## Before you start

LinkedIn shows **Top** posts by default: a feed ranked by your interests and activity, with recommendations from outside your network mixed in. **Recent** shows posts from people you follow in reverse chronological order ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1480504)).

There's a regional difference worth knowing. LinkedIn says that in the European Economic Area, the Recent view shows all updates from your network in time order. Outside the EEA, it shows the updates LinkedIn judges most relevant to you, in time order. So outside Europe, Recent is a re-ordering of a filtered feed, and some posts will still be left out.

The control is mainly a desktop one. LinkedIn's feed help says sorting is only available on the desktop site, and a newer page says that outside the EEA it "may only be available on desktop" ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/62570), [LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1339724)). If you mostly read LinkedIn in the app, this guide may not help you.

## 1. Switch to Recent for one session

1. Go to your LinkedIn homepage on a computer.
2. Click the **Sort by** dropdown above your feed.
3. Choose **Recent**.
4. Scroll as usual.

Next time you sign in, the feed goes back to your default ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1339724)). That's handy for a quick catch-up on what people you follow posted this morning, without changing anything else.

## 2. Sort your LinkedIn feed by most recent for good

1. Click the **Me** icon at the top of the LinkedIn homepage.
2. Choose **Settings & Privacy**. The **Account preferences** page opens.
3. Under **General preferences**, click **Preferred Feed View**.
4. Choose **Most recent posts**.

The change saves itself, and from then on the feed always opens in that view ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1480504)). LinkedIn's help page uses "Top" and "Recent" in the menu and "Most relevant posts" and "Most recent posts" in Settings for the same pair, so expect both names.

With **Most recent posts** set, LinkedIn says your interests and activity data won't be used to sort the feed or make recommendations. Your ad settings are separate and don't change ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1480504)).

## What Recent fixes

- **Posts from strangers.** Recent draws from people you follow, so most "recommended for you" posts from accounts you've never heard of drop away.
- **The same popular post, again.** LinkedIn says how often a post is viewed or engaged with is one of the signals behind Top ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1339724)), so a busy post can keep appearing. Recent moves on.
- **Feedback loops.** If one hate-click taught the feed to send you more of a format, setting Recent as your default stops your activity being used for sorting.

## What it doesn't fix

- **Bait from people you follow.** If a connection posts a keyword giveaway, Recent shows it to you in order with everything else.
- **Prolific posters.** A chronological feed favours whoever posts most. One person posting six times a day can take over the top of your feed on a quiet morning.
- **Ads.** Promoted posts still appear. LinkedIn says none of its ad settings stops ads altogether ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1342443)).
- **The app**, where the option may be missing, as above.

In practice, Recent works best after you've pruned who you follow. The [clean-up guide](/guides/linkedin/linkedin-clean-up-feed/) covers unfollowing without disconnecting, which is the step that makes a chronological feed readable.

## A routine that suits Recent

Chronological feeds reward a little upkeep. Once a month, scroll the first screen of Recent and note who fills it. Anyone who appears three or more times with posts you didn't read is a candidate for unfollowing. Anyone whose posts you'd hate to miss is worth a visit to their profile now and then, since a busy morning can push them below the fold. Ten minutes a month keeps Recent closer to a list of people you chose than a list of people who post most.

## Recent or Top

| If you mainly want to… | Use |
|---|---|
| See everything the people you chose have posted today | Recent |
| Find posts from outside your network on topics you follow | Top |
| Keep LinkedIn from learning from what you click | Recent, as your default |
| Read on your phone | Top, since sorting may not be available there |

## If that doesn't work

- **The Sort by menu isn't there.** You may be in the app, or your account may label the menu differently. Try the Settings route above on a computer.
- **The feed flips back to Top.** The menu setting only lasts a session. Set **Preferred Feed View** to make it stick.
- **Recent still looks filtered.** If you're outside the EEA, that's expected: LinkedIn says the Recent view there still picks the most relevant updates, then sorts them by time.
- **Recent is too quiet.** Follow a few more people whose posts you'd read in full, or switch back to Top for a session when you want to explore.
- **Recent is mostly one person.** Unfollow them, or use **Hide posts by [name]** in the three-dot menu on one of their posts. The [engagement bait guide](/guides/linkedin/linkedin-filter-engagement-bait/) covers the other menu options.
