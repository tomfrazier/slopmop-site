Press Save on a story to add it to your Reading list, and make a few named lists in your Library for the subjects you return to. Then read from your lists instead of the feed: the feed is where you find candidates, the list is where you decide what earns your time. You can also save other readers' public lists, which is often the quickest way to find good writers on a subject.

## Why Medium lists help with filler

Most low-value reading happens on impulse. A headline grabs you, you open it, and ten minutes later you've finished something you'd never have chosen with a minute to think. Saving puts a gap between seeing and reading. Stories that looked irresistible in the feed often look thin a day later in a list, and you can delete them without opening them.

That's the habit this guide sets up: skim the feed, save the candidates, read from the list.

## Before you start

You need to be signed in. Lists work on desktop and in the app, but some controls sit in different places; the steps say where they differ. Medium updates its interface now and then, so a button may be labelled or placed a little differently.

## 1. Save a story to your Medium Reading list

1. On a story preview in your feed, or on the story page, click the Save button (a bookmark icon in most versions).
2. Medium's [help page on lists](https://help.medium.com/hc/en-us/articles/214993247-Create-and-manage-lists) says the story goes to your Reading list by default.
3. In the same menu you can tick any other lists you want it in.

The Reading list is the default and can't be deleted, according to Medium. Treat it as an inbox: everything goes in, and you clear it regularly.

## 2. Create a list for a subject

1. Open Library from the homepage sidebar (on desktop) or from the app's navigation.
2. Click New list.
3. Enter a name and, if you like, a description.
4. Choose whether the list is public or private.
5. Create it. It now appears as an option whenever you press Save.

Medium's help page says private lists can't be accessed by anyone but you. Public lists can be shared with anyone and show on your profile page under the Lists tab.

Keep the number small. Three to six lists is plenty for most readers: one per subject you actually read about, plus one for pieces you'd send to a colleague. Dozens of lists become another feed to sort.

## 3. Add notes to stories in a list

Medium's help page says each list lets you add a note that appears above a story in that list. Use notes for the reason you saved it: "the section on retry budgets", "check the numbers in table 2", "send to Priya before the planning meeting". When you come back a week later, the note tells you whether the story still matters.

On a public list, notes are visible to anyone who opens it, so write them for other readers too.

## 4. Manage, reorder and delete

1. Open the list.
2. Use its three-dot menu. Medium's help page says you can edit the list's details, switch it between private and public, reorder stories and delete the list from there.
3. In the app, Medium says the three-dot menu on a list is where you change its privacy.

To remove a single story, open the list and use the story's own menu, or press Save on the story again and untick the list. The exact control may vary by version.

## 5. Save someone else's list

Other readers' public lists are an underused filter. A list called "Postgres performance, the good ones" made by an engineer who writes well on the subject has already been vetted by a person.

1. Open the public list.
2. Click Save list.
3. Find it later in your Library under Saved lists.

Before saving, open two or three stories from the list. If the list's owner saves everything with the right keyword in it, the list is no better than a topic feed.

## A weekly routine that works

| When | What to do | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Scrolling the feed | Save candidates; don't open them | Seconds each |
| Once a day | Read the top two or three from your Reading list | 20–30 minutes |
| Once a week | Delete anything you no longer want; move keepers to subject lists | 5 minutes |
| Once a month | Prune lists you never open | 2 minutes |

When you delete a saved story without reading it, ask why you saved it. If the answer is the headline, that's a [scroll-stopper](/patterns/scroll-stopper/) doing its job on you, and the writer may be worth muting.

## Tell the feed what you liked

Lists are for you, but Medium also learns from what you follow and read. When a saved story turns out to be excellent, follow the writer from the story page. When one turns out to be filler, back out and use show less like this on similar stories from the feed. The [recommendations clean-up guide](/guides/medium/medium-clean-up-recommendations/) and the [follow guide](/guides/medium/medium-follow-topics-vs-writers/) cover both.

## Using lists to check quality

A list also gives you a second, calmer look at a story. When you sit down to read from your list, run the [two-minute check](/guides/medium/medium-spot-low-quality-articles/) before committing. For member-only stories, the [preview checklist](/guides/medium/medium-member-only-worth-it/) helps you decide which saved stories deserve your limited reads.

## If that doesn't work

If your Reading list grows faster than you read it, you're saving too freely. Set a cap, say 20 stories, and delete the oldest when you go over. If a saved story has disappeared, the writer may have deleted or unpublished it; lists can only hold stories that still exist.
