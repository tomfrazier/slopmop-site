Look for proof that someone used the product: their own photos, measurements, a description of how they tested, and drawbacks. A review that only lists features and praise, with a "Check price" button under every item, was probably written from the product page. If a "best" list can't say why its winner beat the others, with evidence, treat it as advertising.

## Why review sites are hard to judge

Most review sites earn money through affiliate links: when you buy through their link, the seller pays them a commission. That's a reasonable business, and plenty of careful testers rely on it. It also means a page can make money without anyone testing anything, as long as it ranks and you click.

Google's guidance helps here, because it spells out what a good review should contain. Its [advice for review writers](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/ecommerce/write-high-quality-reviews) asks for evidence "of your own experience" such as visuals, "quantitative measurements", benefits and drawbacks "based on your own original research", and, when something is called the best, "first-hand supporting evidence". Its [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) name the opposite, "thin affiliation": affiliate pages whose product descriptions and reviews are "copied directly from the original merchant without any original content or added value". Those two documents make a good checklist for readers too.

## Six checks

### 1. Whose photos are these?

Real testers photograph the product on their desk, in their kitchen, with their hand in the shot, often scuffed or half-assembled. Thin review pages reuse the maker's studio images on white backgrounds. If every image could be from the brand's own website, nobody needed to own the product to write the page.

### 2. Are there numbers the writer measured?

Measured numbers are hard to fake in bulk and easy to check. Look for figures the writer could only get by testing: noise in decibels, battery life in hours under a described load, weight on their own scale, how long a kettle took to boil a litre. Specs copied from the box don't count, because anyone can paste those.

:::slop
This powerful vacuum offers exceptional suction and long-lasting battery life, making it perfect for busy households.
:::
:::fine
On the medium setting it ran for 38 minutes before cutting out, enough for our two-bedroom flat with about 5 minutes to spare. On max it lasted 11.
:::

### 3. Does it say what's wrong?

Every product has trade-offs. A review that finds none is either not looking or not willing to say. The [no-catch promises](/patterns/no-catch-promises/) tell, all benefit and no cost, is the clearest sign of a page written to sell.

:::slop
Lightweight, durable and incredibly versatile, this backpack is the perfect choice for every traveller.
:::
:::fine
The side pockets are too shallow for a 1-litre bottle, and the hip belt isn't removable. Otherwise it was the most comfortable bag we carried.
:::

### 4. Does it compare like with like?

Google's guidance asks reviewers to explain "what sets something apart from its competitors" and which option suits which use. A useful "best" list tells you why item 1 beat item 2 and who should pick item 3 anyway. A thin one gives each product its own paragraph of praise and never mentions the others.

### 5. Is there a test method?

Good review sites explain how they test, either on the page or on a linked methodology page: how long they used each product, in what conditions, what they measured, and how many they tried. Look for a date, too. "We tested 12 kettles in June 2026" is checkable. "Our experts spent hours researching" is [empty praise](/patterns/empty-praise/) aimed at the site itself.

### 6. How does the site make money, and does it say so?

Look for a disclosure near the top of the page or in the footer explaining affiliate links. A disclosure shows the site is honest about money. It says nothing about quality. Its absence on a page full of buy buttons is a bad sign. Beyond that, check whether the site ever recommends against buying something, or picks a cheap option over an expensive one. A site that always lands on the priciest item is worth questioning.

## Quick signs at a glance

| Usually good | Usually weak |
|---|---|
| Own photos, with wear and mess | Maker's studio images only |
| Measured numbers with conditions | Specs copied from the box |
| Specific drawbacks | "Pros" list five long, "cons" list one word |
| Named test period and method | "Our experts researched hundreds of products" |
| Clear reasons one product beat another | Separate praise paragraphs, no comparison |
| Updated when products change | "Best of 2026" with no 2026 products |
| Says when not to buy | Every item "perfect for everyone" |

## Things that tell you less than you'd think

- **A long list.** "37 best headphones" sounds thorough. A short list with real testing is usually more useful.
- **Star ratings.** A site's own 4.8 out of 5 means little without the measurements behind it.
- **The year in the headline.** Pages often change the year without changing the picks. Check whether the products are current.
- **Polished writing and [hype words](/patterns/hype-words/).** Polish says nothing about testing. Hype words are a mild warning.
- **A big brand name.** Large publishers run thin review pages too. Judge the page, not the masthead.

## When you find a good one

Good review sites are worth keeping. Bookmark them, and for future searches add the site directly: `site:example-reviews.com cordless vacuum` searches only that site, using the operator from [How to get fewer slop results in Google search](/guides/websites/web-fewer-slop-search-results/). For a site that keeps failing these checks, [block it](/guides/websites/web-block-sites-search-results/) where your search engine allows.

For claims that matter, such as safety ratings or health effects, trace them to the original source using the steps in [How to check a blog post's sources](/guides/websites/web-check-blog-post-sources/). Slop Mop is coming soon to websites; the checks above work on any review page today.
