Judge a Medium story on what it gives you, not on a guess about who typed it. Generated filler shows itself through generic claims, stock transitions, decorative metaphors and no first-hand detail, and those are reasons to leave whether a model or a person wrote it. A story drafted with AI and checked by someone who knows the subject can be worth your time.

## What Medium's rules say

Medium has a written [AI content policy](https://help.medium.com/hc/en-us/articles/22576852947223-Artificial-Intelligence-AI-content-policy). In brief:

- **AI-generated writing**, which Medium describes as writing where most of the content comes from an AI tool with little or no editing, fact-checking or changes, can't be put behind the Partner Program paywall, whether it's disclosed or not.
- **AI-assisted writing** is allowed, but stories that use it must be clearly labelled. Coverage of the policy from [Plagiarism Today](https://www.plagiarismtoday.com/2024/04/11/medium-sets-new-policies-on-ai-generated-writing/) reported that writers were expected to disclose within the first two paragraphs.
- **Undisclosed** AI writing is limited to the writer's own network of followers.
- **AI images** are allowed if captioned as AI-generated.

Medium's paywall [eligibility page](https://help.medium.com/hc/en-us/articles/31090080813591-Content-not-eligible-for-the-paywall) says tools used for outlining, spelling, grammar or fact checking don't count against a story. The Boost tier, where curators pick stories for extra reach, isn't open to AI-generated stories according to Medium's [Boost eligibility page](https://help.medium.com/hc/en-us/articles/26716922635159-Which-stories-are-eligible-to-be-Boosted). The policy took effect on 1 May 2024.

For readers, this has two uses. A disclosure line near the top is the writer telling you how the piece was made, as Medium asks, so there's nothing to hold against it. And the rules rely on writers declaring honestly and Medium enforcing them, so you'll still meet undisclosed generated stories in your feed.

## Why guessing authorship is a weak filter

Smooth, plain prose is also what careful human writers produce. Technical writers, people writing in a second language and anyone editing toward a strict house style can all sound "too clean" to a suspicious reader, and a guess can't be checked. Treating every smooth paragraph as machine-made is what people call [AI suspicion](/glossary/ai-suspicion/), and publicly accusing writers on that basis has a name too, [AI shaming](/glossary/ai-shaming/).

The question you need answered is narrower and easier: is this story going to tell me something? That you can check from the text.

## What generated filler looks like

These patterns show up often in low-effort generated stories. Each one alone is weak evidence; several together are a strong signal.

### Stock transitions and hedges

"Furthermore", "Moreover", "It's worth noting that", "In today's fast-paced world" and a closing "In conclusion" are [stiff phrasing](/patterns/stiff-phrasing/). Models reach for them to link paragraphs that don't really follow from one another.

### Decorative metaphor

Stories that call a subject "a tapestry", "a symphony" or "a delicate dance" are reaching for tone when they have no claim to make. That's [flowery prose](/patterns/flowery-prose/).

:::slop
In the ever-evolving tapestry of modern software, testing is a delicate dance between speed and quality, a symphony of tools and practices.
:::
:::fine
We run unit tests on every commit (about 90 seconds) and the slow browser suite only on merges to main (about 14 minutes). That split cut our waiting time without losing the bugs the browser suite catches.
:::

### Balanced lists with no position

Generated stories often lay out "pros and cons" or "key considerations" with equal weight and never say which matters more. A writer with experience usually picks one.

### Confident generalities

"Many experts agree", "studies show" and "it is widely recognised" with no expert, study or link. Ask which expert and which study. If the story can't say, it doesn't know.

### No mess

Real experience leaves traces: the step that failed, the cost, the number that was disappointing. Generated stories describe a world where everything works as advertised.

:::slop
By implementing these strategies, you can transform your morning routine and achieve lasting productivity gains.
:::
:::fine
The 6am start lasted eleven days. What stuck was smaller: phone in the kitchen overnight, which got me about 40 extra minutes of reading a week.
:::

## Signs of useful AI-assisted writing

A story can use AI and still pass. Look for:

- a disclosure near the top, as Medium's policy asks
- specifics only the writer could supply: their numbers, their codebase, their classroom, their dates
- a position: a recommendation, a ranking or a "don't do this"
- sources you can click and check
- an ending with a limit or a next step

If those are present, how the draft was produced matters much less than what's in it.

## A quick routine for your feed

1. Read the first two paragraphs. Note any disclosure, and note whether the opening says something specific.
2. Scan one middle section for the tells above.
3. Look for a single first-hand detail. If there isn't one by the halfway point, leave.
4. Back in the feed, use show less like this on the stories that failed, and mute writers who publish filler daily.

The [two-minute check](/guides/medium/medium-spot-low-quality-articles/) is the longer version of step 2, and the [listicles guide](/guides/medium/medium-seo-listicles/) covers the format generated filler uses most.

## If that doesn't work

If generated stories dominate one topic, unfollow or mute the topic and follow a few writers you trust instead, as in the [follow guide](/guides/medium/medium-follow-topics-vs-writers/). You can also read the Following feed, which shows only writers and publications you chose. Slop Mop is coming soon to Medium; like this guide, it judges the writing and isn't an AI detector.

The broader problem has a name: [AI slop](/glossary/ai-slop/). On Medium it's best handled the same way as any other low-value writing, one story and one source at a time.
