Use **Not interested** when one kind of post bothers you, **snooze** when a person, Page or group needs a 30-day break, and **unfollow** when you want their posts gone from your feed but want to stay friends or stay in the group. Facebook says snoozing doesn't notify anyone, and its help pages mention no notification for the other two. Block is a safety tool rather than a feed tool.

## The three feed controls side by side

| Control | What it hides | How long | Still friends or a member? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Not interested | Fewer posts like this one | Feeds into ranking over time | Yes |
| Snooze | All their posts in your feed | 30 days | Yes |
| Unfollow | All their posts in your feed | Until you reconnect | Yes |

Snoozed profiles, Pages and groups aren't notified ([Facebook Help](https://www.facebook.com/help/538433456491590)). Unfollowing keeps you friends, and you'll still be a member of a group you unfollow ([Facebook Help](https://www.facebook.com/help/268028706671439)). When you reconnect later, the person, Page or group isn't told either ([Facebook Help](https://www.facebook.com/help/146865632052048)).

## How to choose

- **Your cousin posts 40 holiday photos a day for a fortnight.** Snooze. In 30 days the holiday is over and they're back.
- **A school friend shares nothing but "share if you agree" posts.** Unfollow. You stay friends and stop seeing the shares.
- **Your feed keeps showing a kind of post, from different sources.** Not interested on each one. Snoozing one Page won't stop the next.
- **A Page you followed once now posts reaction-fishing pictures.** Unfollow the Page, or if you never followed it, use Not interested and the steps in the [suggested posts guide](/guides/facebook/facebook-reduce-suggested-posts/).
- **Someone is harassing you.** Feed controls only change your feed. Block them from their profile, or under **Settings & privacy**, **Settings**, then **Blocking** ([Facebook Help](https://www.facebook.com/help/371675846332829)), and report if needed.

## 1. Not interested

1. On the post, tap or click the three dots at the top right.
2. Choose **Not interested**.

Facebook says this replaces the old **Hide post** option and tells it you'd like less content like this ([Facebook Help](https://www.facebook.com/help/268028706671439)). In the app, some posts also show a **Hide** button next to the three dots, with **Undo** if you change your mind. On a computer, use the three-dot menu.

If you overdo it, choose **Interested** on posts you do want more of.

## 2. Snooze for 30 days

1. On one of their posts, tap or click the three dots at the top right.
2. Choose **Snooze [Name] for 30 days**.
3. To undo straight away, tap **Undo** in the confirmation message.

([Facebook Help](https://www.facebook.com/help/538433456491590))

### End a snooze early

**In the app:**

1. Tap the menu at the top left, scroll down, tap **Settings & privacy**, then **Settings**.
2. Under **Preferences**, tap **Content preferences**.
3. Under **Who you see**, tap **Snooze**.
4. Tap **End Snooze** next to the name.

**On a computer:**

1. Click your profile picture at the top right.
2. Click **Settings & privacy**, then **Content preferences**.
3. Select **Snooze**, then **End Snooze** next to the name.

The same screen lets you restart a snooze or add more time ([Facebook Help](https://www.facebook.com/help/371675846332829)).

## 3. Unfollow

**From a post:**

1. Tap or click the three dots on one of their posts.
2. Choose **Unfollow [Name]**.

**From their profile, Page or group:**

- **Profile:** tap or click **Following**, then **Unfollow**.
- **Page:** tap **Following** or **Liked**, then **Unfollow**. On a computer, click **Following**, then **Unfollow**, then **Update** to confirm.
- **Group:** tap or click **Joined**, then **Unfollow group**.

([Facebook Help](https://www.facebook.com/help/190078864497547))

Unfollowing a group is useful when you want to stay a member (for the occasional event or to search its old posts) without its posts in your feed. A later guide covers group notifications, which are a separate setting.

### Follow again (reconnect)

**In the app:** menu, **Settings & privacy**, **Settings**, then **Content preferences** under Preferences, then **Reconnect**, and choose the name.

**On a computer, or on their profile:** go to the profile or Page and tap or click **Follow** (you may need the three dots near the cover photo first). For a group, open the three dots at the top right and choose **Follow group** ([Facebook Help](https://www.facebook.com/help/146865632052048)).

Facebook's unfollow page gives the older path as Settings & Privacy, Settings, **Feed**, then **Reconnect** ([Facebook Help](https://www.facebook.com/help/190078864497547)), so the menu may be labelled differently on your account.

## An hour-long tidy-up

If your feed has drifted for years, these controls work faster in bulk than post by post.

1. Open the **Feeds** tab and choose the **Friends** filter (the [Feeds and Favorites guide](/guides/facebook/facebook-feeds-tab-favourites/) has the steps). Scroll for ten minutes and unfollow anyone whose posts you skip every time.
2. Switch to the **Pages** filter and do the same. Pages you liked in 2014 for a competition are prime candidates.
3. Switch to **Groups** and unfollow any group you're keeping only for the archive.
4. Back on Home, use Not interested on anything left that you don't want.

:::slop
Repost if you're proud of your kids! 💪 Most people won't, but real parents will.
:::
:::fine
Ella passed her grade 3 piano on Saturday. She'd failed it in March, so this one meant a lot. Thank you to Mrs Okafor for the extra lessons.
:::

You'd want the second from a friend. The first is a reason to unfollow whoever keeps sharing them, without falling out.

## If that doesn't work

- **The snoozed person is back.** Snooze ends after 30 days. Repeat it, or unfollow.
- **You unfollowed, but still see them.** You may be seeing a friend's comment on their post, or their posts in a group you're both in. Unfollow the group, or use Not interested on those posts.
- **The menu option isn't there.** Sponsored posts have their own ad options, and labels can differ between the app and a computer.
