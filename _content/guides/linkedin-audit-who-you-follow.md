Your full list of follows lives under **My Network**, then **Manage my network**, on the left of the page on desktop. Open it, work down the list, and click **Following** next to anyone you want to drop, then confirm **Unfollow**. You stay connected to anyone you unfollow, nobody is told, and newsletters have their own list on the same page.

## Before you audit who you follow on LinkedIn

Unfollowing one person at a time from the feed works, but it only catches people on the days they annoy you. A once-a-year pass through the whole list catches the rest: old colleagues who switched to posting motivational parables, Pages you followed for a single job ad, and influencers you followed during a career change three years ago.

A few things LinkedIn's help pages make clear ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/72150)):

- Unfollowing hides all of that person's updates from your feed.
- If you're connected, you remain connected.
- They aren't notified when you unfollow. They are notified if you follow them again later.
- You can follow up to 5,000 people who aren't connections.

Set aside 20 minutes, and do it on a computer if you can. The list is easier to scan on a wide screen.

## 1. Open your Following list

On desktop:

1. Click the **My Network** icon at the top of the LinkedIn homepage.
2. On the left, under **Manage my network**, click the list of people you follow.

LinkedIn's help pages call this list both **Following & followers** and **People I Follow** ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/72151)), so look for either label. The page has a **Following** tab and a **Followers** tab; you want **Following**.

In the app, tap the **My Network** icon, then **Manage my network**, then **People I follow** ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/72150)).

## 2. Decide who stays

Going through hundreds of names on instinct gets tiring fast. A simple rule helps: for each name, try to recall one post of theirs from the last three months that you were glad to read.

| If you… | Then |
|---|---|
| Can recall a useful post | Keep following |
| Can't recall any post, but want to keep in touch | Unfollow; you stay connected |
| Mostly remember bait, parables or reposts | Unfollow |
| Followed them for one job ad or event that's over | Unfollow |
| Aren't sure | Open their profile and look at their recent activity, then decide |

The middle row is the one people skip. Being connected and following are separate: you can stay in someone's network without their posts in your feed. That's often the kindest outcome for people you like but don't read.

## 3. Unfollow from the list

On desktop, click **Following** next to the name. In the **Unfollow** dialog, click **Unfollow**. The button changes back to **Follow** ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/72151)).

In the app, tap the **More** icon (three dots) next to the name and choose **Unfollow [name]** ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/72150)).

There's no bulk "select all" in LinkedIn's documented controls, so it's one click per person. At a few seconds a name, 20 minutes covers a few hundred.

## 4. Review company Pages

Pages you follow appear in the same list. Company Pages are a common source of promoted-looking posts that aren't ads: hiring pushes, award announcements and reposted press releases. Keep the ones you follow for news you'd act on, such as a client, a competitor or a former employer you might return to. Drop the rest the same way.

## 5. Clear out newsletters

Newsletters work differently from follows, and they're easy to forget about.

- **Find them.** Click **My Network**, then **Newsletters** under **Manage my network** on the left ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/97500)).
- **Leave one.** Open the newsletter's page and click **Subscribed** near the top.
- **Know what's public.** LinkedIn says newsletters you subscribe to are visible to others and on your profile unless you unsubscribe ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/111414)). If you subscribed to something you'd rather not be associated with, that's a second reason to leave.
- **Know what's linked.** Subscribing to a newsletter also makes you follow its author, so leaving the newsletter may not remove the author from your feed. Check the Following list for them too.
- **Email unsubscribe is different.** The Unsubscribe link at the bottom of a newsletter email stops emails from every LinkedIn newsletter, but you stay subscribed on LinkedIn itself ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/97500)).

You may still see a newsletter you left if a connection shares, comments on or likes it. That's expected, and **Not interested** on the post deals with it.

## 6. Check the result after a week

LinkedIn's feed draws on who you follow and who you interact with ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1339724)), so a big prune takes a few days to show. After a week, scroll for five minutes and note who still fills the top of the feed. If they're people you just unfollowed, they may be arriving through a connection's reactions; hide those posts with **Not interested** or **Hide posts by [name]** instead. The [clean-up guide](/guides/linkedin/linkedin-clean-up-feed/) covers that and muting.

If you've set your feed to Recent, an audit matters even more, because a chronological feed shows everyone you follow in order. The [guide to sorting by most recent](/guides/linkedin/linkedin-sort-feed-most-recent/) explains how the two work together.

## If you unfollow someone by mistake

1. Click the **Me** icon at the top of the homepage.
2. Choose **Settings & Privacy**.
3. Under **Account preferences**, in **Site preferences**, click **People you unfollowed**.
4. Click **Follow** next to their name.

In the app, tap your profile photo, then **Settings & Privacy**, **Account preferences**, and **People you unfollowed** under **Site preferences** ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/72150)). Remember they'll get a notification when you follow again.
