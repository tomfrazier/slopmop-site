Engagement farming is posting designed to collect replies, likes, reposts and follows, where the reaction is the point and the content is an excuse. On X it usually looks like a formula question, a follow-back promise or a list with a gap left for you to fill. The quickest test: if the post would lose nothing by having every reply deleted, the replies were the harvest.

## Why X has so much engagement farming

Engagement farming exists on every network, but X gives it a direct cash reason. In October 2024 X changed its creator revenue sharing so that payouts depend on engagement from Premium subscribers, rather than on ads shown in replies. TechCrunch pointed out at the time that this appeared to reward anything that drew replies, for whatever reason ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/10/09/x-changes-creator-payouts-to-depend-on-engagement-not-ads/)).

X has since pushed back on the most blatant forms. In July 2026 its head of product said accounts that repeatedly solicit engagement, with lines such as "I'll follow everyone who replies", would be removed from the revenue programme, and that nearly 4,000 accounts had been removed in one day ([Legit.ng](https://www.legit.ng/people/1719932-x-deletes-4000-accounts-monetisation-programme-issues-rules-nigerians-others/)). Those figures come from X itself. The subtler shapes below are still common.

## Six shapes to recognise

### 1. The follow-back promise

The oldest one. The post offers a follow, a shout-out or a "mutual" in exchange for a reply.

:::slop
Growing my tech circle 🚀 Reply "IN" and I'll follow you back. Let's connect!
:::

There's no information here at all. The whole post is a request.

### 2. The formula question

A question whose answers don't matter to the asker: "Name a film that's better than the book", "Wrong answers only", "Describe your job badly". Each reply is a tiny bit of reach. You can tell it's a formula because the same account asks a new one every few hours, on any subject.

:::slop
Name one thing everyone pretends to like but secretly hates. Wrong answers only 👇
:::
:::fine
I'm moving our team's docs off Confluence next month. If you've done the same with more than 50 people, what broke first? Happy to share our migration plan once it's done.
:::

The second question has an asker who will use the answers, a reason for asking and an offer back. That's a conversation.

### 3. The fill-in-the-blank list

"Five books that changed my life: 1. 2. 3. 4. 5. Drop yours below." Sometimes the list is already filled with famous titles, chosen to draw nods of agreement. The giveaway is the closing request, which is [engagement bait](/patterns/engagement-bait/) in its plainest form.

### 4. The "agree?" opinion

A broad claim most readers already hold, ending with "Agree?" or "Thoughts?". The claim is safe enough to draw nods and vague enough that nobody can be shown wrong.

:::slop
Hard work beats talent when talent doesn't work hard. Agree?
:::

### 5. The manufactured controversy

The opposite tactic: a deliberately provocative claim that invites angry replies, which count just the same as friendly ones. "Unpopular opinion:" is a common opener, usually for an opinion that's neither unpopular nor argued.

### 6. The bait-and-switch hook

A dramatic opener that promises a revelation, a link in the replies, and a closing ask to repost the first post. This one overlaps with the [scroll-stopper](/patterns/scroll-stopper/) tell.

:::slop
I just found the most underrated AI tool of 2026.

It will replace 90% of your workflow.

Link in replies 👇 (repost so others see it)
:::

## Who writes these

Some farming accounts are run by one person posting by hand; others post on a schedule, with text that's easy to generate in bulk because the formula is the whole post. Trying to work out which is which isn't worth your time. A follow-back promise is equally empty whether a person or a tool wrote it, and a sharp question is equally useful either way. Judge the post by what's in it, using the checks below.

## How to tell a farm from a genuine post

Not every question or list is farming. People ask things because they want to know, and a list of five good books can be a real recommendation. Four checks separate them:

- **Does the asker gain anything from the answers beyond the count?** A genuine question has a use for what comes back.
- **Is there anything in the post you didn't know?** Farming posts carry little or no information of their own.
- **Is the account's timeline full of the same shape?** One "wrong answers only" is a joke. Twenty a week is a business model.
- **Do the replies get answered?** Real askers reply to replies. Farmers rarely do, because the next post is already up.

A post that fails all four is safe to skip.

## What to do when you see engagement farming on X

1. **Don't reply, not even to mock it.** A mocking reply counts as a reply.
2. **Mark it Not interested.** X says this feedback shapes what it recommends next ([X Help Center](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/recommendations)).
3. **Mute the phrase if it keeps recurring.** "Wrong answers only", "follow everyone who" and "drop yours below" are good starting entries. See [how to clean up your For You timeline](/guides/x/x-clean-up-for-you-timeline/) for where muted words live.
4. **Unfollow accounts that post mostly farms.** If you'd like to keep an eye on someone without seeing every post, X Lists let you read accounts without following them.
5. **Let a filter fold them.** [Slop Mop](/), a browser extension for Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari and Chromium browsers, reads the posts and replies in your X timeline as you scroll. Hide mode folds likely slop into a one-line strip you can reopen; Highlight mode marks it. Engagement bait is one of the nine tells it scores, and a single "thoughts?" won't fold a post on its own, because the score is cut when only one tell stands out ([How it works](/how-it-works.html) explains why). The free plan covers one feed with 250 checks a day, and it runs on x.com in your desktop browser, not in the X app.

## If you post on X yourself

Asking your readers a question is fine. Ask one you'll use the answers to, say why you're asking, and reply to the people who answer.
