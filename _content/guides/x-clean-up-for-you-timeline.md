X's For You tab is a mix of posts from accounts you follow and posts X recommends, and it changes based on what you do. To clean it up, tell it directly with "Not interested in this post", mute the words that keep bringing in the same junk, and stop replying to bait, even to argue. If it still won't settle after a week, spend more of your time in the Following tab, which only shows accounts you follow.

## Before you start

It helps to know what you're working with. X's help centre describes two tabs at the top of Home: **For you**, which includes recommended posts, and **Following**, which shows posts from the accounts you follow ([X Help Center](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/x-timeline)). X also says it reopens whichever tab you used last.

Recommendations are where most of the noise comes from. X's own page on recommendations says feedback such as "Not interested in this post" is used as a signal for what it suggests next ([X Help Center](https://help.x.com/en/rules-and-policies/recommendations)). That's the lever this guide pulls on.

One more piece of context. Since October 2024, payouts to X creators have depended on engagement from Premium subscribers rather than on ads ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/10/09/x-changes-creator-payouts-to-depend-on-engagement-not-ads/)). Posts built to collect replies therefore have a reason to exist, and you'll see plenty of them. You can't switch that incentive off, but you can stop it reaching your screen.

These steps work in the X app and on x.com unless a step says otherwise. Menus move around, so if a label below doesn't match your screen, look for the nearest equivalent.

## 1. Mark posts Not interested

When a post you don't want shows up in For You:

1. Tap or click the **•••** menu at the top right of the post.
2. Choose **Not interested in this post**. Where X has tagged the post with a topic, you may also see **Not interested in this Topic**.

Do this every time for the first few days, including on posts that are merely dull. One tap is a weak signal; thirty taps on the same kind of post are a strong one. Some people report that the option doesn't appear on every post, so if it's missing, the menu may be labelled differently on your device. Move on to the next post rather than hunting for it.

## 2. Snooze a topic for a day

In April 2026 X added a way to snooze a topic in For You for 24 hours. GIGAZINE reported that it's for X Premium subscribers and available in the iOS app and on the web ([GIGAZINE](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260422-x-custom-timeline)). It's useful when one news story or sporting event has taken over the feed and you want a day off from it. If you don't have Premium, muted words (next step) do a similar job with a duration you choose.

## 3. Mute the words that keep coming back

Muted words remove matching posts from your Home timeline and notifications. X says you can mute words, phrases, usernames, emojis and hashtags, and that it won't recommend content containing your muted words ([X Help Center](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/advanced-x-mute-options)).

- **In the app:** go to Notifications, tap the gear icon, then **Muted**, then **Muted words**, and tap **Add**.
- **On the web:** the same list is under **Settings and privacy**, then **Privacy and safety**, then **Mute and block**, then **Muted words**. The menu may be labelled differently on your account.

Choose whether the mute applies to the Home timeline, notifications or both, and how long it lasts: Forever (the default), 24 hours, 7 days or 30 days. Start with phrases that almost only appear in bait, such as "I'll follow everyone who" or "wrong answers only". Muted words don't apply to search, so you'll still find those posts if you go looking.

## 4. Stop feeding the posts you want less of

Assume that every reply, quote, like and long pause tells the feed you want more of the same. A furious reply to a rage-bait post still counts as a reply. If a post makes you want to argue, mark it Not interested and move on. If you want to discuss it, copy the link and do it somewhere the author's reply count doesn't benefit.

The same goes for bookmarking posts "to read later" that you never will. Keep bookmarks for things you'd genuinely return to.

## 5. Prune the accounts that only post bait

Some of the noise in For You comes from accounts you followed years ago that now mostly post [engagement bait](/patterns/engagement-bait/). Go through your follows once and unfollow anyone whose last ten posts you'd have scrolled past. If you'd rather not unfollow someone, the post's **•••** menu also offers options to mute or block the account; the exact wording varies between the app and the web.

## 6. Give it a week

Recommendation systems adjust gradually. Do steps 1 to 4 consistently for about a week before judging whether it worked. Keep a rough tally if it helps: how many of the first 20 posts each morning were worth reading.

## What to look for

These are the posts most worth marking Not interested, because they tend to multiply:

- **Follow-for-follow promises**: "I'll follow everyone who replies" and its variants.
- **Formula questions**: "Name one film that… wrong answers only", posted to harvest replies.
- **[Scroll-stopper](/patterns/scroll-stopper/) openers**: "Nobody is talking about this", followed by one-line paragraphs and very little else.
- **Reply fringes**: big posts surrounded by replies that say "this" or "huge if true".

:::slop
Most people will scroll past this.

The 1% won't.

I'll follow back everyone who replies with 🔥
:::
:::fine
The council published the new bus timetable today. Route 12 loses its Sunday service from 2 November; the PDF is on their site under "service changes".
:::

The second post is dull and useful, which is the bar worth training the feed towards.

## If that doesn't work

Some days For You won't cooperate, especially during big news events. Switch to the **Following** tab at the top of Home and stay there; it only shows accounts you follow, so the recommendation problem goes away. You can also build a List of accounts that reliably post substance and pin it beside your Home tabs, which X describes in its help centre on Lists.

Slop Mop, a browser extension that folds low-value posts into a one-line strip, is coming soon to X. It doesn't work on X yet; until it does, the controls above are the tools you have.
