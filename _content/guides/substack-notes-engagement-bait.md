Engagement bait on Substack Notes is any note whose main job is to get you to like, reply, restack, follow or subscribe, with little or nothing for you in return. The common forms are "drop your link" threads, "restack if you agree" lines, milestone posts that tease a secret, and quote-restacks of the writer's own post with a hook on top. A useful note, even a very short one, leaves you knowing something you didn't.

## Why Substack Notes attracts engagement bait

Notes are public, short and sit in a shared Home feed, alongside the newsletters you chose. Substack is open about Notes being a growth tool for writers: each note has a **View stats** page showing new free and paid subscribers, new followers, clicks, shares and even revenue generated from that note, and Substack notifies writers when a note brings in at least US$100 a year in new paid subscriptions.

That gives writers a clear reason to post notes that collect replies and restacks. Most do it honestly. Some do it with formulas, and the formulas are easy to learn to spot. We don't know how Substack's feed ranks notes, and this page doesn't guess; it's about what you see on the page.

## The six common shapes

### 1. The link thread

:::slop
Writers! Drop your Substack below 👇 I'll read and restack every one. Let's grow together 🚀
:::

The note itself contains nothing. Its value to the poster is the replies. Your feed then shows the thread again each time someone you follow joins it.

### 2. The agreement ask

:::slop
Unpopular opinion: consistency beats talent. Restack if you agree.
:::

A familiar claim framed as brave, then a request. The [engagement bait](/patterns/engagement-bait/) page covers the wider family.

### 3. The milestone tease

:::slop
1,000 subscribers in 90 days. No ads. No tricks.

The one thing that changed everything? I'll share it tomorrow. Follow so you don't miss it.
:::

A number, a cliffhanger and a follow request. A [scroll-stopper](/patterns/scroll-stopper/) with the content deferred to a future note that is often another tease.

### 4. The hooked self-restack

[Restacking](https://support.substack.com/hc/en-us/articles/14671398684308-What-is-a-restack-on-Substack-Notes) means sharing a post or note into the Notes feed, with or without your own text, or by quoting a highlighted snippet. Writers restacking their own work is normal. It turns into bait when the restack adds a dramatic hook and no information: "I almost didn't publish this one…" over a link.

### 5. The parable

:::slop
A reader emailed me yesterday. She said one sentence that made me cry. That's when I realised why I write.
:::

A [too-tidy story](/patterns/too-tidy-story/): no detail you could check, a feeling, and a moral that points back to the writer.

### 6. The connection chain

:::slop
Who else is writing about mindset and growth? Let's connect! Comment and I'll follow back 🤝
:::

Follow-for-follow, dressed as community.

## What a good short Note looks like

Short isn't the problem. Notes are meant to be quick, and many of the best are three lines long. What they share is a specific thing you didn't have before:

:::fine
Tried publishing at 6am instead of noon for a month. Opens barely moved (44% vs 43%). Replies doubled, mostly from readers in Asia. Keeping 6am.
:::
:::fine
New this week: the county's flood maps were updated on 2 October. Our street moved from low to medium risk. The old map is still the first search result.
:::
:::fine
Restacking this because the section on invoicing late payers is the clearest I've read. The template at the end saved me an afternoon.
:::

The third is a restack, and it's fine: it tells you exactly why the post is worth your time. Asking a real question is fine too. "Has anyone moved a paid list from monthly to annual-only? What happened to churn?" invites replies because the writer wants the answers.

## A quick test

Before you reply or restack, ask three questions:

1. **If I took out the request, would anything be left?** If the note is only a request, it's bait.
2. **Is there a detail the writer couldn't have invented?** A number, date, place or named thing.
3. **Would I miss this note if it hadn't appeared?** If not, scrolling past costs nothing.

Two "no" answers is enough to move on. Replying, even to disagree, gives the note what it asked for.

## What to do about engagement bait on Substack Notes

- **Mute repeat offenders.** Open the three-dot menu on the note and choose **Mute**. They aren't notified, and Substack says it stops recommending muted authors' notes and posts to you. Steps for this and the rest are in [how to clean up your Notes feed](/guides/substack/substack-clean-up-notes-feed/).
- **Mute the formula.** Add phrases such as "drop your Substack" or "restack if you agree" under **Muted words & phrases** in Content preferences. You get 50 entries.
- **Lean on Following.** The Following view of Home is made up of the people and publications you chose, which leaves fewer routes in for strangers' link threads.
- **Mute the restacker.** If one account keeps pulling link threads into your feed, they're the one to mute.
- **Let a filter fold the rest.** On a computer, [Slop Mop](/how-it-works.html) reads the Notes and posts in Substack's web app and asks the same kind of questions as the test above: is there a request with nothing behind it, a hook, a moral? Likely bait folds into a one-line strip you can reopen, and a genuine question or a useful three-line note is left alone. It doesn't mute or report anyone, and it doesn't work in the phone app.

The same six shapes turn up on [LinkedIn](/guides/linkedin/linkedin-filter-engagement-bait/) and [X](/guides/x/x-engagement-farming/), and Slop Mop handles those feeds too.

## If you write Notes yourself

The same test works on your own drafts. If you want replies, ask something you'd genuinely like answered, and say why. If you want restacks, give people something specific enough that sharing it makes them look well informed. Substack's own feature for this is the snippet restack: highlight the best line in your post and restack that, instead of writing a hook about it.

[Add Slop Mop to your browser](/) and it folds notes like the six shapes above for you.
