In Chrome on a computer, right-click the page and choose **Open in reading mode**. On Android, tap the **More** menu and choose **Show Reading mode**. In Safari, use **Show Reader** from the address bar's menu, on Mac or iPhone. Each strips the page to its text and main images, which hides ads and pop-ups and makes padding much easier to spot.

## Before you start

- Steps were checked against Google's and Apple's help pages on 18 October 2026. Icons and menu names change between versions, so yours may look slightly different.
- Reader views only work on pages that look like articles. Shops, search results and web apps usually aren't supported. Chrome says the feature "is not available to all websites", and Safari shows the option only when there's an article to show.
- Reader views remove clutter, not filler. A padded article stays padded, but you'll see that faster.

## Chrome on a computer

Google's [help page](https://support.google.com/chrome/answer/14218344?co=GENIE.Platform%3DDesktop) lists several ways in.

1. Open the article in Chrome.
2. Right-click anywhere on the page.
3. Select **Open in reading mode**.

Chrome opens a full-screen reading view. If you prefer it beside the original page, open the reading mode **Settings** and choose **View**, then **Side panel**; Chrome remembers the choice.

Other ways to open it:

- **Address bar:** select the **Reading mode** icon when it appears.
- **Menu:** **More** (the three dots, top right), then **More tools**, then **Reading mode**.
- **Keyboard:** **Alt + Shift + R** on Windows, Linux and ChromeOS; **⌘ + Option + R** on a Mac.

To keep it one click away, open reading mode's **Settings** and choose **Pin reading mode**. It then sits in your toolbar.

Inside reading mode you can change the font size, the typeface, the colour theme and the line and letter spacing. On a computer, Chrome can also read the text aloud.

## Chrome on Android

From Google's [Android instructions](https://support.google.com/chrome/answer/14218344?co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid):

1. Open the article in Chrome.
2. Tap **More** to the right of the address bar.
3. Tap **Show Reading mode**.

If the page can't be shown this way, Chrome displays "Reading mode is unavailable". To change the font or background, pull up the **Reading mode** sheet at the bottom of the screen.

We didn't verify a reading mode for Chrome on iPhone or iPad, so this guide covers Safari's Reader for those devices.

## Safari on a Mac

From Apple's [Safari guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/safari/hide-ads-when-reading-sfri32632/mac):

1. Open the article in Safari.
2. Click in the **Smart Search field** (the address bar).
3. Choose **Show Reader**.

To leave, click in the Smart Search field and choose **Hide Reader**, or press **Esc**. While Reader is open, a button at the left end of the Smart Search field lets you pick a background theme, font and zoom level. Apple says longer articles also get a table of contents and a summary.

## Safari on iPhone

From Apple's [iPhone guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/hide-ads-and-distractions-iphdc30e3b86/ios):

1. Open the article in Safari.
2. Tap the page menu button in the address bar (the icon may differ between iOS versions).
3. Tap **Show Reader**. Use the same button and **Hide Reader** to go back.

To always open a site in Reader, tap the same button, choose **Website Settings** and turn on **Use Reader Automatically**. To do it for every site that supports it, go to **Settings > Apps > Safari > Reader** and turn on **All Websites**.

The automatic setting is handy for sites you read often and trust, and for recipe and news sites whose layouts get in the way.

## Firefox

Firefox has its own Reader View. Mozilla's support page for it wouldn't load for us when we checked, so we haven't listed the steps here; see [Mozilla's help article](https://support.mozilla.org/en-US/kb/firefox-reader-view-clutter-free-web-pages) for the current instructions.

## Use the plain view to judge the page

Reader mode is useful for more than comfort. With the ads and boxes gone, the writing has nowhere to hide, and the checks from [How to spot an AI slop article](/guides/websites/web-spot-ai-slop-articles/) go faster.

- **Scroll to the end first.** Reader views show the whole article in one column, so you can see how long it really is and where the useful part starts.
- **Look at the first heading.** If the first two or three screens are an introduction before any heading, the page is probably padded.
- **Check the headings in order.** Headings that answer questions ("How long to rest the dough") are a good sign. Headings that restate the topic ("Why bread matters") usually aren't.
- **Watch the links.** Reader views keep links in the text, so you can still trace sources as described in [How to check a blog post's sources](/guides/websites/web-check-blog-post-sources/).

:::slop
Bread has been a staple of human civilisation for thousands of years. Moreover, there's something truly special about the smell of a fresh loaf. In this article, we'll explore everything you need to know.
:::
:::fine
Rest the dough for 30 minutes after mixing, before you add salt. It makes the dough easier to stretch and you'll need less kneading.
:::

The first is three sentences of [stiff phrasing](/patterns/stiff-phrasing/) before anything happens. In a cluttered layout it's broken up by ads and feels like progress. In reader mode it's obviously a delay.

## If reader mode doesn't work

- **The option is missing or greyed out.** The page isn't recognised as an article. Try the page's own print view, which some sites simplify.
- **Parts of the article are missing.** Reader views sometimes drop tables, embedded tools or recipe cards. Switch back to the full page for those sections.
- **The page loads, then a paywall or pop-up covers it.** Reader mode may show the text that loaded, but don't count on it, and respect the site's terms.
- **The keyboard shortcut does nothing, or opens something else.** Google notes that other apps, such as voice control, can claim the same shortcut. Change the shortcut in the other app.
