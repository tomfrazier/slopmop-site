Read in the app if you want newsletters out of your inbox, in one queue, with Substack's reading tools (saving, archiving, listening, the AI scan). Stay with email if you want newsletters next to everything else you read, searchable with your mail tools and readable without a Substack login. You can switch at any time under **Newsletter delivery** in your notification settings, and you can pick both.

## What each option gives you

| | Substack app | Email |
|---|---|---|
| Where posts arrive | The app's **Read** tab, sorted by Latest, Paid or Saved | Your inbox, mixed with other mail |
| Keeping your inbox clear | Yes | No, unless you filter |
| Archive and save | Swipe left to archive; Saved tab for later | Your mail client's own folders and labels |
| Listen to posts | Text-to-speech on many English-language publications | No |
| Scan for AI text | Yes, on iOS (Android coming soon) | Not available in emails |
| Notes, chat and comments | All in the same app | Not in the email itself |
| Search | Within Substack | Your mail client's search, across everything |

A few details from Substack's help pages: text-to-speech is only in the app and only on posts written in English, and not every publication has it. The AI scan works in the app and the web Reader, but Substack says it isn't available in emails or on publications' own custom-domain sites.

## The catch with each

The app puts your subscriptions next to the Home feed. Opening it to read one essay means passing the Notes feed, suggestions and recommendations first. Some readers find that's where their reading time goes. If that's you, email may be the calmer choice even though it's busier, because your inbox doesn't recommend anything.

Email has the opposite problem: newsletters compete with work and personal mail, and they're easy to leave unread for weeks. A folder or label for newsletters, read once or twice a week, fixes most of that.

## 1. Check what you're on now

If you installed the app and your newsletter emails quietly stopped, you probably chose **Smart notifications** during setup. Substack says that with Smart notifications you mainly get push notifications about new posts, and may get an email about posts you've missed if you haven't opened the app for a while.

## 2. Switch delivery in the app

The steps are the same on iOS and Android:

1. Tap your **profile picture** and select **Settings**.
2. Tap **Notifications** and scroll to **Newsletter Delivery**.
3. Choose one:
   - **Prefer email**: new posts arrive by email and also appear in the app's inbox.
   - **Prefer push**: posts arrive in the app with a push notification.
   - **Both email and push**: you get both.

## 3. Switch delivery on the web

1. Go to **substack.com/settings** and scroll to **Notifications**.
2. Under **Newsletter delivery**, select **Prefer email delivery**, **Prefer app delivery**, or **Both email and app**.

Substack notes that if app delivery fails, posts may start arriving by email again. If emails reappear after you chose the app, that's the likely cause.

## 4. Set up the app for reading

If you've moved to the app, three settings make it work better as a reader:

- **Inbox appearance** (in app Settings): choose **dense** to hide preview images and shrink titles, so more posts fit on screen. **Classic** shows the images.
- **Archive as you go.** In the **Latest** view, swipe left on a post to archive it. Archived posts live under **Archive** in the app's Settings; press and hold one to unarchive.
- **Save for later.** Posts and notes you save from Home appear in the **Saved** tab, which keeps the queue for things you actually mean to read.
- **Listen instead.** On a post, tap the **play icon** at the top right and the app reads it aloud, carrying on in the background until you pause or close the app. If a publication or an older post has no audio, you'll see a message saying so. This is a good way to get through long essays on a commute, and it only exists in the app.

## 5. Set up email for reading

If you're staying with email:

- **Get newsletters out of Promotions or Spam.** Substack suggests dragging the email to your main inbox from Promotions, or marking it "Not spam" and adding the writer's Substack address to your contacts.
- **Filter them into one place.** Most mail clients can filter on sender or on words in the message. A rule per writer works, or one rule on a phrase that appears in every Substack footer you receive. Check your mail client's help for the exact steps.
- **Trim at the source.** Fewer emails is better than a better folder. See [how to cut down your Substack subscriptions](/guides/substack/substack-too-many-subscriptions/).

## What to look for, whichever you choose

Neither option fixes a newsletter that wastes your time. A good habit in both is to read the first paragraph before committing to the rest.

:::slop
Grab a coffee, get comfy, because this one's a big one. You won't want to miss what's coming.
:::
:::fine
The council approved the new cycle lane on Tuesday. Work starts in March and closes the bridge for six weeks.
:::

The first is a [scroll-stopper](/patterns/scroll-stopper/) written for an inbox: it tells you to keep reading without saying why. The second tells you what happened and what it means for you, and you could stop there if that's all you needed.

## If that doesn't work

- **Emails stopped and you didn't change anything.** Check Promotions and Spam first, then your Newsletter delivery setting.
- **You get every post twice.** You're on "Both". Pick one.
- **A paid post won't open in email.** Make sure you're signed in to Substack with the same address the email was sent to. Substack says a paywall on a post you've paid for can mean a mistyped email address or a second account.
