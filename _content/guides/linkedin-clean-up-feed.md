Most of the clean-up happens in the three-dot menu on each post. Use **Not interested** on posts you don't want, **Unfollow** people whose posts you always skip (you stay connected and they aren't told), and mute people who only appear because a connection reacted to them. Then stop reacting to the posts you want less of, because LinkedIn reads your reactions as interest.

## Before you start

It helps to know where the posts come from. LinkedIn says your feed mixes posts from your connections, from people and companies you follow, from groups you belong to, recommended posts from outside your network, and sponsored content ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1339724)). Each source has its own off switch, and none of them works on the others.

LinkedIn also says it uses your own activity, meaning what you write, like and comment on, to decide what to show you ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/85598)). So the feed you have is partly a record of what you've engaged with. That's useful: it means you can retrain it.

Changes you make in the mobile app carry over to the desktop site, and the other way round. Give it a week of steady use before you judge the result.

## 1. Hide posts you don't want

This is the control to use most, because each use is also feedback.

1. Click the **More** icon (three dots) at the top right of the post. In the app, tap the three dots to the right of the post.
2. Choose **Not interested** to remove the post and see fewer like it, or **Hide posts by [name]** to reduce posts from that person or Page.

LinkedIn notes that the options vary by post type, and some posts show an **X** icon that removes just that one post, after which you can say why ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/13325)). The author isn't notified, and hiding a post doesn't report it. Older help pages call the same option "I don't want to see this post", so the menu may be labelled differently on your account.

## 2. Unfollow the people you always skip

If someone's posts are reliably not for you, unfollow them instead of hiding posts one at a time.

1. On one of their posts, click the **More** icon at the top right.
2. Click **Unfollow [name]**.

You can also do it from their profile: click **More** in the introduction section and choose **Unfollow**. The steps are the same in the app. If you're connected, you stay connected but stop seeing their updates, and LinkedIn doesn't tell them. It does tell them if you follow them again later ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/72150)).

Unfollowing is all or nothing. A former colleague who posts one useful hiring update a month and four motivational parables goes completely, including the useful one. Step 7 covers that case.

## 3. Mute people you've never heard of

A lot of LinkedIn noise comes from strangers who appear because one of your connections reacted to or commented on their post. You can't unfollow someone you don't follow, but LinkedIn lists **mute** alongside unfollow in the same post menu ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/62570)).

Open the post's **More** menu and look for a mute or hide option with the author's name. LinkedIn's help pages mention muting without giving exact steps, so the label may differ from the one above. **Hide posts by [name]** does a similar job where mute isn't offered.

## 4. Hide promoted posts

Sponsored posts carry a **Promoted** or **Sponsored** label under the company name. Click the **More** icon at the top right and choose **Hide this post** ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/lms/answer/85787)).

There's a limit here. LinkedIn's ad settings change how ads are personalised, and LinkedIn says plainly that none of them stops you seeing ads ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1342443)). Hiding them one by one is the only feed control.

## 5. Stop rewarding the posts you dislike

This step costs nothing and does the most over time. Every reaction and comment tells the feed you want more of that kind of post, and LinkedIn also shows your network the posts you engage with. A hate-comment on a bait post is still a comment.

- Don't type the keyword on "Comment GUIDE and I'll DM you" posts. If you want the document, look for it on the author's site.
- Don't react to argue. Scroll on, or use Not interested.
- React to and comment on the posts you'd like more of. A short, specific comment on a useful post is a stronger signal than a like.

The [engagement bait](/patterns/engagement-bait/) page explains why bait posts collect so many comments and what a fair request for replies looks like.

## 6. Check what you follow

Most of the feed is still people and Pages you follow, so pruning that list once pays off for months. On desktop, click the **My Network** icon and look under **Manage my network** on the left for your following list. Click **Following** next to anyone you want to drop, then confirm **Unfollow** ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/72150)). LinkedIn's own pages call this list both "Following & followers" and "People I Follow", so look for either.

If you'd rather see only people you follow, newest first, you can also switch the desktop feed from Top to Recent with the **Sort by** menu above it ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1480504)). That removes most recommended posts from strangers, but it does nothing about bait from people you follow.

## 7. Fold individual posts with a filter

LinkedIn's controls act on whole people or on one post after you've already read it. None of them looks at what a post says before you reach it, and LinkedIn's help pages don't describe a muted-words feature.

That gap is what Slop Mop is for. It's a free browser extension for Chrome and other Chromium browsers, live on LinkedIn, that reads each post's text as you scroll and either folds likely slop into a one-line strip you can reopen (Hide mode) or just marks it (Highlight mode). It judges the writing, so the colleague's useful hiring update stays visible while their parables fold. You can set sensitivity to Mild, Moderate or Aggressive and vote to overrule any call. The limits: the free plan covers one feed with 250 checks a day, it runs on your computer's browser only, so the LinkedIn app is unchanged, and it never mutes, reports or unfollows anyone.

## If that doesn't work

- **The same person keeps appearing.** Check whether they're appearing through someone else's reaction. If so, mute or hide them; unfollowing won't help.
- **You unfollowed someone by mistake.** Click **Me**, then **Settings & Privacy**. Under **Account preferences**, find **Site preferences** and open **People you unfollowed**, then click **Follow** next to their name ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/72150)).
- **A post breaks the rules.** Hiding isn't reporting. Use **Report this post** in the same menu for harassment, scams or spam ([LinkedIn Help](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/68012)).
- **Nothing seems to change.** The feed adjusts gradually. Keep hiding and keep not reacting for a week, then look again.
