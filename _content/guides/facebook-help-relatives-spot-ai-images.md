The most useful thing you can give a relative is a short habit: before reacting to a striking picture, ask who posted it, what it wants them to do, and whether the Page has a believable history. That catches most AI image posts without anyone needing to judge pixels. Pair it with ten minutes tidying their feed together, and leave the jokes out.

Anyone can be taken in by these posts. Researchers who studied 120 Pages posting AI images found that many commenters didn't seem to realise the pictures were synthetic ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12838)), and a well-made generated image can be hard to tell from a photo at a glance. The fault lies with the posts, which are designed to work on kind, polite people.

## Before you start

- **Pick a calm moment.** Right after they've shared something is the worst time. A quiet cup of tea next week is better.
- **Ask, don't announce.** "I keep seeing these odd pictures on Facebook. Do you get them too?" goes further than "That thing you shared was fake."
- **Bring an example that isn't theirs.** Scroll your own feed or theirs together and find one. Nobody likes their own post used as the cautionary tale.

## Three questions to spot AI images on Facebook

Write these on a card by the computer if it helps.

### 1. Who posted this?

A friend, a group they joined, or a Page they've never heard of? Posts from strangers' Pages are where AI pictures cluster. Show them that any Page has a **Page transparency** section listing when it was created and its previous names ([Facebook Help](https://www.facebook.com/help/323314944866264)): tap the Page's name, then its name under the cover photo, then **Transparency**. In the DiResta and Goldstein study, 46 of the 120 Pages had changed their names, including one that had been a touring band's Page and became a nature Page full of AI images ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12838)).

### 2. What does it want me to do?

Most of these posts ask for something: a "well done", a birthday wish, an "Amen", a share. The study found the same captions ("Made it with my own hands", "No one ever blessed me") copied across Pages that seemed unrelated ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12838)). A post that asks strangers for a kind word is usually just collecting reactions. That's [engagement bait](/patterns/engagement-bait/).

:::slop
Today is my birthday. I'm 100 years old and nobody has wished me happy birthday 😢
:::
:::fine
Mum turned 100 on Sunday. Twenty of us squeezed into the front room at Ashby Road, and she beat my son at dominoes twice.
:::

The first tells you nothing except that it wants a response. The second is full of details a real family would mention.

### 3. Does the rest of the Page add up?

Scroll down the Page. A hundred "handmade" carvings, each by a different elderly maker, mixed with celebrity photos and puppies, isn't one person's hobby. A link to an unknown website in the first comment is another sign: spam Pages in the study often put one there to send people to ad-filled sites ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12838)).

## About labels

Meta adds an **AI info** label to some images, but only when it detects industry-standard signals in the file or the uploader says the content is AI. For images that were only edited with AI, the label sits in the post's menu rather than on the picture ([Meta](https://about.fb.com/news/2024/04/metas-approach-to-labeling-ai-generated-content-and-manipulated-media/)). So a label is worth reading when it's there, and its absence proves nothing. Make sure your relative knows both halves of that.

## Tidy their feed together

Ten minutes on their phone, with them holding it, is a good start.

1. **Find two or three AI picture posts** and choose **Not interested** from each post's three-dot menu. Facebook says this tells it to show fewer posts like it ([Facebook Help](https://www.facebook.com/help/268028706671439)). Let them do the tapping.
2. **Snooze or unfollow** the Pages that keep appearing. The [snooze and unfollow guide](/guides/facebook/facebook-snooze-unfollow-not-interested/) has the steps for each.
3. **Set up Favorites** with the family and friends they most want to see, up to 30 people and Pages ([Facebook Help](https://www.facebook.com/help/1634545223376778)). Their posts move higher in the feed. The [Feeds and Favorites guide](/guides/facebook/facebook-feeds-tab-favourites/) shows how, including pinning the Feeds tab so it's easy to find again.
4. **Quieten groups** that send alerts all day, using the [noisy groups guide](/guides/facebook/facebook-quiet-noisy-groups/).

## When a picture turns into a message

AI picture Pages are mostly after attention, but some lead to scams. The Pages in the study included scams that tried to sell products that don't exist or collect personal details ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12838)). Go through Facebook's own warning signs with them ([Facebook Help](https://www.facebook.com/help/1674717642789671)):

- Strangers or famous people asking for money.
- Fees to claim a prize, loan or winnings.
- Someone claiming to be a friend or relative in an emergency.
- Being asked to move the conversation off Facebook.
- Accounts with no friends, no profile photo or no real activity.

Agree a simple rule: if anyone on Facebook asks for money or a code, they ring you or another family member first. That rule is worth more than any amount of image-spotting.

## What not to do

- **Don't make them feel foolish.** People who are embarrassed stop asking, and you want them to ask.
- **Don't teach "everything is fake".** Real photos of real people, including their own grandchildren, deserve to be believed. Blanket doubt has its own cost: it lets people dismiss genuine evidence as AI, which is the [liar's dividend](/glossary/liars-dividend/).
- **Don't argue in the comments of the post.** If a friend of theirs keeps sharing these, a private word from your relative is kinder and more likely to land.

## If they keep seeing them

Feeds take a while to change, and one session won't clear months of drift. Check in after a couple of weeks and repeat the Not interested taps on whatever's still there. If the main feed stays bad, using the Feeds tab with Favorites is a reliable way to see family posts without the suggestions.
