Open substack.com/settings and scroll to **Subscriptions**: that's your full list, free and paid, in one place. From there, click any publication to unsubscribe, switch off the sections you don't read, or cancel a paid plan. In the iOS or Android app, tap your profile picture, pick a publication, then the three dots and **Unsubscribe**.

The harder part is deciding what goes. The steps below cover both.

## Before you start

- **Use the account you subscribed with.** Substack creates an account for every email address you subscribe with. If some newsletters are missing from your list, you probably signed up to them with a different address, and you'll need to sign in with that one too.
- **Know the three levels of attachment.** Substack separates *following* (you see someone's Notes and some reading activity in your feed, but get no emails), *free subscription* (their free posts by email and/or in the app, plus their Notes) and *paid subscription* (everything they publish). Dropping a level is often enough. If you like someone's Notes but never open their emails, unsubscribe and follow instead.
- **Paid plans need the web.** [Substack says](https://support.substack.com/hc/en-us/articles/360037489252-How-do-I-cancel-my-paid-subscription-on-Substack) you can only fully manage a subscription on the web version of Substack, so cancellations are easiest on a computer or a mobile browser.

## 1. See everything you subscribe to

1. Go to **substack.com/settings** (or click your profile icon and choose **Settings**).
2. Scroll to **Subscriptions**.

On desktop there's also your **Library** at substack.com/library, which lists free and paid subscriptions together. It's a good place to do the triage below, because you can see the whole set at once.

## 2. Sort the list into three piles

Before clicking anything, go down the list and give each publication one of three labels. Be honest about the last month, not about who you'd like to be.

- **Keep:** you opened at least two of its last four issues and something in them changed what you did or thought.
- **Trim:** you read one section or one kind of post and skip the rest.
- **Go:** you archive it unopened, or you subscribed for one post and stayed out of politeness.

A quick test for the Go pile: if the newsletter stopped tomorrow, would you notice within a month? If not, unsubscribe. You can always resubscribe later.

## 3. Unsubscribe from a free publication

There are three ways, and they do the same thing.

- **From an email:** scroll to the bottom of any issue and click **Unsubscribe**, then choose **Unsubscribe from all** in the window that opens.
- **On the web:** in Settings, under **Subscriptions**, click the publication. On the next page, go to **Notifications** and click **Unsubscribe**.
- **In the iOS or Android app:** tap your profile picture to open your library, pick the publication, tap the three dots at the top right and choose **Unsubscribe**.

## 4. Trim sections instead of leaving

Many publications split their output into sections: a weekly essay, a links roundup, a podcast. When you subscribe, you get every section by default.

1. In Settings, under **Subscriptions**, click the publication.
2. Slide the toggle off next to each section you don't want. A grey toggle means emails and app notifications are off for that section.

This is the right fix for the Trim pile. One caveat from Substack: if a writer adds a *new* section later, existing subscribers aren't added to it automatically, so you won't suddenly start receiving it.

## 5. Stop recommendation digests

When a writer you subscribe to recommends another publication, Substack can send you a recommendations digest email. You can unsubscribe from the digest itself, using the link in the email, and it won't affect your subscription to the writer. Substack notes you'll still get these emails if *other* writers you read make new recommendations, so you may need to do this more than once.

## 6. Cancel a paid subscription

1. Go to **substack.com/settings** and, under **Subscriptions**, select the publication.
2. On the **Manage subscription** page, scroll to **Account actions** and click the link next to **To cancel your paid subscription, click here**.
3. On the next page, click **Cancel subscription**, pick a reason, and click **Cancel subscription** again. Some publications offer a free month at this point; click **Continue to cancel** if you still want to go.

You keep paid access until the end of the period you've paid for, and cancelling doesn't give a refund. After that you drop to a free subscription, so unsubscribe as well if you want the emails to stop. If you subscribed inside the iOS app, cancel through the Apple App Store instead.

If you're unsure, Substack also lets you **pause** a paid plan for 1, 3 or 6 months, once every 6 months. Pausing stops both the charges and your paid access, and your remaining paid time is restored when it resumes.

## 7. The nuclear option

The **Danger Zone** section of Settings can unsubscribe you from every Substack publication and stop Substack emails altogether. Substack warns that this removes you completely: no emails, no posts in the app, nothing left under Subscriptions. Use it only if you're leaving Substack, not to tidy up.

## Which Substack subscriptions to drop when you have too many

Volume isn't the only problem. A publication earns its place in your inbox when an issue gives you something you couldn't get from its headline. Compare two openings:

:::slop
This week has been a whirlwind. So many thoughts swirling. Before we dive in, a quick reminder that paid subscribers get exclusive access to our community.
:::
:::fine
Three parking rules change in town this month. The one that affects most readers is the new 2-hour limit on Mill Street, from 4 November.
:::

If most issues from a writer open like the first, that's a strong sign for the Go pile. [Hype words](/patterns/hype-words/) and long throat-clearing are cheap to produce, and they fill an inbox fast.

If you'd like a second opinion while you sort, [Slop Mop](/how-it-works.html) gives one on both sides of Substack. In Substack's web app it reads the Notes and posts in your feed and folds the likely [AI slop](/glossary/ai-slop/). In Gmail and Outlook, the Slop Mop add-ons check what the sender of each newsletter wrote, with personal details replaced by placeholders before anything leaves your mailbox. A publication that gets folded week after week is a good candidate for the Go pile. The email add-ons are part of Slop Mop XL; the free plan covers one feed.

## If that doesn't work

- **A newsletter isn't in your list.** Search your inbox for its emails and check which address they were sent to, then sign in to Substack with that address.
- **Emails keep coming after you unsubscribed.** Check whether you have a second account or were subscribed to a section separately. If you've paid, check whether the plan has ended yet: cancelling leaves you on the free list until you unsubscribe too.
- **You want fewer emails but not fewer writers.** Switch delivery to the Substack app instead; Settings has a Newsletter delivery option for app, email or both.
