In Outlook.com, go to **Settings**, then **Mail**, then **Subscriptions**: it lists the mailing lists Outlook has detected, each with an Unsubscribe button. Then use **Sweep** on one message from a sender to delete or move everything else they've sent, now and in future. Block only the senders who ignore your unsubscribe.

## Before you clean up newsletters in Outlook

- **Which Outlook are you using?** Microsoft has several: Outlook.com (personal Hotmail, Live and Outlook accounts in a browser), Outlook on the web (work or school accounts in a browser), new Outlook for Windows, and classic Outlook. The Subscriptions steps below come from [Microsoft's Outlook.com help page](https://support.microsoft.com/en-gb/office/how-to-manage-email-subscriptions-in-outlook-com-26c8f447-2141-4bff-8781-80a4e6b7f0c8). Sweep is documented for Outlook.com, Outlook on the web and new Outlook. If a menu isn't where this guide says, your version may label it differently.
- **These are desktop and browser steps.** Microsoft's pages for these tools cover the browser and Windows versions, not the Outlook phone app, so do this on a computer.
- **Know what each tool does.** Unsubscribing asks the sender to stop. Sweep tidies what's already arrived and can keep tidying. Blocking stops their mail reaching your inbox but doesn't take you off their list.

## 1. Leave the lists you don't want

1. Select **Settings** (the gear at the top of the page).
2. Select **Mail**, then **Subscriptions**.
3. Under **Your current subscriptions**, find a sender you no longer want and select **Unsubscribe**.
4. Select **OK** in the confirmation window.

Outlook builds this list by checking incoming mail for subscription markers in the message header, after junk mail has been filtered out. So the list only shows senders who identify their email as a subscription. A small newsletter sent from someone's own inbox won't appear.

Microsoft also notes three cases where you won't get the Unsubscribe option: the email went to Junk, you've already blocked the sender or their domain, or the messages aren't all from the same sender.

## 2. Clear the backlog with Sweep

Unsubscribing does nothing about the 400 emails a sender has already sent. Sweep does.

1. Select one email from the sender.
2. Select **Sweep** on the menu bar (on the ribbon in new Outlook).
3. Choose what to do with that sender's messages. Microsoft lists the options as deleting all their email, keeping only the latest one, or deleting email older than 10 days, and you can apply them once or to future messages too. You can also move their messages to a folder rather than deleting them.
4. Select **Sweep** (or **OK**) to run it.

Two limits from Microsoft's help pages:

- Sweep isn't available in **Junk Email**, **Drafts**, **Sent Items** or **Deleted Items**.
- Sweep rules for future mail run once a day. Inbox rules run as soon as mail arrives. If you want a sender's email out of your inbox the moment it lands, use a rule instead (right-click a message, then **Rules** and **Create rule** in new Outlook).

**"Keep only the latest"** is the most useful option for newsletters you half-want. A weekly bulletin from your gym or a software changelog stays readable, but you never have more than one sitting there.

## 3. Block the ones that won't stop

If a sender is still writing a week after you unsubscribed, block them.

From the Subscriptions page:

1. Go to **Settings**, **Mail**, **Subscriptions**.
2. Select the three dots next to the sender.
3. Select **Block** with the sender's name, then **OK**.

You can review everyone you've blocked under **Settings**, **Mail**, **Junk email**.

## Picking which Outlook newsletters to cut

The Subscriptions page gives you a list of names. Some questions to run down it:

- **Would you notice if it stopped?** Most people can name the three or four newsletters they actually open. Everything else is a candidate.
- **Does the subject line ask for something?** Newsletters built on [scroll-stopper](/patterns/scroll-stopper/) subjects and "reply YES to claim" asks are running on [engagement bait](/patterns/engagement-bait/). The content tends to match the subject.
- **Is there a reason it comes to you by email?** Updates that matter on a date (a renewal, a school closure, a change of terms) are worth keeping. Content you could find again on a website whenever you wanted isn't urgent enough for an inbox.

:::slop
Subject: You asked, we listened 👀 (open before it's gone)
We've been working on something BIG behind the scenes, and honestly? We can't keep quiet any longer…
:::
:::fine
Subject: Pool closed 3–9 November for retiling
The main pool is shut for a week from Monday 3 November. The learner pool stays open 7am to 9pm.
:::

The first one wants your click. The second one has done its job before you've opened it.

## Messages to leave alone

Don't Sweep or block senders whose mail you may need to act on, even if it's dull: banks, utilities, payroll, your employer's IT team, two-factor codes, delivery notices. If they're cluttering the inbox, move them to a folder with a rule rather than deleting them.

## If that doesn't work

- **Subscriptions isn't under Settings, Mail.** You may be on a work or school account, or on classic Outlook. Try the unsubscribe link at the bottom of the email, and use Sweep or a rule for the backlog.
- **The sender isn't in the Subscriptions list.** Outlook didn't detect it as a subscription. Use the unsubscribe link in the email, or Sweep everything from them to Deleted Items.
- **Sweep is greyed out.** Check you're not in Junk, Drafts, Sent Items or Deleted Items.
- **The email looks like a scam.** Don't click anything inside it. Mark it as junk or block the sender.
