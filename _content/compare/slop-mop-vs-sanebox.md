People looking for a SaneBox alternative usually want one of two things: a cheaper way to sort mail, or a way to spend less time on the mail they still read. Slop Mop only does the second. It doesn't sort, file or unsubscribe, so it's a replacement for SaneBox only if sorting was never your real problem.

## What each one does

**SaneBox** is an email assistant that works with any email provider and client, with nothing to install ([SaneBox](https://www.sanebox.com/)). It moves unimportant mail out of your inbox into separate folders, and adds a set of tools around that: a Daily Digest summarising unimportant mail you haven't opened, BlackHole for senders you never want to hear from again, one-click unsubscribe, reminders when someone hasn't replied, Snooze for mail that should come back later, and SaneNews, which rounds up newsletters into one folder ([SaneBox tour](https://www.sanebox.com/tour)). SaneBox says it leaves your email on your mail server and analyses only the headers to decide what's important ([SaneBox security](https://www.sanebox.com/security)). It's a paid service after a free trial. As of October 2026, a third-party pricing tracker lists three plans, from about US$7 a month for one account to about US$36 a month for four ([Toolradar](https://toolradar.com/tools/sanebox/pricing)); check SaneBox's own pricing page before you buy.

**Slop Mop** judges writing. In Gmail and Outlook, the Slop Mop for Gmail and Slop Mop for Outlook add-ons check what the sender wrote: an AI model designed for calibrated decisions answers twelve questions about it, nine tells of slop, such as [hype words](/patterns/hype-words/) and [stiff phrasing](/patterns/stiff-phrasing/), and three counter-signals, such as whether the email is useful to readers. You get a score out of 100 and the reasons behind it. The same check runs on LinkedIn, X, Reddit, Facebook, Substack, Medium and the articles you read, where Hide mode folds likely slop into a one-line strip and Highlight mode marks it. [How it works](/how-it-works.html) goes through the twelve questions.

## Where Slop Mop and SaneBox differ

**What they judge.** SaneBox asks whether an email matters to you, based on its headers and on how you train it. Slop Mop asks whether the writing is worth reading: whether it's built on hooks, bait and padding, and whether it says anything useful. The two questions overlap less than you'd think. An important email from your manager can be badly written, and a well-written newsletter can be one you never open. For triage, where who sent it often matters most, SaneBox's question is the more useful one.

**Where they work.** SaneBox works on any provider and in any mail app, including on your phone, because it sorts on the server. Slop Mop's email support covers Gmail and Outlook only, on desktop. If your mail lives with another provider, or you mostly read it on a phone, SaneBox is the one that fits. Slop Mop's advantage is reach in the other direction: one subscription covers your inbox and your feeds.

**What happens to the item.** SaneBox moves mail between folders on your mail server, and BlackHole sends a sender's mail away for good. Slop Mop changes nothing in your mailbox. It never moves, deletes, reports or blocks anything, and it won't unsubscribe you.

**How you correct it.** In SaneBox you refile mail, and the Daily Digest has bulk actions that train it where to file messages ([SaneBox tour](https://www.sanebox.com/tour)). On feeds, Slop Mop lets you vote Not sure, No, Maybe or Probably, and your vote overrides the model. Voting isn't available for email, so there you read the reasons and decide for yourself.

**Privacy.** SaneBox needs access to your mailbox to do its job, and says it analyses headers and doesn't store email bodies that optional features retrieve ([SaneBox security](https://www.sanebox.com/security)). Slop Mop works the other way round: it looks at the body, not the headers. Before an email is checked, personal details are replaced with placeholders in your mailbox, and only what the sender wrote is sent, never attachments or quoted earlier messages. Spam, private, legal, health and transactional mail are never read unless you check one by hand.

**Price.** SaneBox has no permanent free plan, per the same tracker. Slop Mop is free for the one feed you pick, with 250 checks a day. Email comes with Slop Mop XL, at US$5.99 a month or US$59.99 a year, which also adds every feed, the sites you read and a higher daily limit.

## When SaneBox is the better choice

- You spend too long each day deciding which email to open first.
- You want low-priority mail gathered into one digest instead of scattered through your inbox.
- You want follow-up reminders, Snooze and an easy way to banish a sender.
- You use a provider other than Gmail or Outlook, or do most of your email on a phone.

## When Slop Mop works as a SaneBox alternative

- You already know which mail matters; the problem is long, padded messages that take a paragraph to say nothing.
- You get a lot of [workslop](/glossary/workslop/) or templated cold outreach and want it flagged with reasons.
- You'd like the same check on LinkedIn, X, Reddit or Substack under one subscription.
- You want nothing moved or deleted in your mailbox.
