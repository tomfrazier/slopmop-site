It's tempting to think of slop as a new kind of spam, which makes a spam filter look like the obvious comparison. The two overlap less than the nickname suggests. Spam is mail you didn't ask for, much of it bulk or fraudulent. Slop is usually posted by someone you chose to follow, through the normal channels, and the only harm is the minute it takes to realise there's nothing in it.

## What each one does

**Spam filters** sit on your email provider's servers and move unwanted or dangerous messages (bulk mail, scams, phishing) out of your inbox before you see them. They learn from what people report: Gmail's help pages say that as you report more spam, [Gmail identifies similar emails as spam more efficiently](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/1366858?hl=en). If it gets one wrong, you can mark it Not spam, add the sender to your contacts, or make a filter. Spam filtering comes free with nearly every email service and works on every device you read mail on.

**Slop Mop** is a browser extension that reads posts in your LinkedIn feed before you reach them and judges the writing. An AI model designed for calibrated decisions rates nine tells, from [engagement bait](/patterns/engagement-bait/) to [too-tidy stories](/patterns/too-tidy-story/), and three counter-signals, and gives each post a score out of 100. Likely slop folds into a one-line strip you can reopen, or, in Highlight mode, stays visible with a coloured mop icon.

## Where they differ

**What counts as unwanted.** A spam filter is looking for messages that shouldn't have reached you at all. A post from a former colleague announcing that they've "never been more humbled" isn't spam by any definition: it's from a real account you follow, sent in the ordinary way. Slop Mop is built for exactly that kind of post. It ignores who sent something and looks at what's written.

:::slop
Humbled and honoured to share that I've been named a Top Voice. None of this would be possible without YOU. Drop a 🙌 if you're ready to grow in 2027!
:::
:::fine
We were named a Top Voice for posts on payroll compliance. The two that did best were the checklist for contractors in Germany and the one on holiday accrual errors.
:::

**Safety.** This is where a spam filter does something Slop Mop doesn't attempt. Spam filters are a security tool: they catch phishing and fraud. Slop Mop doesn't check links, senders or attachments and isn't a defence against scams.

**Where they run.** Spam filtering happens on the server for every message, so it's on everywhere without any setup. Slop Mop runs in Chrome and other Chromium browsers, on the feed you choose. It sends the text of each post (never your name or the author's) to its scoring service, which keeps a one-way hash and the scores, not the text.

**What happens to the item.** A spam filter moves mail to a separate Spam folder. Slop Mop never deletes, reports, mutes or blocks anything. It folds the post in place, and one click opens it.

**Coverage today.** As of October 2026, Slop Mop works only on LinkedIn. Slop Mop for Gmail and Outlook is coming soon. When it arrives it would sit on top of your spam filter, judging the legitimate mail that gets through, and won't replace the filter.

## When to use a spam filter

Always. It's already switched on, it's free, and it handles junk and scams better than any reading tool could. If spam is getting through, report it so the filter learns, and use your provider's block and unsubscribe options for senders you no longer want.

## When to use Slop Mop

- Your problem is posts from real people and companies that take time and give nothing back.
- You want to see why something was judged low-value, and overrule it when you disagree.
- You read LinkedIn in a Chromium browser on a computer, which is where Slop Mop works today.

## Using both

They answer different questions, so there's nothing to choose between. The spam filter decides whether a message should reach you at all; Slop Mop, where it works, decides whether something that legitimately reached you is worth reading.

If your inbox problem is newsletters and low-priority mail more than spam, [email triage tools](/compare/slop-mop-vs-email-triage-tools/) such as SaneBox are the closer comparison. If you're wondering whether Slop Mop works out who, or what, wrote a post, see [Slop Mop vs AI detectors](/compare/slop-mop-vs-ai-detectors/).
