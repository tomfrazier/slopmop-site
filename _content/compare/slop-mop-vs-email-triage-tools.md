Email triage tools and Slop Mop both aim to give you back time spent on mail that wasn't worth it. They come at it from opposite ends. Triage tools decide which senders deserve your attention. Slop Mop decides whether a particular piece of writing does. That makes them easier to combine than to choose between.

## What email triage tools and Slop Mop each do

**Newsletter unsubscribe and email triage tools** connect to your mailbox and act on senders and categories. Examples include [SaneBox](/compare/slop-mop-vs-sanebox/), which sorts incoming mail by importance, and [Unroll.Me](/compare/slop-mop-vs-unroll-me/), which lists your subscriptions so you can unsubscribe in bulk or roll them into a daily digest. They work with Gmail, Outlook and other providers. Pricing ranges from free to paid subscription, and the business models differ: [Unroll.Me's data page](https://unroll.me/your-data) says it's owned by NielsenIQ and uses data from your commercial emails for market research and advertising products, while [SaneBox](https://www.sanebox.com/) is a paid service with a free trial.

**Slop Mop for Gmail and Slop Mop for Outlook** are add-ons that check what the sender wrote. An AI model designed for calibrated decisions rates nine tells of slop, such as [hype words](/patterns/hype-words/) and [stiff phrasing](/patterns/stiff-phrasing/), and three counter-signals, then gives a score out of 100, the strongest signs and a verdict. The email add-ons are part of Slop Mop XL, which also covers LinkedIn, X, Reddit, Facebook, Substack, Medium and the sites you read. [How it works](/how-it-works.html) explains the score.

## Email triage tools vs Slop Mop: where they differ

**Senders versus writing.** Triage tools judge the sender: is this someone you reply to, a newsletter you read, a shop you bought from once? That works well for clearing out whole categories. It can't help with the newsletter you subscribed to on purpose that has slipped into filler, or the internal update from a colleague that says nothing in 600 words (what's sometimes called [workslop](/glossary/workslop/)). Judging each message on its writing is what Slop Mop is built to do.

:::slop
Hi team! Just circling back to touch base on our Q3 learnings journey. Moving forward, let's continue to align on synergies and keep the momentum going! 🚀
:::
:::fine
Q3: we shipped the export fix and missed the SSO deadline by two weeks. SSO is now due 14 November; Priya owns it. No action needed from you.
:::

Both come from the same sender, so a sender-based tool treats them the same.

**What happens to the mail.** This is where triage tools are stronger. They move, roll up, block or unsubscribe, so the clutter actually leaves your inbox. Slop Mop never deletes, moves, blocks, reports or unsubscribes anything. It tells you how a message scores and why, and leaves the decision with you.

**Reasons and control.** Slop Mop shows the score and strongest signs for each message. You can't vote on an email score the way you can on a feed post; voting isn't available for email. Triage tools generally let you train them by moving mail between folders or marking senders.

**What gets read.** Slop Mop is careful about what it looks at. Before an email is checked, personal details are replaced with placeholders in your mailbox, and only what the sender wrote is sent: never attachments, and never the earlier messages quoted below. Spam, private, legal, health and transactional mail are never read unless you check one by hand. Data handling varies a lot between triage tools, from paid services to free ones funded by data use, so read each tool's terms before connecting your mailbox.

**Devices and cost.** Triage tools work at the mailbox, so their sorting shows up wherever you read mail, phone included. Email is part of Slop Mop XL at US$5.99 a month or US$59.99 a year; the free plan covers one feed of your choice with 250 checks a day.

## When to use an email triage tool

- Newsletters, notifications and promotions are swamping your inbox.
- You want to unsubscribe from many senders at once.
- You'd like low-priority mail moved out of the way and summarised in a digest.
- You want the sorting to follow you to your phone.

## When to use Slop Mop

- You keep a newsletter or a colleague's updates on purpose, but don't always want to read them in full.
- You'd like a quick read on whether a message is worth your time, with the reasons shown.
- The same filler bothers you in your feeds and on the sites you read, and you want one tool for all of it.

## Using both

The two fit together well. A triage tool clears out senders you don't want, and Slop Mop judges the writing of what's left. Neither replaces your [spam filter](/compare/slop-mop-vs-spam-filters/), which handles junk and scams. If you'd rather move newsletters out of email entirely, [feed readers](/compare/slop-mop-vs-rss-readers/) are another option. For the mail you keep, add [Slop Mop for Gmail and Outlook](/) with Slop Mop XL and it checks what each sender wrote.
