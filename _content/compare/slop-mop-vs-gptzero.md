## What each one does

GPTZero and Slop Mop both read text and give it a score. GPTZero's score is about who or what wrote it. Slop Mop's is about whether it was worth reading.

**GPTZero** is an AI-content detector best known in education. You paste in text or upload a document, and it returns an overall AI score with sentence-level highlighting that shows where it thinks AI was used, plus a written explanation of why. Around that core it offers a plagiarism checker, grammar checking and writing feedback, and an authorship tool that replays how a Google Doc was written. Its Chrome extension shows AI detection and typing-pattern information on web pages and in Google Docs, and there are LMS integrations and an API. GPTZero's homepage also says it "checks writing quality". There's a free tier (scans over 10,000 characters need a free account) and paid individual and team plans.

**Slop Mop** is a browser extension that works on your LinkedIn feed as you scroll. Before you reach a post, it sends the text to its scoring service, where an AI model designed for calibrated decisions answers twelve questions: nine tells of slop and three counter-signals (sounds like a person, useful to readers, reader response). Likely slop is folded into a one-line strip you can reopen, or, in Highlight mode, marked and left visible.

## Where they differ

**The question.** GPTZero is built to tell AI writing from human writing. Slop Mop isn't an AI detector and judges the writing itself: a useful post written with AI help survives, and an empty one typed by hand can be folded. If authorship is what you need to know, GPTZero answers it and Slop Mop doesn't.

**Passive or on demand.** GPTZero is mostly something you reach for: you have a document and you want to check it. Slop Mop runs in the background on a feed you're already reading, and acts on each post without being asked.

**What you get back.** GPTZero gives a probability that text is AI-written and marks the sentences behind it. Slop Mop gives a slop score out of 100, a verdict (Likely slop, Possibly slop, Looks fine or Not sure), and the Slopprint, a chart of all nine tells, so you can see what kind of slop a post is. It can also fold the post away, which a detector isn't designed to do.

**Coverage.** GPTZero works on any text you can paste or upload, and its extension reaches web pages and Google Docs. Slop Mop is live on LinkedIn only; X, Reddit, Facebook, Substack, Medium, websites and Gmail and Outlook are coming soon.

**Audience.** GPTZero is aimed at teachers, students, institutions and businesses checking documents. Slop Mop is aimed at people who read a lot of social posts and want fewer hooks, bait and filler.

**Price.** Both have a free way in. GPTZero lists its paid plans on its pricing page. Slop Mop is free for one feed with 250 checks a day, and Slop Mop XL (coming soon) will be US$5.99 a month or US$59.99 a year.

**How much weight to give the result.** GPTZero is open about the limits of detection. Its homepage says no AI detector is 100% accurate, that accuracy is better on longer English text, and that results shouldn't be used to punish or as the final verdict. Slop Mop's verdicts are lower-stakes by design: a folded post is one click from being reopened, your vote overrides the model in your feed, and nothing is ever deleted, reported, muted or blocked.

## When to use GPTZero

- You're a teacher or lecturer checking coursework and want sentence-level detail to start a conversation with a student.
- You need plagiarism checking alongside AI detection.
- You want to see how a Google Doc was written, not only what it says.
- You're checking a document, not a feed.

If you're in that position, our glossary entry on [AI suspicion](/glossary/ai-suspicion/) is worth a read on what can go wrong when a score is treated as proof.

## When to use Slop Mop

- You want your LinkedIn feed to show fewer scroll-stoppers, engagement bait and hype.
- You'd rather judge posts on value than on whether AI was involved.
- You want something that works while you scroll, with no pasting.
- You want each call explained and easy to overrule.

## Using both

They rarely compete. A teacher might use GPTZero on essays during the week and Slop Mop to read LinkedIn on the train home; the tools never look at the same text. Where they might meet is a post you find suspicious in your feed: Slop Mop will tell you whether it's worth your time, and GPTZero will give you its view on whether AI wrote it.
