Feed blockers and Slop Mop start from the same complaint, that the feed takes more than it gives, and reach different conclusions. A feed blocker says the feed is the problem and removes it. Slop Mop says some of the posts are the problem and folds those. Which suits you depends on whether you want the feed at all.

## What feed blockers and Slop Mop each do

**Feed blockers and hiders** are browser extensions that remove a site's feed or recommendation panels, so you can still use the rest of the site. [News Feed Eradicator](/compare/slop-mop-vs-news-feed-eradicator/) replaces the feed on Facebook, X and other social networks [with an inspirational quote](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/fjcldmjmjhkklehbacihaiopjklihlgg). [Unhook](/compare/slop-mop-vs-unhook/) does a similar job on YouTube, [removing recommendations and Shorts](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/khncfooichmfjbepaaaebmommgaepoid). Smaller extensions hide particular post types, such as promoted or suggested posts on LinkedIn. Most are free and run entirely in your browser.

**Slop Mop** keeps your feed and reads each post before you reach it, on LinkedIn, X, Reddit, Facebook, Substack and Medium. An AI model designed for calibrated decisions rates nine tells of slop, such as [scroll-stopper hooks](/patterns/scroll-stopper/) and [engagement bait](/patterns/engagement-bait/), and three counter-signals, then scores the post out of 100. Likely slop folds into a one-line strip you can reopen; in Highlight mode, it stays visible and its mop icon turns yellow or red. It also judges the articles you read and the mail in Gmail and Outlook. [How it works](/how-it-works.html) explains the score.

## Slop Mop vs feed blockers: where they differ

**Remove or filter.** A feed blocker removes everything in the feed, the good posts with the bad. That's the point: no feed, nothing to scroll. Slop Mop leaves the posts it judges worth reading exactly as they were and folds the rest. You still have a feed to scroll; there's just less in it to scroll past.

**The goal.** Feed blockers are built for attention and habit. They help people who open a site for one thing and lose half an hour to [doomscrolling](/glossary/doomscrolling/). Slop Mop is built for signal-to-noise, for people who want the feed and find too much of it is hooks and filler. It does nothing to limit how long you scroll.

**Judgement.** Feed blockers make no judgements, so they can never misjudge a post, and they need no server. Slop Mop judges every post, which means it can sometimes call one differently from you. It shows its reasons when you click the mop, and your vote overrides the model in your own feed. To judge posts it sends their text (never your name or the author's) to its scoring service, which keeps a one-way hash and the scores rather than the text.

**Reach.** The overlap is bigger than it was. Both work on Facebook and X. Slop Mop also covers LinkedIn, Reddit, Substack, Medium, the sites you read and your inbox. Feed blockers reach places Slop Mop doesn't, YouTube above all: Slop Mop reads text and has nothing to say about a page of video thumbnails.

**Cost and limits.** Feed blockers win here. Most are free with no limits. Slop Mop is free for one feed with 250 checks a day. Slop Mop XL, at US$5.99 a month or US$59.99 a year, adds every feed, the sites you read, Gmail and Outlook, and a higher daily limit.

## When to use a feed blocker

- You want to stop scrolling a site altogether while still using its search, messages or profiles.
- Your main concern is time spent, more than what you read.
- You want YouTube's recommendations and Shorts gone.
- You want nothing sent off your machine.

## When to use Slop Mop

- You read a feed on purpose, for work, hiring, a hobby subreddit or keeping up with people, and want the good posts.
- You're fine with the time you spend, but not with how much of it goes on slop.
- You'd like the reasons for each call, and the option to overrule it.

## Using both

On the same site they pull in opposite directions: once a feed blocker has removed the feed, Slop Mop has nothing to judge. Across different sites, they can work well together. Some people block the feeds they only scroll out of habit and keep the one they read for work, such as LinkedIn or Reddit, with Slop Mop folding the slop.

If what bothers you is ads more than posts, see [Slop Mop vs ad blockers](/compare/slop-mop-vs-ad-blockers/). If you'd keep the feed but drop particular people, [unfollowing and muting](/compare/slop-mop-vs-unfollowing-and-muting/) is the simpler fix. For the feeds you keep, [add Slop Mop to your browser](/) and it folds the slop in them.
