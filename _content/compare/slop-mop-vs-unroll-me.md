Unroll.Me and Slop Mop both promise a quieter inbox, in different ways. Unroll.Me works sender by sender: keep this newsletter, block that one. Slop Mop for Gmail and Outlook reads what each sender wrote and flags the filler. If you're looking for an Unroll.Me alternative because of its data terms, Slop Mop covers some of the same ground, but it won't unsubscribe you from anything.

## Slop Mop vs Unroll.Me: what each one does

**Unroll.Me** is a free email tool. It finds the subscription emails in your inbox and lists them, and for each one you choose to keep it, block it, or "roll it up" into a single daily digest with the others ([Unroll.Me](https://unroll.me/)). It supports the major email providers and has a mobile app.

It's worth reading how it's paid for before you sign up. Unroll.Me's data page says it's owned by NielsenIQ, an e-commerce measurement and consumer data company. When you register, it accesses your commercial emails (subscriptions, purchase receipts, travel bookings, shipping confirmations) and uses that data in advertising products, measurement products and datasets for its clients. It shares information about the commercial emails you receive, with some demographic details, with clients including e-commerce businesses and data brokers for market analysis. The same page says your identifiable personal information will never be sold or shared to target you directly, and that it isn't interested in your personal emails ([Unroll.Me data page](https://unroll.me/your-data)).

**Slop Mop** judges writing. In Gmail and Outlook, through the Slop Mop for Gmail and Slop Mop for Outlook add-ons, it checks what the sender wrote: an AI model designed for calibrated decisions answers twelve questions about it, nine tells of slop, such as [engagement bait](/patterns/engagement-bait/) and [no-catch promises](/patterns/no-catch-promises/), and three counter-signals, such as whether the email is useful to readers. You get a score out of 100 and the reasons behind it. The same check runs on LinkedIn, X, Reddit, Facebook, Substack, Medium and the articles you read, where Hide mode folds likely slop into a one-line strip and Highlight mode marks it. [How it works](/how-it-works.html) goes through the twelve questions.

## Where Slop Mop and Unroll.Me differ

**Where they work.** Unroll.Me supports the major email providers and has a phone app. Slop Mop's email support covers Gmail and Outlook, on desktop. If your mail is elsewhere, or you do most of it on a phone, Unroll.Me fits better.

**Sender versus writing.** Unroll.Me works at the level of the sender: this newsletter, every issue, kept, blocked or rolled up. That's quick, and for most subscription clutter it's the right level, because you usually know which senders you're done with. Slop Mop works at the level of each piece of writing. It reads the email and judges whether it's built on hooks, bait and padding or says something useful, so one issue of a newsletter can be flagged while the next is left alone. If you already know you're done with a sender, unsubscribing beats any amount of flagging.

**Who decides.** Every call in Unroll.Me is yours, made once per sender. Slop Mop makes a call on every post for you, which saves effort but can be wrong. It shows its reasons and says Not sure and leaves an item alone when it isn't confident. On feeds you can vote to overrule it; voting isn't available for email.

**What happens to the item.** Unroll.Me can block a sender and bundle the rest into a digest. Slop Mop never deletes, reports, mutes or blocks anything, and doesn't move mail between folders.

**Data and money.** Unroll.Me is free to use, and its own data page describes the commercial-email data use above. Slop Mop is paid for by subscriptions, and handles email narrowly. Before an email is checked, personal details are replaced with placeholders in your mailbox, and only what the sender wrote is sent, never attachments or quoted earlier messages. Spam, private, legal, health and transactional mail, which covers the receipts and shipping confirmations Unroll.Me's data page mentions, are never read unless you check one by hand ([Slop Mop privacy](/privacy.html)). Slop Mop is free for one feed with 250 checks a day; email comes with Slop Mop XL, at US$5.99 a month or US$59.99 a year, which also adds every feed, the sites you read and a higher daily limit. Unroll.Me costs nothing, so the trade is money against data.

## When Unroll.Me is the better choice

- Your inbox is crowded with newsletters and promotions you signed up for and no longer read.
- You'd rather make one decision per sender than one per email.
- You want some subscriptions kept but bundled into a single daily email.
- You've read its data page and you're comfortable with the trade.

## When Slop Mop works as an Unroll.Me alternative

- You want to keep your newsletters but stop reading the padded issues to the end.
- You'd rather not hand a data company your commercial email, and you're willing to pay instead.
- You want writing judged one piece at a time, so a good issue from a noisy sender still gets through.
- The same clutter bothers you on LinkedIn, X or Substack, where you can't unsubscribe from a post.
