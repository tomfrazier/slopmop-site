Unroll.Me and Slop Mop both promise less clutter, but in different places and in different ways. Unroll.Me works on email today. Slop Mop for Gmail and Outlook is coming soon, but it isn't live: as of October 2026, Slop Mop works only on LinkedIn. So for an inbox full of newsletters right now, Unroll.Me is the one that can act.

## What each one does

**Unroll.Me** is a free email tool. It finds the subscription emails in your inbox and lists them, and for each one you choose to keep it, block it, or "roll it up" into a single daily digest with the others ([Unroll.Me](https://unroll.me/)). It supports the major email providers and has a mobile app.

It's worth reading how it's paid for before you sign up. Unroll.Me's data page says it's owned by NielsenIQ, an e-commerce measurement and consumer data company. When you register, it accesses your commercial emails (subscriptions, purchase receipts, travel bookings, shipping confirmations) and uses that data in advertising products, measurement products and datasets for its clients. It shares information about the commercial emails you receive, with some demographic details, with clients including e-commerce businesses and data brokers for market analysis. The same page says your identifiable personal information will never be sold or shared to target you directly, and that it isn't interested in your personal emails ([Unroll.Me data page](https://unroll.me/your-data)).

**Slop Mop** is a Chrome and Chromium extension that reads each LinkedIn post as you scroll. An AI model designed for calibrated decisions answers twelve questions about the writing: nine tells of slop, such as [engagement bait](/patterns/engagement-bait/) and [no-catch promises](/patterns/no-catch-promises/), and three counter-signals, such as whether the post is useful to readers. Each post gets a score out of 100. Hide mode folds likely slop into a one-line strip you can reopen; Highlight mode marks it and hides nothing. Slop Mop for Gmail and Outlook is planned for the same kind of filler in email; it's coming soon, not live.

## Where they differ

**Availability.** Unroll.Me works on email now. Slop Mop doesn't yet. That settles it for anyone who needs an inbox fix this week.

**Sender versus writing.** Unroll.Me works at the level of the sender: this newsletter, every issue, kept, blocked or rolled up. That's quick, and for most subscription clutter it's the right level, because you usually know which senders you're done with. Slop Mop works at the level of each piece of writing. It reads the post and judges whether it's built on hooks, bait and padding or says something useful, so one post from someone can be folded while their next is left alone.

**Who decides.** Every call in Unroll.Me is yours, made once per sender. Slop Mop makes a call on every post for you, which saves effort but can be wrong. It shows its reasons when you click the mop icon, says Not sure and leaves a post alone when it isn't confident, and lets you vote to overrule it in your own feed.

**What happens to the item.** Unroll.Me can block a sender. Slop Mop never deletes, reports, mutes or blocks anything; it only changes what you see in your browser, and a folded post is one click from being read.

**Data and money.** Unroll.Me is free to use, and its own data page describes the commercial-email data use above. Slop Mop sends post text to its scoring service, but never your name or the author's. Its server keeps a one-way hash of the text and the scores; the one exception is a post where you vote against the score, whose text is kept so the miss can be reviewed ([Slop Mop privacy](/privacy.html)). Slop Mop is free for one feed with 250 checks a day; Slop Mop XL, coming soon, will be US$5.99 a month or US$59.99 a year and will add Slop Mop for Gmail and Outlook.

## When to use Unroll.Me

- Your inbox is crowded with newsletters and promotions you signed up for and no longer read.
- You'd rather make one decision per sender than one per email.
- You want some subscriptions kept but bundled into a single daily email.
- You've read its data page and you're comfortable with the trade.

## When to use Slop Mop

- The clutter you want gone is in your LinkedIn feed, where you can't unsubscribe from a post.
- You want writing judged one piece at a time, so a good post from a noisy author still gets through.
- You want to see why each call was made and correct it.
- For email, Slop Mop is a future option. If you want it there, watch for its Gmail and Outlook support.

## Using both

Today they cover different places: Unroll.Me for subscription email, Slop Mop for LinkedIn. Once Slop Mop's email support arrives they'd fit in sequence, with the sender-level clear-out first and the writing-level judgement on what you chose to keep.
