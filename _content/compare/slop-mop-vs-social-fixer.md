Social Fixer is one of the oldest feed-tidying extensions around. It began in 2009, under the name Better Facebook, and Matt Kruse still builds it as a personal project ([Social Fixer](https://socialfixer.com/about.html)). Slop Mop started in September 2026. They also take different approaches: Social Fixer gives you rules to write, and Slop Mop makes a judgement about each post.

## Slop Mop vs Social Fixer: what each one does

**Social Fixer** is a free browser extension that changes how the Facebook website looks and behaves. Its features include hiding sponsored posts, hiding political posts, filtering the feed by keyword, author and other properties, switching to the Most Recent feed, hiding parts of the page, and not showing posts again once you've read them ([Social Fixer](https://socialfixer.com/)). You can write filters yourself or pick from shared filter subscriptions. It runs on Chrome, Firefox, Safari, Opera and Edge, or as a user script, on desktop only ([download page](https://socialfixer.com/download.html)). Its author says your data is stored in your browser and never sent to any other site ([FAQ](https://socialfixer.com/faq.html)). It's free and supported by donations and ads on its website.

**Slop Mop** is a browser extension for Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari and Chromium browsers that reads each post as you scroll. An AI model designed for calibrated decisions answers twelve questions about the writing: nine tells of slop, such as [engagement bait](/patterns/engagement-bait/) and [no-catch promises](/patterns/no-catch-promises/), and three counter-signals, such as whether the post is useful to readers. Each post gets a score out of 100. Hide mode folds likely slop into a one-line strip you can reopen; Highlight mode marks it and hides nothing. On Facebook it reads the posts in your feed, including posts from groups and pages; it does the same on LinkedIn, X, Reddit, Substack and Medium, on the articles you open, and in Gmail and Outlook. [How it works](/how-it-works.html) explains the scoring.

## Where Slop Mop and Social Fixer differ

**Site.** Both work on the Facebook website on desktop, and neither works in the Facebook phone app. Social Fixer is Facebook only, and goes much deeper there. Slop Mop does one thing on Facebook and the same thing on your other feeds.

**Rules versus judgement.** A Social Fixer filter does exactly what you tell it: hide posts containing this word, from this author, of this type. That's precise and predictable, and you know why every post vanished. The limit is that slop rarely shares a keyword. A post can be pure bait without any word you'd think to block, and a word you block will also catch good posts that use it. Slop Mop looks at how a post is written (hooks, bait, [hype words](/patterns/hype-words/), padding) and weighs that against signs it's useful. It can be wrong, so it shows its reasons on request, says Not sure when it isn't confident, and lets you vote to overrule it.

**Range.** Social Fixer is a toolkit with dozens of tweaks to Facebook's interface, from tabbed feeds to a friend manager. Slop Mop does one job: score posts and fold or mark the weak ones.

**Upkeep.** As of October 2026, Social Fixer's download page lists version 31.1.0, released 8 September 2023; the page notes that browsers may update the extension automatically, so it may not show the latest ([download page](https://socialfixer.com/download.html)). Facebook changes its pages often, so check the release notes and the support group before relying on any one feature.

**Privacy.** Social Fixer processes the page in your browser. Slop Mop sends post text (never your name or the author's) to its scoring service; the server keeps a one-way hash of the text and the scores, except for posts where you vote against the score.

**Price.** Social Fixer is free. Slop Mop is free for one feed with 250 checks a day, so Facebook alone costs nothing; Slop Mop XL is US$5.99 a month or US$59.99 a year for every feed, the sites you read, and Gmail and Outlook.

## When to use Social Fixer

- You use Facebook on a desktop browser and want fine control over its layout and sections.
- You know what you want gone: sponsored posts, political posts, a particular page, a recurring topic.
- You'd like to see only posts you haven't read yet.
- You want filters that run entirely in your browser.

## When to use Slop Mop

- Your Facebook feed, or your LinkedIn or X feed, is full of filler that's hard to describe as a list of words.
- You want posts judged one by one, including from people you'd never mute.
- You want to see why each call was made and correct the ones you disagree with.

## Using both

They can share a page. Use Social Fixer's rules for things you can name, such as sponsored posts and particular topics, and Slop Mop's judgement for posts that are simply empty. Expect some posts to be caught by both; Slop Mop only folds or marks what's still on the page.
