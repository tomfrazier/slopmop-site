## What your feed looks like

A recruiter reads three streams for work. LinkedIn is the main one: you use it to see who just left a company, who is quietly open to a move, which clients are hiring and which have gone silent. Reddit is where candidates talk more frankly, in subreddits for their profession or city, about pay, interview loops and why they're leaving. Your inbox carries the rest: replies from candidates, briefs from hiring managers, and a growing pile of outreach from other agencies and sourcing tools.

The trouble is that recruiting itself produces a lot of the noise. The usual crowd on LinkedIn:

- Other recruiters posting "hot jobs" with a row of fire emojis and a request to comment "interested".
- Talent leaders posting the same three opinions about ghosting, counter-offers and culture fit, each with a dramatic opening line.
- Candidate-advice accounts promising a "secret" CV format if you comment a keyword.
- Hiring stories where a candidate did one surprising thing in an interview and got the job on the spot.

Reddit has its own versions: the career post that turns out to be an ad for a course, and the long "how I got hired" story that reads as if it was assembled to collect upvotes. In email, it's the vendor sequence that opens with "I hope this finds you well" and the AI-drafted candidate pitch that could describe anyone.

Underneath all that are the posts you actually came for: an engineer explaining why they left, a hiring manager describing what the team is building, a company posting a plain update about a new office. Those are often short and unpolished, and they get buried by posts built to win the algorithm.

## Examples

:::slop
🔥 HIRING 🔥 Senior Backend Engineer, remote, amazing culture, rockstar team! Comment "ME" below and I'll reach out! ♻️ Repost to help someone in your network land their dream role!
:::
:::fine
We're hiring a backend engineer for the payments team in Leeds, hybrid two days a week. The stack is Go and Postgres. Salary band is £70k to £85k. The hiring manager is Priya, and she'll do the first call herself.
:::

:::slop
A candidate once showed up 10 minutes late to an interview. Everyone wrote her off. Then she said something I'll never forget. That day I learned that resilience beats punctuality every time.
:::
:::fine
Three of our last five offers were declined because the final round was scheduled more than a week after the first. We've cut the process to two stages and now book the final round on the same call.
:::

The first pair shows [engagement bait](/patterns/engagement-bait/) dressed as a job ad. The second shows a [too-tidy story](/patterns/too-tidy-story/), the anecdote that exists to deliver a moral. The fine versions give a reader something to act on: a salary band, a named manager, a change to a process.

## How Slop Mop helps recruiters

Slop Mop is a browser extension for Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari and Chromium browsers such as Brave and Arc, on desktop. It reads each post before you reach it and scores the writing against nine tells of slop and three counter-signals. Click the mop beside a post and you see the score and the reasons. [How it works](/how-it-works.html) explains each part of the panel.

On the networks a recruiter uses:

- **LinkedIn.** It reads the posts in your feed and marks or folds the ones that look like slop. LinkedIn's own [feed preferences](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/72151) let you unfollow and hide by author; Slop Mop works post by post, so a useful contact's one bad post doesn't cost you all their others.
- **Reddit.** It reads posts and comments in your feed, which helps in long career threads where the useful first-hand comment sits under a dozen generic ones. See [Slop Mop for Reddit](/for/reddit/).
- **Email.** Slop Mop for Gmail and Slop Mop for Outlook check what the sender wrote. Before a message is checked, personal details are replaced with placeholders in your mailbox, and attachments and quoted earlier messages are never sent. Private, legal, health and transactional mail is never read unless you check one by hand. See [Slop Mop for email](/for/email/).

It judges the writing, not the person or whether AI was involved. That matters in your job. A candidate who used AI to tidy up an "I'm looking" post still gets a fair read if the post says what they do, where and for whom. Slop Mop isn't an AI detector and doesn't touch anyone's profile. It never deletes, reports, mutes or blocks anything.

## Setting up a LinkedIn feed filter for recruiters

Start with **Highlight** mode on **Moderate** sensitivity.

Highlight hides nothing. It tints each post's mop yellow for Possibly slop and red for Likely slop. For a recruiter that's the safer start, because some of the people you most want to find write badly. A nervous job-seeker might open with a hook because they've been told hooks work. You still want to see that post. A red mop just tells you to read past the opening line.

Moderate is a sensible middle. Mild flags only the most obvious bait, which leaves most "hot jobs" posts unmarked. Aggressive flags more, which is fine in Highlight mode since nothing disappears, but the colour starts to lose meaning if half your feed is yellow.

After a week, if the red posts are almost all recruiter spam and influencer advice, switch to **Hide**. Folded posts sit in a one-line strip you can reopen with one click, so you can still check anything that looks like it might be a candidate.

Use your vote. Click the mop on a post and vote No if Slop Mop got a candidate's post wrong. Your vote overrides the score in your own feed, and when it disagrees with the verdict a person at Artimovo reviews it. Voting isn't available for email.

Pick your plan by where you spend the day:

- **Free** covers the one feed you pick, with 250 checks a day, resetting at midnight UTC. For most recruiters that feed is LinkedIn. If you scroll it for hours, you may hit the limit in the afternoon.
- **Slop Mop XL** costs US$5.99 a month or US$59.99 a year. It adds every feed (LinkedIn, Reddit and the rest), the sites you read, Gmail and Outlook, and a higher daily limit, on up to five browsers. It suits a recruiter who sources on Reddit and works from a busy inbox as well.

## If you post too

Your job ads and market updates are read by candidates who see dozens a day. The ones that get replies tend to say the salary, the location, the stack and who the hiring manager is.

- Drop the comment-a-keyword request. A plain "apply here" link or "message me" is enough. See [engagement bait](/patterns/engagement-bait/).
- Skip "rockstar", "fast-paced" and "exciting opportunity". They read as [hype words](/patterns/hype-words/) and say nothing about the role.
- If you share a hiring story, keep the detail and lose the moral. A real number from your own process is more useful than a candidate who said something unforgettable.
- Avoid opening with a single dramatic line and a pause. The [scroll-stopper](/patterns/scroll-stopper/) page shows what that looks like.
- The same goes for outreach emails. A first line about the role and the salary gets read; a first line about hoping they're well gets skipped.

If you worry that candidates will assume your outreach came from a chatbot (the habit described in [AI suspicion](/glossary/ai-suspicion/)), specific detail is the quickest way to show a person wrote it. [Add Slop Mop to your browser](/) and it marks the hot-jobs bait in your feed while the candidate posts stay in view.
