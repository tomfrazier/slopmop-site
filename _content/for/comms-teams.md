## What your feed looks like

Communications and PR people read LinkedIn differently from everyone else. You're watching for mentions of your organisation, what competitors are announcing, what journalists on your patch are posting, and how your own leaders' posts are landing. You also write a fair share of what appears there, under other people's names.

That puts you on both sides of the slop problem. The feed you monitor is thick with announcement language: "thrilled to announce", "proud to partner with", "an exciting milestone", "humbled". Competitor launches and agency case studies use the same dozen phrases. Employee-advocacy programmes produce waves of near-identical reshares with "So proud to be part of this team!" on top. Meanwhile the posts that matter to you, such as a customer complaint gaining traction, a journalist asking for comment or a former employee describing a policy, are often short and blunt, and easy to scroll past.

What you want from the feed is early warning and an honest read of tone. What you want from your own posts is for readers to finish them.

## Examples

:::slop
We're thrilled to announce our strategic partnership with a world-class leader in sustainable innovation. Together, we will unlock new possibilities and deliver unparalleled value to our customers. 🌱
:::
:::fine
From January, our delivery vans in Bristol and Cardiff will charge at their depot overnight on a tariff we've agreed with the local energy co-op. It covers 140 vans. The rest of the fleet stays diesel until at least 2028.
:::

:::slop
I'm incredibly proud of our amazing team. This year has been a truly remarkable journey, and I couldn't be more grateful. Here's to an even brighter future together!
:::
:::fine
Our customer support team answered 41,000 tickets this year with two fewer people than last year. I asked them what to fix first in 2027. The answer was the returns form, so that's the first project.
:::

The slop examples are almost pure announcement language: hype words, empty praise and a closing line that could end any post. The fine versions answer the questions a reader or reporter would ask next: where, how many, when, and what's not changing.

## How Slop Mop helps

Slop Mop is a browser extension for Chrome and other Chromium browsers. It reads each post in your LinkedIn feed before you reach it, rates it on nine tells of slop and three counter-signals, and gives a score and a verdict. Click the mop beside the post to see which tells drove it, shown as a Slopprint chart.

For a comms team that panel is the useful part. It shows you, post by post, which habits make corporate writing read as filler. It judges the writing, so it doesn't matter whether a post was drafted by a person, a team or a model. It isn't an AI detector.

It's live on the main feed of desktop LinkedIn. X, Reddit, Facebook, Substack, Medium, websites and email are coming soon, so it won't help with press inboxes or other social channels yet. It never deletes, reports, mutes or blocks posts, which matters if you're reading about your own organisation. The free version covers one feed with 250 checks a day per install, so each person on a team installs their own.

## Setting it up for communications and PR teams

Start with **Highlight** mode at **Mild** sensitivity.

Highlight hides nothing, which is the right default for anyone monitoring. A sloppy post about your company is still a post about your company, and you need to see it. With Mild, only the clearest cases get a yellow or red mop, so the marks stay meaningful and your feed doesn't fill with colour.

Use the marks as a tone check on announcements in your sector. If a competitor's launch post scores red, open the panel and look at the Slopprint. It's a quick lesson in which phrases to keep out of your own.

Vote when it gets one wrong. A **No** on a blunt, useful post that was marked overrides the score in your feed. Votes that disagree with the verdict are reviewed by a person at Artimovo. You can't vote on your own posts; the panel shows no vote there.

Team members who use LinkedIn mainly for reading can switch to **Hide** at **Moderate** for a shorter scroll.

## If you post too

Most comms teams post every day, for the company page and for leaders. Readers who use Slop Mop will see those posts scored like anyone else's. The tells that show up most in corporate writing:

- **"Thrilled", "proud", "humbled"** in the first line. Lead with the fact. See [empty praise](/patterns/empty-praise/).
- **Partnership and launch vocabulary**: "unlock", "unparalleled", "world-class". See [hype words](/patterns/hype-words/).
- **Approved-statement hedging**: "may help to support positive outcomes". Say what changes and for whom. See [stiff phrasing](/patterns/stiff-phrasing/).
- **Leadership anecdotes with a lesson attached**, especially when ghostwritten. See [too-tidy story](/patterns/too-tidy-story/).

When you draft for an executive, ask them for one detail only they would know: a place, a number, a name they'd be happy to share. That detail does more for the post than any amount of polish.
