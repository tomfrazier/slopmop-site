## What your feed looks like

For anyone who sells, LinkedIn is part of the job. You're there to notice when a prospect changes role, when a target account starts hiring for the team you sell to, when a customer complains about a competitor, or when someone you met at an event posts about a problem you can solve. X does the same for buyers in tech and media, who complain about tools in public. Reddit's industry subreddits are where buyers ask each other which product to pick, often more frankly than they'd tell a rep. And email is where the deal actually moves.

The trouble is that sellers follow other sellers, and sales content is its own genre:

- Sales influencers posting "the one email that booked me 40 meetings", with the email available if you comment a keyword.
- Quota-crushing stories with a big deal size and a lesson about grit.
- Lists of objection-handling lines that promise to work on any buyer.
- Vendor posts about "the death of cold calling", usually followed by a pitch for something that replaces it.
- In your own inbox, the vendor sequences pitching you sales tools, and AI-drafted "quick question" emails from other reps.

The signals you need are quieter. "We're rebuilding our data team" from a VP at a target account. A customer's post about rolling out a new tool to 200 people. A prospect asking on Reddit whether anyone has used a product like yours. Those posts don't use hooks, and they can be hard to find between the motivational ones.

## Examples

:::slop
This ONE cold email got a 73% reply rate. 📈 No fluff. No tricks. Works in every industry. Comment "EMAIL" and I'll send you the exact template. ♻️ Repost for your SDRs.
:::
:::fine
We tested a three-line first email against our usual six-line one across about 400 prospects in logistics. The short one got slightly more replies but fewer meetings, because people replied to ask what we did. We now use the short one only for follow-ups.
:::

:::slop
I lost a $500k deal on the last day of the quarter. I cried in my car. Then my mentor said six words that changed my career forever.
:::
:::fine
Our procurement cycle with NHS trusts runs about five months from first call to signature. The step that slows it most is the information-governance review, so we now send our security pack before the first demo.
:::

The first slop example is [engagement bait](/patterns/engagement-bait/) on top of a [no-catch promise](/patterns/no-catch-promises/): a big number that works everywhere and costs nothing. The second is a [too-tidy story](/patterns/too-tidy-story/) built to deliver a moral. The fine posts are the ones a buyer or a colleague could actually use.

## How Slop Mop helps sales teams

Slop Mop is a browser extension for Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari and Chromium browsers such as Brave, Arc and Comet, on desktop. It reads each post before you reach it and scores the writing against nine tells of slop and three counter-signals. [How it works](/how-it-works.html) explains the score.

Where sellers look:

- **LinkedIn and X.** It reads the posts in your feed and timeline. One counter-signal, "reader response", counts comments and reposts for more than one-tap reactions, and discounts posts with hundreds of reactions and almost no comments. That's what a post propped up by an engagement pod tends to look like.
- **Reddit.** It reads posts and comments, so in a "which tool should we buy" thread the buyer's detailed answer stands out from the vendor plants. See [Slop Mop for Reddit](/for/reddit/).
- **Email.** Slop Mop for Gmail and Slop Mop for Outlook check what the sender wrote, which clears the vendor sequences in your own inbox. Before a message is checked, personal details are replaced with placeholders in your mailbox, and attachments and quoted earlier messages are never sent. Private, legal and transactional mail (contracts and order confirmations included) is never read unless you check one by hand. See [Slop Mop for email](/for/email/).

Slop Mop doesn't see your CRM, doesn't track prospects and doesn't send anyone's name to its scoring service. It never deletes, reports, mutes or blocks anything.

## Setting up a LinkedIn feed filter for sales teams

Start with **Hide** mode on **Moderate** sensitivity.

Hide folds likely slop into a one-line strip you can reopen with one click. For a seller that's useful, because the posts you're looking for are buried in a feed full of sales motivation and template giveaways. Moderate catches the comment-a-keyword posts and the grit stories without being so strict that prospect posts start folding.

A few points specific to selling:

- **Prospects write slop too.** A target account's VP might post a hype-heavy announcement about a new initiative. That's still a buying signal. When you see a strip from someone on your account list, open it.
- **Use the vote.** If Slop Mop folds a prospect's post you needed, vote No. Your vote overrides the model in your own feed. Voting isn't available for email.
- **Pick your plan by your day.** The free plan covers the one feed you pick (usually LinkedIn) with 250 checks a day, resetting at midnight UTC. Sellers who scroll all day for signals can run through that. Slop Mop XL, at US$5.99 a month or US$59.99 a year, covers every feed, the sites you read, Gmail and Outlook, with a higher daily limit, on up to five browsers.

Each person on a team installs it in their own browser, and each person's votes apply only to their own feed.

## If you post too

Sales posts and emails are read by buyers, and buyers can spot a pitch dressed as a lesson.

- Share what you learned from real deals, with the cost or the limit included. The [no-catch promises](/patterns/no-catch-promises/) page explains why "works for everyone" reads as a pitch.
- Drop the comment-for-the-template ask. If the template is useful, post it. See [engagement bait](/patterns/engagement-bait/).
- Skip the drumroll opener ("I lost a deal. Then everything changed."). The [scroll-stopper](/patterns/scroll-stopper/) page shows why.
- If you use AI to draft outreach or posts, read the result before sending. Forwarding AI output unread has earned a nickname since August 2026, reported by [Allwork.Space](https://allwork.space/2026/08/meat-proxy-is-the-new-insult-for-coworkers-who-let-ai-do-the-thinking/): [meat proxy](/glossary/meat-proxy/).
