## What a freelancer's feed looks like

For a freelance writer, designer, developer, editor or translator, every network is partly a shop window and partly a lead source. You post your work so past clients remember you, and you scroll looking for the posts that turn into paid work. On LinkedIn that's "Does anyone know a good copywriter for a B2B launch?" or a former client moving to a new company. On X it's an editor asking for pitches or a founder who needs a designer this week. On Reddit it's the hiring subreddits and the trade communities where agencies ask for freelance capacity. In Facebook groups it's local businesses asking for recommendations. And in your inbox it's the enquiry that arrives between the newsletters.

Those posts are short and plain, and they sit in feeds full of people selling freelancing itself. You know the type: "How I went from $0 to $20k months in 90 days", "Stop trading time for money", "Comment CLIENTS and I'll DM you the cold outreach script that books 10 calls a week". There are endless posts telling you to raise your rates, written by people whose income comes from telling you to raise your rates. Plus the AI tools that will "replace your entire workflow", and the reflective stories about the client from hell, each ending in a lesson about boundaries.

What you want is leads, honest notes from peers about rates and contracts, and news from the companies you'd like to work for.

## Examples

:::slop
90 days ago I had zero clients.

Today I'm fully booked at $250/hour.

The secret? I stopped selling and started serving.

Comment "SCRIPT" and I'll send you my exact outreach template. 👇
:::
:::fine
Looking for a freelance illustrator for a children's science book, 24 spot illustrations, black and white. Budget is £3,000 to £3,500. Deadline is end of February. Portfolio links in a DM are fine.
:::

:::slop
A client once refused to pay my invoice. I was devastated. But that moment taught me the most important lesson of my career: know your worth. 💜
:::
:::fine
I've switched to a 30% deposit on any project over £2,000. I've lost two enquiries since June because of it. I also haven't chased a late invoice in four months.
:::

The slop examples are a [scroll-stopper](/patterns/scroll-stopper/) with "not X, but Y" framing and a comment-for-script giveaway, and a [too-tidy story](/patterns/too-tidy-story/) whose only job is the moral. The fine ones are a real lead with a budget and a deadline, and a pricing change with its cost stated.

## How Slop Mop helps freelancers

Slop Mop is a browser extension for Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari and Chromium browsers such as Brave and Vivaldi, on desktop. It reads each post before you get to it in your LinkedIn feed, X timeline, Reddit (posts and comments), Facebook feed and groups, Substack and Medium, and in the articles you open on other sites. It scores the writing on nine tells of slop and three counter-signals, and either folds likely slop into a one-line strip or marks it, depending on your settings. Click the mop on any post to see the reasons; [How it works](/how-it-works.html) explains the scoring.

It judges what a post says and how. It doesn't guess whether AI wrote it. A hurried lead post from a client is judged on what it says, and a polished income boast written entirely by hand can still be flagged. It never deletes, reports, mutes or blocks anyone, so a client whose post gets folded never knows.

In Gmail and Outlook, Slop Mop checks what the sender of an email wrote, with your personal details replaced by placeholders first. Attachments and quoted earlier messages are never sent, and transactional mail such as invoices and payment receipts is never read unless you check one by hand.

## Setting up Slop Mop for freelancers

Decide where your leads come from first. The free plan covers one feed you pick, with 250 checks a day, so choose the network that has sent you the most paid work. Slop Mop XL covers every feed, the sites you read, and Gmail and Outlook, with a higher daily limit, on up to five browsers, for US$5.99 a month or US$59.99 a year. If your leads are spread across LinkedIn, Reddit and your inbox, XL is the plan that covers all three.

Start in **Highlight** mode at **Moderate** for your first week, then switch to **Hide** at **Moderate**.

A missed lead costs you money, so it's worth seeing how the extension treats the posts you care about before you let it fold anything. In Highlight mode nothing is hidden; posts get a yellow or red mop. Watch what happens to "looking for" posts and to updates from past clients. If they come through unmarked, as you'd expect for plain, specific posts, you can switch to Hide with some confidence.

Hide makes the scroll much quicker. Folded posts sit in a one-line strip, and you can reopen any of them with one click. If you see a client's name on a strip, open it.

If a lead or a client's post gets marked or folded, vote **No**. Your vote overrides the score in your feed, and because it disagrees with the verdict, a person at Artimovo will review that post's text to see why it missed. Vote **Probably** on any income boast that gets through. Voting isn't available for email.

Aggressive will fold more of the "freelance coach" posts, but it also catches more borderline posts from real people. For a lead source, Moderate is the safer balance. On LinkedIn, the platform's own guide to [customising what you see in your feed](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/62570) is worth ten minutes too.

## If you post too

Freelancers post to stay in clients' minds, and the format that seems to perform is the one readers have learned to skip. The tells to keep out:

- **Income and booking claims** with no detail behind them. Show the work and say who it was for, if you're allowed. See [no-catch promises](/patterns/no-catch-promises/).
- **"Comment X and I'll DM you"** giveaways. If you have a template worth sharing, link it. See [engagement bait](/patterns/engagement-bait/).
- **Client-from-hell stories with a lesson.** Readers who might hire you wonder what you'd say about them. See [too-tidy story](/patterns/too-tidy-story/).

A short post with the project, the brief, one image and what you'd do differently next time does more for a freelancer than a motivational thread, because it shows a prospective client exactly what they'd be buying. [Add Slop Mop to your browser](/) and it folds posts like these for you.
