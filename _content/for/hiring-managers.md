## What a hiring manager's feed looks like

If you manage a team and hire into it, reading the feeds is a side job. You check LinkedIn between meetings, usually to see what peers in your field are doing, to keep an eye on people you might want to hire one day, and to look at the candidates a recruiter has just sent over. You might follow practitioners in your field on X, read a candidate's blog posts on Medium or Substack before an interview, and dip into Reddit communities where people in your profession talk shop. Your inbox gets the recruiter pitches, agency CVs and internal updates.

What fills the gaps is a particular kind of management content:

- Leadership aphorisms built on contrast: management is one thing, leadership is another.
- Posts announcing that "the best hires" all share one trait, usually something you can't test for.
- Applause posts for a team, a company or a conference, with lots of adjectives and no detail about what anyone did.
- Interview "hacks" that claim one question reveals everything about a candidate.
- Agency emails promising "top 1% talent", ready to start tomorrow.

What you want sits underneath: a peer explaining how they structured a take-home task, an engineer writing about the bug they spent a week on, someone you interviewed last year posting about what they shipped since. Those posts show how people think about the work, which is what you're judging when you hire.

## Examples

:::slop
Hiring isn't about skills. It's about character. Skills can be taught, but you can't teach hunger. The best people I've hired didn't have the best CVs. They had the biggest hearts.
:::
:::fine
We dropped the take-home test for our analyst roles and replaced it with a 45-minute paired session on a real (anonymised) dataset. Fewer candidates dropped out, and the session showed us how people asked questions, which the take-home never did.
:::

:::slop
So incredibly proud of this amazing team. Truly world-class people doing truly world-class work. Grateful every single day. 🙏
:::
:::fine
Proud of the support team this quarter. They rewrote 40 help articles after the pricing change, and tickets about invoices fell back to their old level within three weeks.
:::

The first slop example leans on ["not X, but Y" framing](/patterns/not-x-but-y/), defining good hiring by what it supposedly isn't. The second is [empty praise](/patterns/empty-praise/). Notice that the fine version of the second is still a proud-of-my-team post. Pride is fine. The detail is what makes it worth reading.

## How Slop Mop works as a feed filter for hiring managers

Slop Mop is a browser extension for Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari and Chromium browsers such as Brave and Arc, on desktop. It reads posts in your LinkedIn feed and X timeline, Reddit posts and comments, Facebook, Substack and Medium, and the articles and blog posts you open on other sites. As you scroll, it scores each one against nine tells of slop and three counter-signals, including whether the post is useful to someone doing the job. [How it works](/how-it-works.html) explains the scoring.

When you click the mop on a post, you see the score and the strongest signs, plus a chart of all nine tells called the Slopprint. If you're looking at a candidate's recent posts or blog, it won't tell you whether to hire them, and it says nothing about whether they used AI. It shows which writing habits a post leans on, which you can weigh however you like.

It doesn't delete, report, mute or block anything, and it never sends your name or the author's name to the scoring service.

## Setting up the feed filter for hiring managers

Choose a plan. The free plan covers one feed you pick, with 250 checks a day, which is usually plenty for a between-meetings scroll of LinkedIn. Slop Mop XL covers every feed, the sites you read, and Gmail and Outlook, with a higher daily limit, on up to five browsers, for US$5.99 a month or US$59.99 a year. In email it checks what the sender wrote, such as an agency's pitch, with your personal details replaced by placeholders first; attachments, including CVs, are never sent.

Start with **Hide** mode on **Moderate** sensitivity.

You have limited time in your feeds and you're mostly there for peers. Hide folds likely slop into a one-line strip ("This post was hidden") that you can reopen with one click, so the feed you do see is shorter and denser. Moderate catches the obvious leadership aphorisms and applause posts without folding plain posts that happen to have one enthusiastic line, since a single tell on its own has its score cut.

Two adjustments worth knowing:

- **Looking at a specific candidate.** Folded posts are never lost. Open the strip, then click the mop to see why it was folded. A candidate's post that leans on a hook may still contain a real project. On LinkedIn you can also [follow people without connecting](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/72150), so a promising candidate's posts reach your feed.
- **If too much is folded.** Drop to Mild. If peers' posts keep getting through with obvious bait, try Aggressive for a few days and see whether you miss anything.

Vote on the misses. If Slop Mop folds a post you found useful, vote No. Your vote overrides the model in your feed, and posts where your vote disagrees with the verdict are reviewed by a person at Artimovo. Voting isn't available for email.

## If you post too

Hiring managers post job ads, team announcements and the occasional reflection on management. Readers include people who might apply, so your posts are a preview of what working for you is like.

- Write the job ad you'd want to read: what the team does, what the first three months look like, the salary band. See [hype words](/patterns/hype-words/) for the vocabulary to drop.
- When you thank your team, name what they did. The [empty praise](/patterns/empty-praise/) page has more on this.
- Avoid framing your view of management as a correction of everyone else's. One concrete thing you changed and what happened says more.
- If you share an interview lesson, base it on a pattern you saw across several interviews. The [too-tidy story](/patterns/too-tidy-story/) page explains why the single-anecdote version reads as invented.

The internal version of this problem, reports and updates that look finished but say little, has its own name: [workslop](/glossary/workslop/). [Add Slop Mop to your browser](/) and it folds posts like these for you.
