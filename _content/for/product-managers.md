## What your feed looks like

Product management has a large and loud online community, and a lot of it is people selling courses, templates and coaching to other product managers. It shows up wherever PMs read. LinkedIn has the "PMs who don't do X will be replaced by AI" warning, the carousel of 12 prioritisation frameworks and the "great PMs vs good PMs" table. X has the thread that turns a well-known essay into 14 numbered tweets. Reddit's product subreddits have the "how do I break into PM" question answered by someone selling a course. Substack and Medium have newsletters and listicles that restate the same frameworks with new cover art, and your inbox gets the lot as a weekly digest.

Underneath sit the post asking you to comment "PRD" to get a template and the confident list of what a FAANG interviewer really wants. None of it is wrong, exactly. Most of it is just the same ten ideas, and reading it doesn't change what you'll do on Monday.

What you want are posts that show the work: a launch with its adoption numbers, a post-mortem on a feature that got pulled, a PM explaining how they actually ran discovery with eight customers, a designer's thread on the onboarding change that moved activation. Those posts cost the author something to write, and they're often less polished.

## Examples

:::slop
Good PMs write specs.
Great PMs write stories.

Good PMs ship features.
Great PMs ship outcomes.

It's not about the roadmap. It's about the problem. ♻️ Repost for your product team.
:::
:::fine
We killed our in-app chat in August. Weekly users were 3% of accounts, and half the messages were people asking how to cancel. Support tickets went up 12% for a month after we removed it, then settled below where they'd been.
:::

:::slop
I've reviewed 500+ PRDs. Most are bad. Comment "SPEC" and I'll send you the exact template top PMs use to ship 10x faster. 👇
:::
:::fine
Our PRDs used to be 9 pages. We cut them to one page plus a list of open questions. Engineers say they read them now. The cost: design reviews take longer because the detail moved there.
:::

The slop examples run on parallel "good vs great" lines, ["not X, but Y"](/patterns/not-x-but-y/) framing, a repost request and a comment-for-template giveaway. The fine examples give a date, a metric, a trade-off and what happened next.

## How Slop Mop helps product managers

Slop Mop is a browser extension for Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari and Chromium browsers such as Arc, Brave and Vivaldi, on desktop. As you scroll, it scores each post ahead of you against nine tells of slop and three counter-signals: sounds like a person, useful to readers, and reader response. The panel behind the mop shows the score, the strongest signs and a Slopprint chart of all nine tells. [How it works](/how-it-works.html) goes through each part.

It covers the places PMs read: posts on LinkedIn and X, posts and comments on Reddit, Substack Notes and posts, Medium stories, the articles you open on other sites, and, with Slop Mop for Gmail and Outlook, what the sender wrote in your newsletters. Medium lets you [refine your recommendations](https://help.medium.com/hc/en-us/articles/224488047-Refine-recommendations) by showing less of a topic or writer; Slop Mop works one story at a time, so a good writer's weaker listicle gets marked without losing the writer.

Usefulness matters a lot in the score. A post with specific numbers, named examples or steps a reader could follow gets a large share taken off its slop score, though never all of it. That's why a launch post with real metrics tends to survive even if the author is excited about it.

It scores the writing and ignores how it was produced, so it isn't an AI detector. It never deletes, reports, mutes or blocks anything.

## Setting up Slop Mop for product managers

Start with **Hide** mode at **Aggressive** sensitivity.

PM feeds have more formula posts than most, and the useful posts usually carry the specific details that the usefulness counter-signal rewards. Aggressive folds more of the borderline framework content, and because usefulness takes a large share off a post's score, the case studies and post-mortems have a cushion. Folded posts sit in a one-line strip and open with one click, so you can spot-check what it's catching.

Give it a week, and open a few folded strips each day. If you keep finding posts you wanted, drop to **Moderate**. If the strips are reliably formula posts, leave it.

Treat your votes like feedback on a beta. A **No** on a wrongly folded post overrides the score in your feed; a **Probably** on one that slipped through does the same in the other direction. When your vote disagrees with the verdict, the post's text goes to a person at Artimovo for review, and the misses are used to tune the tells. Voting isn't available for email.

If you rely on a feed for competitor launches, keep in mind that some company announcements are written in exactly the style Aggressive folds. Follow those accounts' pages directly, or use **Highlight** so nothing is hidden.

On plans: the free one covers the one feed you pick, with 250 checks a day, so pick the network where you lose the most time, usually LinkedIn. Slop Mop XL, at US$5.99 a month or US$59.99 a year, covers every feed, the sites you read, Gmail and Outlook, with a higher daily limit, on up to five browsers. It's worth it if your reading is split between LinkedIn, Substack and a pile of newsletters.

## If you post too

Product people write well about their own work and badly about "product" in the abstract. A few tells to watch:

- **Good-vs-great parallel lines** and "it's not about features, it's about outcomes". State the outcome. See ["not X, but Y"](/patterns/not-x-but-y/).
- **"Comment TEMPLATE"** giveaways and repost requests. If the template is good, link it. See [engagement bait](/patterns/engagement-bait/).
- **The one-line-paragraph drumroll** before a modest insight. See [scroll-stopper](/patterns/scroll-stopper/).
- **Internal docs padded by AI**: a spec or status update that looks finished and says little has a name, [workslop](/glossary/workslop/).

A launch or post-mortem with one metric, one trade-off and one thing you'd do differently is worth more to the readers you want than another framework. [Add Slop Mop to your browser](/) and it folds the framework posts so the case studies come first.
