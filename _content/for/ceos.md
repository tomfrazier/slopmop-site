## What a CEO's feed looks like

A chief executive reads in more places than people assume, and most of them are crowded. LinkedIn connects you to your own staff, customers, board members, other chief executives and a large number of people who want to sell you something. X is where analysts, journalists and the loudest people in your industry argue. Industry newsletters on Substack and articles on trade sites fill the gaps. And the inbox takes the rest: vendor pitches, internal updates and reports, all landing in the same column.

What tends to pile up:

- Vendor posts that tag you or your company in a "conversation" about a trend, setting up a sales message, and the cold email that follows a day later.
- Other executives' posts in the polished, agency-written voice: values, purpose, the future of work, with nothing a reader could disagree with.
- "Honoured and humbled" award posts, keynote announcements and panel recaps.
- Trend pieces about AI on X and in newsletters, written to make leaders nervous about falling behind.
- Internal updates that look finished and say little, which now has a name: [workslop](/glossary/workslop/).

What you need to see is different. A support lead posting about a tough week with a customer. An engineer explaining what the team shipped. A customer writing, unprompted, about your product, good or bad. A peer being candid about a mistake. Those posts tell you how the company actually feels from the inside and the outside, and they're easy to miss under the polish.

## Examples

:::slop
As leaders, we must embrace change with courage and humility. In today's rapidly evolving environment, purpose-driven organisations will be the ones that thrive. Furthermore, it is our responsibility to lead with empathy.
:::
:::fine
We moved our annual planning from December to October this year so teams weren't writing plans over the holidays. It made the plans shorter. Two teams said they'd prefer the old timing because their customers' budgets aren't set until November, so we'll split it next year.
:::

:::slop
Our people are the beating heart of everything we do, a living tapestry of passion, talent and dreams, woven together by a shared vision.
:::
:::fine
Our warehouse team in Doncaster went from two shifts to three in August. Hiring took longer than planned, and for three weeks managers covered the night shift themselves.
:::

The first slop example is [stiff phrasing](/patterns/stiff-phrasing/): stock transitions and safe statements that could come from any executive. The second is [flowery prose](/patterns/flowery-prose/), where an image replaces a claim. The fine versions say what changed, who it affected and what didn't work.

## How Slop Mop for CEOs works

Slop Mop is a browser extension for Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari and Chromium browsers such as Brave and Arc, on desktop. It reads posts in your LinkedIn feed and X timeline, Substack Notes and posts, Reddit, Facebook and Medium, and the articles you open on other sites. Slop Mop for Gmail and Slop Mop for Outlook check email: before a message is checked, personal details are replaced with placeholders in your mailbox, and only what the sender wrote is sent, never attachments or quoted earlier messages. Private, legal, health and transactional mail is never read unless you check one by hand.

As you scroll, it scores each post against nine tells of slop and three counter-signals, including "sounds like a person", which rewards specific personal detail and an uneven, recognisable voice. That counter-signal tends to favour staff posting in their own words over executive posts written by committee. [How it works](/how-it-works.html) explains the scoring.

It judges the writing, not the author, and it isn't an AI detector. It never sends your name or the author's to its scoring service, and it never deletes, reports, mutes or blocks anything. Folding happens only in your own browser.

## Setting up Slop Mop for CEOs

Pick the plan that matches where you read. The free plan covers one feed you choose, with 250 checks a day; for most chief executives that's LinkedIn. Slop Mop XL adds every other feed, the sites you read, Gmail and Outlook, and a higher daily limit, on up to five browsers, for US$5.99 a month or US$59.99 a year. If vendor email is the bigger drain, XL is the one that reaches it.

Start with **Hide** mode on **Mild** sensitivity.

Mild folds only the clearest slop: the posts with several strong tells at once, such as a vendor's tag-bait or a stack of platitudes. That's deliberate. Your own employees post about the company, sometimes clumsily, and those posts are worth seeing even when they're a bit over-excited. A gentle setting keeps them in your feed.

Folded posts become a one-line strip ("This post was hidden") that one click reopens, so nothing is gone for good.

After a week or two:

- If vendor and peer platitudes are still getting through, try **Moderate**.
- If you want to see how your own company's posts read, switch to **Highlight** for a day. Nothing is hidden, and each post's mop turns yellow or red. It's a quick, private read on the tone of your staff's and comms team's posts.

Vote when it gets one wrong. Click the mop and vote No on a post you wanted to see. Your vote overrides the model in your feed. Voting isn't available for email.

## If you post too

Many executive posts are drafted by someone else, sometimes with AI. That's normal, but readers can tell when a post went from a draft to publication without anyone adding a real detail.

- Add one thing only you would know: a meeting, a number, a customer's comment. That detail is what makes a post sound like yours.
- Cut the stock transitions ("Furthermore", "In today's environment"). The [stiff phrasing](/patterns/stiff-phrasing/) page lists the usual ones.
- Name the cost of a decision as well as the benefit. See [no-catch promises](/patterns/no-catch-promises/).
- Thank people for something specific. [Empty praise](/patterns/empty-praise/) from the top reads as a form letter.

Since August 2026, people who pass along AI output without reading it have had an unkind nickname, [meat proxy](/glossary/meat-proxy/), as [Allwork.Space reported](https://allwork.space/2026/08/meat-proxy-is-the-new-insult-for-coworkers-who-let-ai-do-the-thinking/). Reading your own post before it goes out is the simplest way to avoid earning it. [Add Slop Mop to your browser](/) and it folds posts like these for you.
