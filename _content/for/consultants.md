## What a consultant's feed looks like

A consultant's reading is mostly other consultants. On LinkedIn, rivals and solo advisers post for the same reason you do: to stay visible to people who buy advice. On Substack, many of the same people run newsletters, and their Notes restack each other's frameworks. On X there's the hot-take version, and the industry sites and trade publications you read for client news are full of sponsored "insight" pieces. Your inbox gets the gated reports and webinar invitations.

That produces a particular kind of slop. There are 2x2 grids with four words in them, "five things every board should ask about AI" with nothing a board couldn't have guessed, and transformation posts that name no client, no sector and no result.

Buried in that are the posts you actually scroll for. A client's operations director mentions they've paused a system migration. A prospect's new CFO describes the first thing they changed. A former colleague shares the real numbers from a pricing project in a Substack post. Those posts are often short and plain, and louder ones built to be shared push them down.

What you want from the feed is signal about specific organisations and specific problems. Generic advice about "change" is the thing you already sell, so reading other people's version of it costs you time and gives you nothing to act on.

## Examples

:::slop
Most transformations fail. Not because of strategy. Because of people.

The firms that win in 2027 will be the ones that put culture at the centre of every decision. Agree? 👇
:::
:::fine
We spent six weeks mapping how purchase orders move through a 900-person distributor. The approval step everyone blamed took a day. The real delay was a weekly batch job nobody owned, which added up to four days.
:::

:::slop
Introducing our proprietary RESET Framework™: Reimagine, Engage, Scale, Embed, Transform. A proven methodology to unlock value at every stage, with zero disruption to the business.
:::
:::fine
The framework we use for supplier consolidation has three steps and the second one usually fails. Suppliers you cut will raise prices on the categories you kept. Budget for that before you announce savings.
:::

The slop piles up tells: a dramatic opener, ["not X, but Y"](/patterns/not-x-but-y/) framing, an engagement ask, hype words and a promise with no cost. The fine posts name a size, a number and a downside you could use with a client.

## How Slop Mop works as a feed filter for consultants

Slop Mop is a browser extension for Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari and Chromium browsers such as Brave and Arc, on desktop. It reads each post before you reach it in your LinkedIn feed, Substack Notes and posts, your X timeline, Reddit, Facebook and Medium, and the articles you open on industry and news sites. It asks nine questions about the tells of slop and three about whether the post is useful, sounds like a person and has drawn real responses, then gives it a score. Click the mop on any post to see the reasons; [How it works](/how-it-works.html) explains the scoring.

It judges the writing and makes no guess about who wrote it, so it isn't an AI detector. A partner who used AI to tidy up a genuinely useful case write-up will usually pass. A hand-typed post that is all framework and no substance can be flagged.

It works alongside each platform's own controls. Substack, for example, lets you [control what you see in your feed](https://support.substack.com/hc/en-us/articles/54536393980692-How-do-I-control-what-I-see-on-my-feed-on-Substack); Slop Mop then marks what's left.

## Setting up the feed filter for consultants

Start with your plan. The free plan covers one feed you pick, with 250 checks a day; for most consultants that's LinkedIn, where clients post. Slop Mop XL, at US$5.99 a month or US$59.99 a year, covers every feed, the industry sites you read, and Gmail and Outlook, with a higher daily limit, on up to five browsers. In email it checks what the sender wrote, with your personal details replaced by placeholders first, and it never reads private, legal, health or transactional mail unless you check one by hand.

Then set **Highlight** mode at **Moderate** sensitivity.

Highlight hides nothing. Possibly slop gets a yellow mop and Likely slop gets a red one. That matters for consultants because a client or prospect might write a post full of tells, and you still want to see it. A sloppy post from the CEO of an account you're bidding for is still useful intelligence about what they care about. Hide mode would fold it into a strip, which you could miss.

With Highlight on, you can scan for unmarked posts first, then go back to the red ones only if the author matters to you. After a week, if the red posts are all from people you don't work with, switch to **Hide**: folded posts open again with one click, and nothing is deleted, muted or reported.

Use the vote when it gets someone wrong. If a client's dry, hedged update gets marked, vote **No**. Your vote overrides the score in your own feed. If you know a post is padding that slipped through, vote **Probably**. When you disagree with the verdict, the post's text goes to a person at Artimovo for review, which is how the tells get tuned. Voting isn't available for email.

Aggressive marks more borderline framework posts, but also more of the short, plain posts from clients, which are the ones you most want to see clearly.

## If you post too

Consultants post to be found, and the pressure to sound authoritative pushes writing towards the tells. A few habits to watch, on LinkedIn or in a newsletter:

- **Framework names and acronyms** read as packaging. Say what the steps are and which one fails most often. See [hype words](/patterns/hype-words/).
- **"It's not about strategy, it's about people"** is the most common consulting line on LinkedIn. State the finding directly. See ["not X, but Y"](/patterns/not-x-but-y/).
- **Benefits with no cost.** Clients know every change has a cost, so a post that names none reads as a pitch. See [no-catch promises](/patterns/no-catch-promises/).
- **Client stories with a neat moral.** If you can't name the sector, the size and what went wrong, the story will read as invented. See [too-tidy story](/patterns/too-tidy-story/).

Client confidentiality limits what you can share, but a sector, a headcount band and one honest number usually survive an NDA. Those details are what make a post worth reading. [Add Slop Mop to your browser](/) and it marks posts like these for you.
