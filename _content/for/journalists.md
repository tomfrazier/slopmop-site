## What your feed looks like

Reporters read more channels than almost anyone, and follow a lot of people they would never choose to read for pleasure. On X it's officials, analysts, rivals and the accounts that break news first. On Reddit it's the local and industry subreddits where people describe what happened to them before anyone has written it up. Substack carries newsletters from people on your beat, and Notes, where they argue about them. LinkedIn is company spokespeople, executives and agency staff. Then there's the web, where the same wire story appears on forty sites with different headlines, and the inbox, where press releases arrive by the hundred.

Much of it is announcement posts. A product launch is "thrilled and humbled". A redundancy round becomes "an exciting new chapter". A conference panel turns into three paragraphs about how inspiring everyone was, with no record of anything anyone said. The press release in your inbox says the same in more words.

On top of that sits the viral layer: the first-person story with a twist ending, the "I asked 100 hiring managers" post with no method, the X thread that summarises a report it hasn't read, the Reddit post about a rude client that's been reposted every six months for years. Many read as constructed, and checking them takes time you would rather spend elsewhere.

What you want is the opposite: a post where someone at a company says something specific and on the record, an engineer explains what broke, a former employee describes a policy with a date attached. Those posts tend to be plain and short, and they get fewer reactions than the polished ones.

## Examples

:::slop
Today I'm incredibly proud to share that we're entering an exciting new chapter. This is a truly transformative moment for our people and our customers. More to come! 🚀
:::
:::fine
From 1 November our Leeds warehouse moves to a four-day week for packing staff. Pay stays the same. We tried it on one shift since June and error rates didn't go up.
:::

:::slop
A cleaner at our office once asked me why I looked tired.

I told her the truth.

She said something I'll never forget.

That day I learned what leadership really means. ♻️ Repost if this moved you.
:::
:::fine
Our support team logged 212 complaints about the new billing page in its first week. Most were about one button. We moved it on Thursday and the count dropped to 30 the following week.
:::

The first slop example is announcement padding: [empty praise](/patterns/empty-praise/) in place of what actually changed. The second is a [too-tidy story](/patterns/too-tidy-story/) built around a moral and finished with a repost request. The fine posts give you dates, places and numbers you could phone someone to confirm.

## How Slop Mop helps journalists

Slop Mop is a browser extension for Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari and Chromium browsers such as Brave, Arc and Vivaldi, on desktop. It reads what you scroll before you reach it, scores each item against nine tells of slop and three counter-signals, and shows its reasons when you click the mop. [How it works](/how-it-works.html) explains the panel.

Across a reporter's channels:

- **X.** It reads the posts in your timeline and marks the threads and quote-posts that are all hook and no source. Beat [lists on X](https://help.x.com/en/using-x/x-lists) still do the first job of choosing whose posts you see; Slop Mop judges each post those accounts write.
- **Reddit.** It reads posts and comments, which helps when the eyewitness comment is buried under jokes and recycled stories.
- **Substack.** It reads Notes and posts.
- **LinkedIn.** It reads the posts in your feed, where most corporate announcements land first.
- **Websites.** It reads the articles you open, which is a quick way to see which version of a story is the rewrite padded for search.
- **Email.** Slop Mop for Gmail and Slop Mop for Outlook check what the sender wrote, so a press release that's all adjectives gets marked. Before a message is checked, personal details are replaced with placeholders in your mailbox, and attachments and quoted earlier messages are never sent. Private, legal, health and transactional mail is never read unless you check one by hand, which matters if sources email you.

It judges the writing. It is not an AI detector, and you shouldn't treat a score as evidence that a person or a machine wrote something. A high score means the item has more of the tells and reads as low-value. For a reporter it's a reading aid. It can't check facts, and it says nothing about whether a claim is true. It never deletes, reports or blocks anything.

## Setting up Slop Mop for journalists

Start with **Highlight** mode at **Aggressive** sensitivity.

That combination sounds odd, so here is the reasoning. Highlight never hides anything, so a spokesperson's padded post about a restructure stays in view. That post may be the story, and you need to read it whatever its score. Aggressive means more posts get marked, so the yellow and red mops act as a quick colour code. Unmarked posts are the ones most likely to contain something concrete, and you can read those first.

When the panel opens on a red post, the Slopprint shows which tells drove the score. A spike in too-tidy story and engagement bait on a viral anecdote is a useful prompt to be careful before you quote it, though the post could still be true.

Vote when the mark is wrong. A source's terse post that gets flagged can be voted **No**, and your vote overrides the score in your feed. Votes that disagree with the verdict are reviewed by a person at Artimovo. Voting isn't available for email.

If you use a network mainly for reading and not for monitoring, switch to **Hide** at **Moderate** there. Folded posts sit in a one-line strip and open again with one click.

On plans:

- **Free** covers the one feed you pick, with 250 checks a day, resetting at midnight UTC. Pick the network you monitor hardest; for many reporters that's X.
- **Slop Mop XL** is US$5.99 a month or US$59.99 a year. It covers every feed, the sites you read, and Gmail and Outlook, with a higher daily limit, on up to five browsers. For a reporter working six channels and a press inbox, that's the plan that matches the job.

## If you post too

Reporters promoting their own work fall into a few habits, on every network:

- **"Thrilled to share my latest piece"** adds nothing. Lead with the finding itself. See [empty praise](/patterns/empty-praise/).
- **The cliffhanger opener** ("What we found shocked us") borrows from headline writing and reads as a hook in a feed. See [scroll-stopper](/patterns/scroll-stopper/).
- **"Please share widely"** is a mild form of the ask the model looks for. A strong story travels without it. See [engagement bait](/patterns/engagement-bait/).

The best post about a story is usually two or three sentences with the most surprising fact in the first one, plus the link. [Add Slop Mop to your browser](/) and it marks the padding so the posts with facts in them are easier to find.
