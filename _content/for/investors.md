## What your feed looks like

Investors use LinkedIn as a loose early-warning system: which founders are hiring, which portfolio companies are quiet, who just left a company you're watching, what operators are saying about a market before it shows up in a report. You also post, or your firm does, and a lot of founders write with you in mind.

That last part shapes the feed. Much of what you see is written to be noticed by investors:

- Traction posts with large, unexplained numbers: "10x growth", "massive demand", "the fastest-growing company in the category".
- Market-map and "state of AI" posts that rename a trend every quarter and promise that a category will be worth trillions.
- Investor content about investing: "what I look for in founders", built on a few contrasts and a borrowed quote.
- Congratulations threads under funding announcements, each one a variation on "huge news, well deserved".

What you want is the opposite of a pitch: an operator's honest numbers, a founder explaining why churn went up, an engineer's account of what a new model actually changed in production, a customer saying why they switched. Specific posts are easier to check, and checking is your job.

## Examples

:::slop
Insane week. 🚀 Our AI-native platform is seeing explosive, unprecedented demand. Pipeline is through the roof. Category-defining moment. More soon. 👀
:::
:::fine
Monthly revenue reached £140k in September, up from £95k in June. About a third of that is from two customers on three-month pilots, so we're reporting it separately until they convert.
:::

:::slop
Massive congratulations to this incredible team on this incredible milestone. Truly visionary founders building the future. Couldn't be more excited!
:::
:::fine
We led this round because their median customer went from one team to four within a year, without a sales hire. The open question is whether that holds outside UK mid-market companies.
:::

The first pair contrasts [hype words](/patterns/hype-words/) with a number and its caveat. The second contrasts [empty praise](/patterns/empty-praise/) with an investment thesis someone could disagree with. In 2025 one investor quoted by [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2025/09/28/how-is-arr-calculated-startups-venture/) described pilots and one-off deals counted as recurring revenue as [vibe revenue](/glossary/vibe-revenue/). The first fine example shows the plain alternative: report the pilots separately.

## How Slop Mop helps

Slop Mop is a browser extension for Chrome and other Chromium browsers, live on LinkedIn today. X, Reddit, Facebook, Substack, Medium, websites and email are coming soon. If a lot of your reading happens in newsletters or on X, Slop Mop doesn't cover that yet.

It reads each post before you reach it and scores the writing against nine tells of slop and three counter-signals. Click the mop on any post and you see the score, the strongest signs and the Slopprint, a chart of all nine tells. For a founder's traction post, that's a quick way to see whether it leans mostly on hype words, on a no-catch promise, or on a scroll-stopping opener.

Be clear on what it measures. A post's writing quality says nothing reliable about the company behind it. Plenty of strong teams post badly, and Slop Mop isn't an AI detector or a due-diligence tool. It never deletes, reports, mutes or blocks anything.

## Setting it up for investors

Start with **Highlight** mode on **Aggressive** sensitivity.

Highlight hides nothing, which matters when a single post from a portfolio company or a target might be the one you needed to see. Each post's mop turns yellow for Possibly slop or red for Likely slop. Because nothing is folded, an Aggressive setting costs you nothing in coverage. It just marks more posts, which makes the unmarked ones (usually the specific, numbers-first posts) easier to find in a fast scroll.

Ways to adjust:

- **Too much yellow.** If most of your feed is marked and the colours stop helping, step down to Moderate.
- **You never open the red posts.** Switch to Hide mode. Folded posts become a one-line strip, and one click reopens any of them.
- **A portfolio founder keeps getting flagged.** Click the mop to see why. If it's wrong, vote No. Your vote overrides the score in your feed, and when it disagrees with the verdict, a person at Artimovo reviews it.

## If you post too

Investor posts are read closely by founders, so they set the tone for how founders pitch you.

- Explain the thesis behind a deal in a sentence a founder could argue with. That's more useful to them than adjectives. See [empty praise](/patterns/empty-praise/).
- Skip the market-sizing superlatives and the predictions with no date or number attached. The [hype words](/patterns/hype-words/) page lists the usual vocabulary.
- If you post "what I look for", give a real example from a past decision, with the outcome, including the misses. The [too-tidy story](/patterns/too-tidy-story/) page explains why the perfect anecdote reads as made up.
