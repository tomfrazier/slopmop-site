## What your feed looks like

If you write, film or draw for an audience on LinkedIn, your feed is shaped by the other people doing the same. The algorithm shows you creators because you engage with creators, and a lot of creator content is about creating content.

The familiar shapes:

- "How I grew from 0 to 50,000 followers in 6 months", with the method behind a comment keyword.
- Hook templates and "swipe files" of opening lines, often posted with those same opening lines.
- "I quit my job to create full-time" stories with a perfect turning point and no mention of income.
- Posts with hundreds of reactions from the same small circle and comments that say only "Great post!" or "So true 🔥".

What you're after is the conversation about the work itself: a writer explaining why a post they loved flopped, a video maker showing how they script, someone posting their real numbers from a newsletter launch, a reader telling a creator what they found useful. That material is harder to find when every second post is about growth.

## Examples

:::slop
Stop scrolling. ✋

This one hook formula got me 2.3M impressions.

Most creators will ignore this.

Don't be most creators.

Comment "HOOK" and I'll send you all 47. 👇
:::
:::fine
My best-performing post this year was a plain list of the five books I reread. My worst was a post I spent a week on about pricing a workshop. I think the pricing one failed because I buried the price in paragraph six. I've rewritten it with the number first and I'll post it again in November.
:::

:::slop
A year ago I sat in my car outside the office and decided to quit. Today I run a six-figure creator business. The lesson? Bet on yourself. Always.
:::
:::fine
Eighteen months in, about 60% of my income comes from two corporate workshops a quarter, and the newsletter covers its own costs and a bit more. I'm still not sure I could live on the audience alone.
:::

The first slop example is a full [scroll-stopper](/patterns/scroll-stopper/) with [engagement bait](/patterns/engagement-bait/) at the end. The second is a [too-tidy story](/patterns/too-tidy-story/) where the anecdote exists to prove a slogan. The fine versions share the messy result and the open question, which is what another creator can learn from.

## How Slop Mop helps

Slop Mop is a browser extension for Chrome and other Chromium browsers, live on LinkedIn today. X, Reddit, Facebook, Substack and Medium are coming soon, along with websites and email. If you also publish on Substack or X, it can't read those yet.

It scores each post against nine tells of slop and three counter-signals. The "reader response" counter-signal counts comments and reposts for more than one-tap reactions, and it gives less credit to posts with lots of reactions and few comments or reposts, which is what an engagement pod tends to produce.

It judges the writing, not who wrote it or whether AI helped. It never deletes, reports, mutes or blocks anything, and it never sends your name or the author's to the scoring service.

## Setting it up for creators

Start with **Hide** mode on **Moderate** sensitivity.

Creators read their feed partly for ideas and partly to keep up with peers, and the growth-hack genre is heavy enough to drown both. Hide folds likely slop into a one-line strip ("This post was hidden") that you can reopen with one click. Moderate folds the hook templates and comment-keyword posts while leaving plain posts alone, even ones with a single enthusiastic opener, because one tell on its own has its score cut.

Two habits help:

- **Open the strips from creators you follow closely.** Sometimes a good post has a bad first line. Click the mop to see whether it was folded for one thing or several.
- **Vote on what you'd defend.** If a peer's post was useful and Slop Mop folded it, vote No. Your vote overrides the model in your own feed.

If you want to study what's spreading in your niche, switch to **Highlight** for a while. Nothing is hidden, and each post's mop turns yellow or red.

The free plan covers one feed with 250 checks a day.

## If you post too

You do, so this part matters most. Your own posts get a mop in your feed. You can't vote on them, and the reader-response bar is left out, but the panel shows the Slopprint, so you can see which tells a post leans on.

- Don't open with a one-line drumroll. Start with the most interesting fact in the post. See [scroll-stopper](/patterns/scroll-stopper/).
- If you have something to give away, link it. Comment-for-access asks are [engagement bait](/patterns/engagement-bait/), and some readers now fold them away.
- Share the messy version of your story, with the numbers and the parts you haven't figured out. The [too-tidy story](/patterns/too-tidy-story/) page explains why the neat arc reads as invented.
- Write like yourself. The "sounds like a person" counter-signal rewards specific detail, asides and uneven rhythm. The [stiff phrasing](/patterns/stiff-phrasing/) page shows what flattens a voice.

Readers who feel the feed has filled with posts nobody really wrote sometimes reach for the [dead internet theory](/glossary/dead-internet-theory/). Posts with your own detail in them are the counter-example.
