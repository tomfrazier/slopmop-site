Stiff phrasing is writing assembled from stock parts. Sentences open with "Furthermore", "Moreover" or "It's worth noting that", claims are wrapped in "may potentially help to support", and the post ends with "Ultimately" or "In conclusion". Each part is grammatical. Together they read like a form filled in by someone who wasn't there.

## What stiff phrasing looks like

:::slop
Furthermore, it is worth noting that this approach may potentially offer a range of additional benefits. Ultimately, it can help to support long-term outcomes.
:::
:::fine
It also cut our error rate. We'll know in six months whether that lasts.
:::

:::slop
In today's fast-paced business environment, it is increasingly important for organisations to consider the various implications of remote working. Moreover, a number of key factors should be taken into account.
:::
:::fine
Remote work saved our team about 5 hours a week in commuting. It made onboarding harder: our two newest hires took twice as long to get up to speed.
:::

:::slop
In conclusion, it is clear that effective communication plays a crucial role in fostering successful collaboration across teams.
:::
:::fine
The weekly 15-minute call between design and engineering stopped most of the "I didn't know you'd changed that" problems.
:::

Common parts:

- **Stock transitions:** furthermore, moreover, additionally, in addition, consequently, that said.
- **Throat-clearing:** it's worth noting that, it's important to remember, needless to say.
- **Stacked hedges:** may potentially, could possibly, can help to support, in some cases.
- **Scene-setting openers:** in today's fast-paced world, in the current climate, in an era of.
- **Summary closers:** in conclusion, ultimately, all in all, at the end of the day.
- **Vague quantities:** a number of, various, a range of, key factors.

## Why it wastes your time

Stock phrases add words without adding meaning. "It's worth noting that the deadline moved" says the same as "The deadline moved", in twice the space. Across a post, the padding can make up a third of what you read.

Hedges are a subtler cost. "May potentially help to support" commits to almost nothing, so you can't tell whether the writer saw an effect, expects one or is guessing. Honest uncertainty sounds different: it says what isn't known and why.

The stiffness also hides the writer. Orwell, in ["Politics and the English Language"](https://www.orwellfoundation.com/the-orwell-foundation/orwell/essays-and-other-works/politics-and-the-english-language/), described prose that "consists less and less of words chosen for the sake of their meaning, and more and more of phrases tacked together like the sections of a prefabricated hen-house". That was in 1946. The hen-house parts have changed a little; the effect on the reader is the same.

## When it's fine

Formal phrasing has proper uses, and some readers depend on it.

- **Legal, regulatory and academic writing,** where conventions matter and precise hedges ("in most cases", "where applicable") carry real meaning.
- **Hedges that report real uncertainty.** "The trial had 40 patients, so the result may not hold in a larger group" is a hedge with a reason. That's good practice.
- **Formal letters and official notices,** where readers expect the register.
- **Writers working in a second language.** Many people were taught "Furthermore" and "In conclusion" as the correct way to structure an argument. Their posts can sound stiff while being full of real experience, and a stiff opener says nothing about whether the content is worth reading.
- **Long documents,** where a signpost such as "the next section covers costs" genuinely helps a reader find their place.

The test: delete the phrase. If the sentence loses information, keep it. If it only loses formality, leave it out.

## How to fix it in your own writing

1. **Strike every opener that only announces the sentence.** Furthermore, moreover, it's worth noting, in today's world.
2. **Count the hedges in each claim.** Keep at most one, and only if you can say why you're unsure.
3. **Swap vague quantities for numbers.** "A number of customers" becomes "14 customers" or "about a fifth of customers".
4. **Use the verb that describes the action.** "Plays a crucial role in fostering" usually means "helps" or "causes".
5. **Delete the closing summary.** If the post is short, readers remember what they just read.

:::slop
Moreover, it is important to note that implementing regular feedback sessions can potentially play a key role in supporting employee engagement. Ultimately, organisations should consider a range of approaches.
:::
:::fine
We started monthly 20-minute feedback chats in April. In the October staff survey, the share of people who said they knew how they were doing rose from 41% to 63%. We haven't yet seen any change in staff turnover.
:::
