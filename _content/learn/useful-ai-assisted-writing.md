Slop Mop folds slop whether a person or an AI wrote it, and it leaves useful posts alone the same way. That position surprises some people. It follows from a simple observation: what makes a post worth reading is what's in it, and an AI tool can help put that in or help leave it out.

## What makes AI-assisted writing useful

Useful AI-assisted writing shares the traits of any useful writing:

- **It starts from something the writer knows.** A project, a mistake, a number from their own work. The tool helps with structure or wording; the substance comes from the person.
- **It's specific.** Names, dates, amounts, places, steps. Details a reader could check or act on.
- **It has been read, cut and corrected.** The writer took responsibility for every sentence, including deleting the ones the tool added that weren't true or weren't needed.
- **It admits a cost.** Real advice has trade-offs. A post with none is usually a [no-catch promise](/patterns/no-catch-promises/).

Oren Etzioni and Daniel S. Weld proposed a name for the good end of this in their [GeekWire glossary of AI-era language](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/etzioni-on-ai-the-colorful-and-cranky-language-of-the-ai-era-from-slop-to-slap/): [AI cream](/glossary/ai-cream/), the rare AI-assisted writing that's genuinely worth reading.

## When AI-assisted writing turns into slop

The failure mode is just as recognisable. The writer starts with a topic instead of something to say, accepts the first draft, and posts it. The result reads smoothly and says nothing.

:::slop
In today's fast-paced world, leadership is more important than ever. Great leaders don't just manage; they inspire. Here are 5 game-changing habits that will elevate your leadership journey. 🚀
:::
:::fine
I asked a model to tidy up my notes from our September offsite, then cut half of what it gave me. What's left: we moved one-to-ones from weekly to fortnightly for senior engineers, and asked for written agendas. Two people said it felt distant. We're keeping it for a quarter and checking again in January.
:::

The second was drafted with a tool and says so. It has a decision, a reason, a cost and a date to review it. That's what Slop Mop's "useful to readers" counter-signal looks for, and it counts the same whoever typed the words. [How it works](/how-it-works.html) explains how that counter-signal lowers a score.

The glossary has names for the bad version too. Sending AI output you never read is an [AI slap](/glossary/ai-slap/); the person who does it is a [meat proxy](/glossary/meat-proxy/). In the office it's [workslop](/glossary/workslop/): polished-looking work that pushes the real effort onto whoever receives it, which a [Harvard Business Review study](https://hbr.org/2025/09/ai-generated-workslop-is-destroying-productivity) found was common enough to measure.

## A checklist for AI-assisted writing before you post

1. **Can you say the point in one sentence?** If not, the post doesn't have one yet.
2. **Is there one detail only you could have written?** A number, a name, what actually happened.
3. **Did you delete what isn't true?** Tools invent plausible facts. Check every claim.
4. **Did you cut the stock phrases?** Watch for [stiff phrasing](/patterns/stiff-phrasing/) ("Furthermore", "Ultimately"), [hype words](/patterns/hype-words/) and ["not X, but Y"](/patterns/not-x-but-y/) slogans.
5. **Would you answer a question about any line in it?** If not, that line goes.

Paste a draft into the [content value density calculator](/calculators/content-value-density/) to see how many concrete details it carries before you post it.

## Using AI-assisted writing with a clear conscience

You don't need to hide that you used a tool, and readers who judge the writing won't care. What they'll notice is whether you had something to say.
