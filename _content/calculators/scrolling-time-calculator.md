Forty-five minutes a day doesn't feel like much. Over a year it's more than 270 hours. The scrolling time calculator splits that time into the part spent on posts worth reading and the part you wouldn't miss, then puts the second part in a unit people tend to feel: books.

## How the scrolling time calculator works

1. **Minutes a day in feeds.** Your phone's screen-time report and your browser history give a real figure. Use it rather than a guess; most people's guesses are low.
2. **Share spent on things worth reading.** The posts you'd be glad you read: a colleague's lesson from a project, a thread with real numbers, an answer on Reddit that solved your problem.
3. **Hours to read a typical book.** Six hours is the example; adjust it for the books you read.

The calculator multiplies by 365 days and shows the year in hours, split into the two parts, and the "wouldn't miss" hours divided by the length of a book. At the example settings that's about 205 hours, or 34 books.

## The scrolling time formula

| Step | Calculation |
|---|---|
| Hours a year in feeds | minutes a day × 365 ÷ 60 |
| Hours worth reading | hours a year × share worth reading |
| Hours you wouldn't miss | hours a year − hours worth reading |
| Books' worth | hours you wouldn't miss ÷ hours per book |

It runs in this page and sends nothing anywhere.

## Reading versus scrolling: what changes the split

The share worth reading is the input most people can move. It depends less on how long you scroll than on what the feeds put in front of you:

- **Who you follow.** A pass through who you follow, using the controls in [Slop Mop for LinkedIn](/for/linkedin/) or [for Reddit](/for/reddit/), shifts the mix more than any single setting.
- **Chronological feeds.** Following and Recent views show fewer recommended posts, which is where much of the [engagement farming](/glossary/rage-bait/) lives.
- **Knowing the tells.** Once you recognise [too-tidy stories](/patterns/too-tidy-story/) and [hype words](/patterns/hype-words/), you stop reading them to the end.
- **Folding the slop.** Slop Mop reads each post before you reach it on LinkedIn, X, Reddit, Facebook, Substack and Medium and folds likely slop, so the minutes you do spend go further. [How it works](/how-it-works.html) explains the twelve questions behind each score.

## What the scrolling time calculator can't tell you

It treats every "wouldn't miss" minute the same, and it can't see why you open a feed. Some scrolling is rest, and some is a habit that, as Merriam-Webster's [doomscrolling definition](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/doomscrolling) puts it, keeps you reading bad news you'd rather not. Nielsen Norman Group's piece on [information pollution](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/information-pollution/) is a useful read on why low-value information costs more than its length suggests.

If the hours surprised you, [add Slop Mop to your browser](/) and it folds the posts you wouldn't miss.
