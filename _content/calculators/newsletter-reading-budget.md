Newsletters arrive one at a time, so their cost hides. Eighteen of them, each sending one or two issues a week that take six minutes to read, ask for over two and a half hours a week. The newsletter reading budget calculator puts that number next to the time you actually want to give them.

## How the newsletter reading budget works

Five inputs:

1. **Newsletters you subscribe to.** Gmail's [Manage subscriptions](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/15621070) page lists your senders in one place, which makes counting easy; the [guides](/guides/) walk through it for Gmail and Outlook.
2. **Issues a week from each, on average.** Daily newsletters count as five or seven; monthly ones as a quarter.
3. **Minutes to read one issue.** Long-form essays take ten minutes or more; a link roundup takes two.
4. **Share you'd miss if they stopped.** The honest test. If one stopped arriving, would you notice?
5. **Minutes a week you want to spend.** Your budget.

The results show the full weekly cost, the yearly hours, the time spent on the ones you'd miss, how many newsletters fit the budget at your average length, and how many to cut or demote to a folder you read once a week.

## The newsletter budget formula

| Step | Calculation |
|---|---|
| Minutes per newsletter a week | issues a week × minutes per issue |
| Minutes a week for all of them | newsletters × minutes per newsletter |
| Minutes on the ones you'd miss | all minutes × share you'd miss |
| Newsletters that fit | budget ÷ minutes per newsletter, rounded down |
| Newsletters to cut or demote | newsletters − newsletters that fit |

It runs in this page. Nothing you enter is sent anywhere.

## Getting your inbox inside the newsletter reading budget

Cutting isn't the only option:

- **Demote, don't delete.** A filter that skips the inbox and labels a newsletter "Weekend" keeps it without the daily cost. Gmail filters and Outlook rules both do this; the [comparison with email triage tools](/compare/slop-mop-vs-email-triage-tools/) covers the options.
- **Judge issues, not senders.** A good newsletter still sends a padded issue now and then. The glossary entry on [bacn](/glossary/bacn/), email you asked for and never read, covers why the pile grows.
- **Let something read ahead.** Slop Mop for Gmail and Outlook, part of Slop Mop XL, checks what each sender wrote and marks or folds the padded issues, after replacing personal details with placeholders in your mailbox. [How it works](/how-it-works.html) explains the scoring, and [Slop Mop for email](/for/email/) covers the privacy handling.

When the number of newsletters to cut reads zero at a budget you're happy with, you're done. If not, [get Slop Mop for Gmail and Outlook](/index.html#pricing) and let it mark the issues that aren't worth your minutes.
