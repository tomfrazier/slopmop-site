## What it means

Outrage porn is media made to provoke anger because anger pays. The story, clip or post exists to give you a jolt of indignation, then a reason to share it so others can feel the same. The "porn" in the name is about the transaction: a reliable, repeatable hit with little else attached.

It runs on a few familiar moves: an extreme example presented as typical, a quote stripped of context, a villain you can judge in one line, and a headline that tells you how to feel before you know what happened. Researchers at Tufts University, Jeffrey Berry and Sarah Sobieraj, described outrage media as both a genre and a style, using overgeneralisation, sensationalism, misleading information and personal attacks. They also noted that it tends to react to news already reported rather than break its own.

Why it works has been studied too. In 2014 Jonah Berger of the Wharton School described anger as a "high-arousal emotion" that makes people more likely to pass things on.

## Where it came from

The cartoonist and essayist Tim Kreider coined the term in a *New York Times* piece in July 2009. He wrote that "it sometimes seems as if most of the news consists of outrage porn, selected specifically to pander to our impulses to judge and punish and get us all riled up with righteous indignation."

The media critic Ryan Holiday picked it up in his 2012 book *Trust Me, I'm Lying*, using it for manufactured online controversy. The idea now covers cable news segments, talk radio, blogs and the feed posts that copy their style.

## Examples

Two posts about the same planning decision:

:::slop
They're doing it AGAIN. 🤬 The council has quietly approved 400 luxury flats on the park YOUR kids play in. No consultation. No shame. Share this before they delete it.
:::
:::fine
The planning committee approved 140 flats on the former depot next to Elm Park on 9 October, with 30% at social rent. The park itself isn't affected. Objections closed on 2 September; minutes are on the council site.
:::

The first gets the number, the site and the process wrong, tells you what to feel, and asks you to spread it before checking. The second might still make you angry, but it gives you the facts to decide.

## The criticism

Kreider himself drew a line: "outrage is healthy to the extent that it causes us to act against injustice." The label can be used to dismiss anger that's justified, as if any strong reaction were a sign of manipulation. Telling outrage porn from legitimate outrage means asking whether the piece informs or only inflames, and people on different sides of an issue will often answer differently.
