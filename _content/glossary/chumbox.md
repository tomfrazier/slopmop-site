## What it means

A chumbox is the block of thumbnail ads that sits under an article, usually labelled something like "Around the Web" or "You may also like". Each tile pairs a lurid picture with a headline, and each one leads off the page to a slideshow, an affiliate site or a product pitch.

The name comes from chumming, the fishing practice of throwing fish scraps into the water to draw fish in. The comparison is unkind and deliberate: the content is bait, and the reader is the fish.

Chumbox ads tend to promise easy fixes for hard medical or money problems, use phrases like "one weird trick", and suggest that someone powerful doesn't want you to know. Some look amateurish on purpose, so the product seems to come from an outsider.

## Where it came from

Earlier uses exist, but the term was popularised by John Mahoney's 2015 article in The Awl, which catalogued the images that keep turning up: the skin thing, the pill, the weight-loss thing, the weird food, the celebrity, the money thing, and an older person's face for ads aimed at older readers.

The format grew in the early 2010s, when Outbrain and Taboola became the main providers and the boxes spread across major news sites. By 2016 they were on 41 of the top 50 news websites. Publishers run them because they pay reliably. Some started to reconsider in 2016, worried about what the ads did to their brands.

When the Reply All podcast visited Taboola in 2018, its founder said he had never heard the word chumbox and called the ads "recommendations".

## Examples

An invented chumbox, described tile by tile:

:::example
Six tiles under a news story about council tax. "Doctors stunned: this kitchen spice melts belly fat." "Homeowners in your area are claiming this before Friday." "Remember her? Try not to gasp when you see her now." "The one thing banks hope you never notice on your statement." "Retirees are switching to this instead of a pension." A close-up of something that might be a fruit.
:::

None of the tiles relates to the article. Two use your location to seem local, three promise a result with no cost, and every headline is written to make you click before you've decided whether you care.
