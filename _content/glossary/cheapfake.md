## What a cheapfake is

A cheapfake is a piece of audio, video or imagery that misleads without any sophisticated technology. Someone crops out the part of a photo that changes its meaning, slows a clip so a speaker sounds drunk, splices two answers together, or posts a real video from 2015 with a caption saying it happened yesterday.

The common methods:

- **Speed changes:** slowing a clip down or speeding it up.
- **Selective editing:** cutting out the question, the context or the second half of a sentence.
- **Recontextualising:** real footage, wrong place or wrong date.
- **Simple editing software:** a Photoshop job, or a lookalike standing in for the real person.

The contrast with a [deepfake](/glossary/deepfake/) is the method. A deepfake uses machine learning to generate or swap faces and voices. A cheapfake uses tools anyone already has, and sometimes no tool at all. Both can mislead, and a cheapfake can be harder to dismiss because every frame in it is real.

## Where the word cheapfake came from

Britt Paris and Joan Donovan coined "cheap fakes" in [*Deepfakes and Cheap Fakes: The Manipulation of Audio and Visual Evidence*](https://datasociety.net/library/deepfakes-and-cheap-fakes/), a report published by the research institute Data & Society on 18 September 2019. Their point was that deceptive media has never needed advanced technology, and that a purely technological fix wouldn't be enough. You'll also see it written as one word, "cheapfake", and the near-synonym "shallowfake". Oren Etzioni and Daniel S. Weld included it in their [October 2026 GeekWire glossary of AI-era language](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/etzioni-on-ai-the-colorful-and-cranky-language-of-the-ai-era-from-slop-to-slap/) as "manipulation made with cheap software or none at all", with the reminder that most of the damage doesn't need AI.

## Examples

These are invented to show the patterns, and they don't describe real events:

:::example
**Old footage, new caption:** a video of a flooded high street, filmed years ago in another country, reshared during a local storm as "our town right now".
:::
:::example
**A slowed clip:** a 12-second excerpt of a council meeting played at 75% speed, so the chair seems to slur.
:::
:::example
**A crop:** a photo of a near-empty concert venue, cropped so the packed stalls are out of frame, posted with "nobody turned up".
:::

Checking a cheapfake usually means finding the original: the full clip, the uncropped photo, the first time the image was posted. A reverse image search or the source's own upload often settles it.
