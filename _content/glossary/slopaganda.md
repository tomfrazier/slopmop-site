## What it means

Slopaganda is AI-generated content produced in bulk and spread to change what people believe about politics. The word joins "slop", meaning unwanted AI-generated content, with "propaganda".

What makes it different from older propaganda is cost and volume. When a model can produce thousands of articles, images or posts a day, a campaign no longer needs each item to be convincing. It needs enough of them, aimed at the right audiences, that the message feels familiar and the corrections can't keep up.

The term is for the flood: many items, cheaply made, distributed with some targeting. A single viral fake doesn't fit the definition on its own.

## Where it came from

The term comes from a paper by the researchers Michał Klincewicz, Mark Alfano and Amir Ebrahimi Fard, "Slopaganda: The interaction between propaganda and generative AI", first posted to arXiv on 3 March 2025. The authors say plainly that the word is their own neologism.

Their working definition: "unwanted AI-generated content that is spread in order to manipulate beliefs to achieve political ends." They build on a definition of propaganda as the intentional manipulation of beliefs for political ends, and on "slop" as the name for unwanted AI output. The paper is careful about scope. It sets aside one-off AI fakes posted by people with very large audiences, because those rely on the poster's reach more than on volume and targeting.

The paper argues that two things make slopaganda new: it can be tailored to small audiences, down to individuals, and it can pose as trusted local news. It also draws on known effects such as repeated exposure making claims feel truer, whether or not they are.

The term is still mostly used in research and commentary. It hasn't settled into everyday speech the way [AI slop](/glossary/ai-slop/) has.

## Examples

These are invented. Neither refers to a real campaign or party.

:::slop
LOCAL NEWS: Residents in Millbrook are SHOCKED by what the council just approved. Nobody is talking about this. Share before it gets taken down. (One of 40 near-identical posts, each naming a different town.)
:::
:::fine
Millbrook council voted 6–3 on Tuesday to rezone the old depot site for housing. The minutes and the planning report are on the council website.
:::

The first post has the marks of slop and of a campaign: urgency, no source, a share request and a template repeated across towns. The second states what happened and where to check it.

## The criticism

The main caution is about how the word gets used. It's easy to call any political content you dislike "slopaganda", and that turns a description of a method into an insult. Telling whether something is part of a coordinated, AI-driven campaign needs evidence about who made it and how it spread, which a single post rarely shows.

The paper itself is a conceptual argument with illustrative cases. It doesn't measure how much slopaganda exists or how many minds it changes, so treat claims about its scale with the same care as any other number you see online.
