## What botshit means

Botshit is chatbot output that isn't true, used by a person as if it were. A chatbot invents a statistic, a court case or a product feature; someone copies it into a report, an email or a post; now it's out in the world with a human name on it.

The word covers the whole chain, which is what makes it useful. A chatbot getting something wrong is a [hallucination](/glossary/hallucination/). It becomes botshit when a person picks that error up without checking and uses it to inform, persuade or decide. The bot supplies the falsehood; the human supplies the credibility.

The person may not be lying. Usually they just didn't look. That's why the term sits close to [AI slap](/glossary/ai-slap/), sending AI output you never read, and to the person who does it, the [meat proxy](/glossary/meat-proxy/).

## Where the term botshit came from

The term was introduced by the business scholars Timothy R. Hannigan, Ian P. McCarthy and André Spicer in "[Beware of botshit: How to manage the epistemic risks of generative chatbots](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bushor.2024.03.001)", published in Business Horizons in 2024. It plays on the philosopher Harry Frankfurt's account of [bullshit](/glossary/bullshit/) as speech produced with no regard for whether it is true.

Their argument is that chatbots predict plausible responses without knowing what those responses mean, so some of what they produce is untrue. When people use that untrue content for tasks, it becomes what the authors call botshit. The paper sets out four ways of working with chatbots, which it calls authenticated, autonomous, automated and augmented, and pairs each with a botshit-related risk: ignorance, miscalibration, routinisation and black boxing.

## Examples

A line from a sales deck, as it came out of a chatbot and after someone checked it:

:::slop
According to a 2023 Gartner study, 78% of mid-sized firms that adopted automated invoicing saw a 3x return within six months.
:::
:::fine
We couldn't find a published study for the figure we'd been quoting, so we've removed it. Our own numbers: the three clients who switched last year cut invoice processing from about 9 days to 4.
:::

The first sentence has the shape of a real citation: a named firm, a year, a precise percentage. Nobody checked whether the study exists. The second is smaller and less impressive, and every part of it can be traced.

:::example
A lawyer files a brief citing three earlier cases. The cases were suggested by a chatbot, and none of them exists.
:::
