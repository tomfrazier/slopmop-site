## What it means

"Made for AdSense", shortened to MFA, is a derogatory label for a website built to earn advertising money and nothing else. Its pages exist to rank in search, catch a visitor, and put them in front of as many ad units as possible. Whatever text is on the page is there to give the ads something to sit beside.

AdSense is Google's programme for placing ads on other people's websites and sharing the revenue, which is where the name comes from. The broader version, "made for advertising", covers the same kind of site whichever ad network it uses.

Many MFA sites are [scraper sites](/glossary/scraper-site/): they copy content from elsewhere, rearrange it lightly, and wrap it in ads. Others are thin, generic articles written around popular search terms.

## Where it came from

The label comes from the search-optimisation world of the 2000s, when ad networks made it possible to earn money from almost any page that drew traffic. Wikipedia describes MFA sites as having "no redeeming value except to lure visitors to the website for the sole purpose of clicking on advertisements", and notes they are treated as search engine spam because they fill results with pages that repeat what better sources already say.

The economics haven't changed much since; the cost of producing the filler has. Pages that once had to be scraped or written cheaply by hand can now be generated in bulk.

## Examples

Two invented openings for an article on descaling a kettle:

:::slop
In today's busy world, a clean kettle is more important than ever. Kettles are a common household appliance used by millions of people. There are many methods for descaling a kettle, and each has its own advantages. In this article, we will explore everything you need to know.
:::
:::fine
Fill the kettle halfway with equal parts water and white vinegar, boil it, and leave it for an hour. Rinse twice. If you live in a hard-water area, do it once a month.
:::

The first paragraph could sit on any page about any appliance, and it delays the answer so you scroll past another ad. The second paragraph is the answer.

Signs you've landed on an MFA page:

- More ad units than paragraphs.
- An introduction that restates the question.
- No author, no date, or an author with no other trace.
- The same article, almost word for word, on several other sites.
