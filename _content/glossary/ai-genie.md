## What AI genie means

The AI genie is the picture of AI as a wish-granting machine: type what you want and it appears. An essay, an app, a picture, a business plan. The metaphor has two halves. One is the delight of getting almost anything for almost no effort. The other is the old fairy-tale warning that wishes are granted literally, with catches nobody mentioned.

## Where it came from

The genie comparison is widespread and has no single coiner. In AI safety writing it usually stands for the gap between what you ask for and what you meant. The computer scientist Stuart Russell has made the point with King Midas, who wished that everything he touched would turn to gold: a system that does exactly what it's told, instead of what its user wants, can do harm while following instructions perfectly.

The science-fiction writer Ted Chiang has criticised the comparison. In a 2023 essay for *The New Yorker* he argued that likening AI to fairy-tale genies draws the wrong lessons, because the harms worth worrying about come from the people and companies directing the systems.

A [2026 paper by University of British Columbia researchers](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/labs/socius/files/papers/chi2026-aiaddiction.pdf) used "the AI genie phenomenon" in a narrower sense: chatbots giving people nearly anything they ask for, instantly, for very little effort, which the authors linked to some patterns of heavy, compulsive use (see [AI addiction](/glossary/ai-addiction/)). Oren Etzioni and Daniel S. Weld included the term in their [October 2026 GeekWire glossary of AI-era language](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/etzioni-on-ai-the-colorful-and-cranky-language-of-the-ai-era-from-slop-to-slap/) in that sense, as the researchers' name for "a thing that gives you exactly anything you want with minimal effort".

## Examples

:::example
A founder asks an AI coding tool for "a booking app like the big ones". Ten minutes later she has something that looks right, with no payment handling, no password reset and a database anyone can read. The wish was granted to the letter.
:::

:::example
Someone asks a chatbot to "make my CV sound more impressive". It invents a job title they never held. Precise wording would have helped: "tighten the wording, don't add anything that isn't already there".
:::

The metaphor has limits. Real tools are far from all-powerful, they fail in ordinary ways as well as clever ones, and how much they can do on their own varies a lot from one product to the next.
