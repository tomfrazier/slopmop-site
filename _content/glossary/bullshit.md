## What it means

In everyday English, bullshit means nonsense. The philosopher Harry G. Frankfurt gave it a narrower meaning: speech produced without any concern for whether it is true. The bullshitter wants to impress, persuade or get through the meeting, and picks words for that purpose alone. Some of what they say may be accurate. They didn't check, and they wouldn't have changed it if they had.

Frankfurt's key move was to separate bullshit from lying. A liar knows the truth and steers you away from it, so the liar still has to keep track of it. The bullshitter has stopped tracking it at all. That's why Frankfurt thought it the more corrosive of the two: "Bullshit is a greater enemy of the truth than lies are."

He also had a theory of why there is so much of it. People are expected to have an opinion on everything, and when they speak beyond what they know, bullshit fills the gap.

## Where it came from

Frankfurt, an American philosopher, first published "On Bullshit" as an essay in the *Raritan Quarterly Review* in 1986. Princeton University Press reissued it as a small book in 2005, and it spent 27 weeks on *The New York Times* best-seller list. He followed it with *On Truth* in 2006.

The idea found a second life with large language models. In June 2024 Michael Townsen Hicks, James Humphries and Joe Slater, published "ChatGPT is bullshit" in *Ethics and Information Technology*. They argued that chatbot falsehoods, usually called "hallucinations", are better described as bullshit in Frankfurt's sense, because the models are "in an important way indifferent to the truth of their outputs". The uncritical use of that output by people has its own name: [botshit](/glossary/botshit/).

## Examples

A post that sounds authoritative and isn't anchored to anything, followed by one that says what the writer actually knows:

:::slop
Research consistently shows that companies with a strong culture outperform their peers by up to 4x. The data is clear: culture eats strategy for breakfast, and the leaders who get this will win the next decade.
:::
:::fine
I don't have a study for this, only our own two teams. The one that held a 20-minute retro every Friday shipped 11 releases this quarter; the one that didn't shipped 6. Small sample, but we're trying it on the third team in November.
:::

The first may even be roughly true. The writer gives no sign of caring which study, which peers or what "4x" measures. The second is smaller and checkable.

## The criticism

Frankfurt's book was a best seller and widely praised, and also criticised as too narrow: it focuses on one motive, indifference to truth, and has little to say about how audiences spot bullshit or why they tolerate it. Applying the term to software adds a further problem, since Frankfurt described a speaker's attitude and a model has no attitude in the ordinary sense. Hicks and his co-authors take this on directly, distinguishing two senses of bullshitter and arguing that chatbots meet at least one of them.
