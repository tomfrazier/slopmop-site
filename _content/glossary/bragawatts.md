## What bragawatts means

[Cambridge Dictionary](https://dictionaryblog.cambridge.org/2026/01/26/new-words-26-january-2026/), which listed it among its new words in January 2026, defines bragawatts as "the practice of AI companies boasting about very large projects to create excitement and attract investment, even though many of these projects are never completed." The joke is in the unit: a bragawatt is power that exists mainly in an announcement.

The word is used, usually with a raised eyebrow, for AI data centre plans that promise gigawatts of capacity before the land, the grid connection, the turbines or the money are in place. AI models need a lot of electricity to train and run, so power has become a way for AI companies to signal size. Announcing a 5-gigawatt campus gets headlines and impresses investors whether or not it ever draws 5 gigawatts. Calling it bragawatts is a way of saying: show me the substation.

A useful way to read any big power number is to ask which of these it is:

- **Announced:** someone said it at a launch or on an earnings call.
- **Contracted:** there's a signed deal for the land, the power or the equipment.
- **Operating:** the electricity is flowing and the servers are running.

Bragawatts live in the first group and get reported as if they were in the third. Its sister term [vibe revenue](/glossary/vibe-revenue/) does the same thing to the income statement.

## Where bragawatts came from

According to Erin Griffith's May 2026 column on the term, it started more than a decade ago in the energy industry, where it described power from solar or wind projects that had no realistic chance of being built. As AI companies grew bolder about their infrastructure plans in 2025, commentators picked it up again. Griffith names Waldemar Szlezak, head of infrastructure at the private equity firm KKR, as one of those who repurposed it, in a Financial Times column urging investors to look at the real state of the power grid.

The same column notes that executives now use it as trash talk: my project is real, my rival's is bragawatts. Spellings vary. "Braggawatts" and "brag-a-watts" both turn up in industry newsletters and trade coverage.

Oren Etzioni and Daniel S. Weld included it, with Cambridge's definition, in their [October 2026 GeekWire glossary of AI-era language](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/etzioni-on-ai-the-colorful-and-cranky-language-of-the-ai-era-from-slop-to-slap/), calling it "a unit of measurement for boasting".

## Examples

Two announcements of the same data centre project, written our own way:

:::slop
We're thrilled to unveil a game-changing 10GW AI campus that will power the next era of intelligence. The future is being built right here. 🚀
:::
:::fine
We've signed a lease on 400 acres and applied to connect 1.2GW to the grid. The utility's study is due in March. If it's approved, the first 300MW could be running in 2028.
:::

A dated usage, as you might see it in a trade newsletter:

:::example
"Of the 40GW announced in the region this year, about 6GW has a signed grid agreement. The rest is bragawatts until proven otherwise."
:::

The second announcement is smaller and much easier to believe, because it tells you what's done, what isn't and when you'll find out.
