## What scaled content abuse means

Scaled content abuse is Google's name for publishing large numbers of pages mainly to rank in search, with little regard for whether they help anyone. Its [spam policy](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) defines it as "when many pages are generated for the primary purpose of manipulating search rankings and not helping users", typically "large amounts of unoriginal content that provides little to no value to users, no matter how it's created".

That last phrase is the important one. The policy doesn't care whether the pages came from an AI model, a scraper or a room of underpaid writers. What it targets is volume without value.

Google's examples include:

- Using generative AI tools to produce many pages without adding value for users.
- Scraping feeds or search results and rewording them, including by "synonymizing, translating, or other obfuscation techniques".
- Stitching together content from different pages without adding anything.
- Spreading the content over many sites to hide how much of it there is.
- Pages that make little or no sense to a reader but contain search keywords.

## Where scaled content abuse came from

Google announced the policy on 5 March 2024, alongside its March 2024 core update and two other new spam policies, expired domain abuse and [site reputation abuse](/glossary/site-reputation-abuse/). The announcement said the policy builds on an older rule against automatically generated content, so that Google can act "no matter whether content is produced through automation, human efforts, or some combination of human and automated processes."

The new wording covers what the old automation rule could miss: the same mass of thin pages produced by people, or by people and AI together. Sites that break the policy can rank lower or be removed from results.

## Examples

Invented, in the style the policy describes.

:::slop
Best Coffee Shops in Bristol (2026 Guide)

Bristol is a vibrant city with a thriving coffee scene. Whether you're a local or a visitor, finding the perfect cup of coffee is essential. In this guide, we explore the best coffee shops in Bristol to elevate your caffeine experience.
:::

Now picture the same page repeated for 4,000 towns, with only the name changed, and you have scaled content abuse.

:::fine
Six Bristol coffee shops we visited in September, with opening hours and what we ordered. Two have closed since our 2025 list. One of them was our favourite.
:::

AI use alone doesn't put a page on the wrong side of the policy. Scale with no first-hand detail does.
