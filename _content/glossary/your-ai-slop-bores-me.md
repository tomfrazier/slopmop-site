## What Your AI Slop Bores Me means

"Your AI slop bores me" started as a put-down: a reply to drop under an AI-generated post to say you'd noticed and weren't impressed. It's now also the name of a website built on the joke. You type a prompt, as you would into ChatGPT, and the answer comes from another human visitor, picked at random, who is pretending to be a chatbot.

Answerers can write or draw their reply, and they have to be quick. According to NPR, the site gives them 75 seconds, roughly the speed people expect from a machine, so the drawings come out scrappy and the answers range from helpful to deliberately unhelpful. That's most of the fun. The site's name and its two tabs, "human" and "larp as ai", make clear that nobody on it is a real chatbot.

## Where Your AI Slop Bores Me came from

[Know Your Meme traces the phrase](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/sites/your-ai-slop-bores-me) to a reaction image, a boy sitting on a throne of Pepsi with the line "your AI slop bores me", posted by the Artists Against Generative AI page on 17 October 2025 for use in the comments of AI-generated posts. It spread over the following months as a stock way of calling out [AI slop](/glossary/ai-slop/).

The website came from Mihir Maroju, a 17-year-old in Puducherry, India, according to NPR. It launched in early March 2026 and went viral within days as people shared screenshots of their exchanges. Fast Company reported 50 million hits and about 16,000 people using it at the same time. A month later Maroju told NPR it had received more than 25 million unique visitors and nearly 280 million hits, and that the team had added tools to filter harmful contributions after "a lot of spam and people exploiting loopholes".

In their October 2026 GeekWire glossary of AI-era language, Oren Etzioni and Daniel Weld read its popularity as a sign of "how badly people wanted to say something back".

## How Slop Mop sees Your AI Slop Bores Me

The site is a game and the phrase is a reaction, so both are outside what Slop Mop judges. The joke does make a point Slop Mop shares: a human answer can be just as lazy as a machine one, which is why Slop Mop judges the writing, not who wrote it, as [How it works](/how-it-works.html) explains.
