## What it means

Churnalism is journalism that processes material instead of finding it. A press release or a wire story arrives, a reporter rearranges the paragraphs, adds a headline and perhaps one quote, and it's published as news. Nobody rang anyone, checked a figure or asked who benefits from the story.

The result often reads smoothly, because press releases are written by professionals. What's missing is the reporting: the second source, the awkward question, the number the company left out. Readers get the version of events that someone paid to put out, presented as an independent account.

## Where it came from

The word blends "churn" and "journalism". It is credited to the BBC journalist Waseem Zakir, who has said he used it years before it caught on. It reached a wide audience through Nick Davies's 2008 book *Flat Earth News*, a critique of British newspapers.

Davies drew on research by a team at Cardiff University. As reported, it found that 60% of stories in the UK quality press consisted wholly or mainly of wire copy or PR material, and a further 20% contained clear elements of it. Different summaries of the book quote different figures from the same work, so treat any single number with some care.

The Media Standards Trust later launched churnalism.com, a tool that let anyone paste in an article and compare it with a database of press releases to see how much text they shared.

## Examples

A company announcement, then two stories about it. Both are invented.

:::slop
Brightwell Foods today announced an exciting new partnership with GreenCrate that will revolutionise sustainable packaging. "This is a game-changing moment for our industry," said CEO Dana Hirst. The partnership will see Brightwell move to 100% recyclable packaging, cementing its position as a leader in sustainability.
:::
:::fine
Brightwell Foods says it will switch to recyclable packaging through a deal with GreenCrate, but has not given a date. Its current trays are a mixed plastic that most UK councils don't collect. GreenCrate's material is accepted by about half of them, according to the company. Brightwell declined to say what the switch will cost.
:::

The first story is the press release with "today announced" kept in. The second uses the same announcement as a starting point, then adds what a reader needs to judge it: no date, the recycling caveat, and the question the company wouldn't answer.
