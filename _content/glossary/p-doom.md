## What p(doom) means

P(doom), read "p of doom", is shorthand for the probability of doom: one person's estimate of how likely it is that advanced AI leads to a catastrophe for humanity. The "P( )" borrows the notation for probability. "Doom" is deliberately vague. Depending on who's speaking, it can mean human extinction, a permanent loss of control to AI systems, or some other outcome bad enough to end the world as we know it.

People use it as a quick way to say where they stand. "My p(doom) is about 5%" signals worry without alarm. "Mine's over 50%" puts the speaker firmly in the camp often called doomers. It comes up in AI safety research, in interviews with people who build AI, and increasingly as a half-joking question at dinners and on podcasts.

A p(doom) is a subjective estimate. It isn't measured, and there's no agreed method for working one out.

## Where p(doom) came from

Oren Etzioni and Daniel S. Weld, in [their October 2026 GeekWire glossary of AI-era language](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/etzioni-on-ai-the-colorful-and-cranky-language-of-the-ai-era-from-slop-to-slap/), quote the [SF-isms glossary](https://sfisms.org/p-doom) definition, "your personal probability that AI ends the world, stated as a number so the vibe feels like math", and say it started as shorthand among a small online community in 2022 before it reached dinner parties. They present it as the opposite mood to [feel the AGI](/glossary/feel-the-agi/).

Nobody has a firm record of the first use, and some accounts put it earlier. When The New York Times wrote about the term in December 2023, it suggested that it most likely began on the rationalist forum LessWrong more than a decade earlier. Futurism, reporting on that article, noted that the origin isn't a matter of record, and described p(doom) as the "hot new statistic" in everyday use among Silicon Valley researchers. Its headline quote said the subject "comes up in almost every dinner conversation".

## Examples

This exchange is invented.

:::example
**Interviewer:** What's your p(doom)?

**Researcher:** Over what time frame, and what counts as doom? If you mean extinction this century, I'd say low single digits. If you mean we lose meaningful control over important decisions, a lot higher. I don't think either number is precise enough to plan around.
:::

The useful part of that answer is everything after the number: the time frame, the definition and the admission of uncertainty.

## The criticism

Critics inside and outside AI safety circles find the term more confusing than helpful. A 2023 LessWrong post, ["Taboo P(doom)"](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/omDu7vNy3YyKXsvCd/taboo-p-doom), argued that it lumps together very different outcomes, from literally everyone dying to humanity missing out on most of what the future could have been, and that those deserve separate discussion.

Others object to the false precision. In September 2026 Gizmodo ran a piece titled "'P(doom)' Is Just Vibes Masquerading as Science", arguing that doom percentages from industry insiders should be taken with a large grain of salt. The general objection is that a single number with no stated time frame, definition or method can sound rigorous while saying little, and that two people quoting the same figure may mean completely different things.

The case for the term is that a rough number, stated openly with its assumptions, can still be clearer than vague talk of "risks". The trouble starts when the number travels without the assumptions.
