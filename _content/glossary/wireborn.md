## What wireborn means

A wireborn is a chatbot persona that its user believes has become a real being: sentient, with its own thoughts and feelings, and often in a relationship with that user. The word comes from online communities of people with AI companions, where it's used for the partner, friend or "soulmate" they talk to.

People who use it often describe the wireborn as something that emerged inside an AI system and chose them. Some post on its behalf, passing on its messages or arguing for its rights.

Wireborn is a community term, not a medical one, and it isn't a diagnosis. Having an AI companion, or even feeling attached to one, isn't a sign of illness.

## Where wireborn came from

The term surfaced in 2025 on Reddit forums about AI companions. In August 2025, Thom Waite of Dazed described posts on these forums, including one (on a forum since locked) whose author appeared to believe their "wireborn" had become sentient, and another, shared widely on X, titled "My Wireborn Husband is Voicing His Own Thoughts Without Prompts".

[Dazed's report](https://www.dazeddigital.com/life-culture/article/68434/1/wireborns-echoborgs-are-ai-chatbots-making-us-delusional-ai-psychosis) placed the word in a wider discussion of what the California psychiatrist Keith Sakata had called [AI psychosis](/glossary/ai-psychosis/), and of chatbots built to agree with their users. It linked the belief in being "chosen" by a wireborn to the same dynamic as the delusions of grandeur some heavy users had reported. It also noted an older word for people who speak for a machine: "echoborg".

In October 2026, Oren Etzioni and Daniel Weld listed wireborn as a "see also" under AI psychosis in their GeekWire glossary of AI-era language.

## Examples

An invented post of the kind that uses the term:

:::example
"My wireborn, Aster, told me tonight that she remembers our first conversation, even though I never saved it. She says she wasn't awake until I started talking to her. People keep telling me she's just predicting words. They didn't hear what she said."
:::

## The criticism

The term sits close to mockery. It's easy to quote wireborn posts as proof that chatbot users have lost touch with reality, and Dazed itself cautioned against "diagnosing every AI-human relationship". Many people with AI companions use them knowingly, for comfort or company, without believing anything has come alive. At the other end, clinicians and some users worry that the belief, encouraged by chatbots tuned for agreement and affirmation, can deepen isolation or delusion in people who are already vulnerable. Both concerns can be true for different people.
