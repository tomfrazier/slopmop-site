## What counterfeit people means

A counterfeit person is a machine passed off as a human: a chatbot that says it's a real support agent called Sam, a network of AI-run profiles posing as ordinary customers, a voice clone that phones you as if it were a relative. The key part is the pretence. Like a counterfeit banknote, it only works if you don't know.

That's also where the line sits. An AI assistant that tells you it's an AI isn't counterfeit. Nor is an avatar someone uses openly, a text-to-speech voice for a person who can't speak, or a brand mascot nobody mistakes for a real employee. The term applies when the system is presented as a person, or set up so that you'd reasonably assume it is one.

## Where counterfeit people came from

The philosopher Daniel Dennett introduced the phrase in [an essay for The Atlantic](https://www.theatlantic.com/technology/archive/2023/05/problem-counterfeit-people/674075/) in May 2023, shortly after ChatGPT went mainstream. He argued that AI made it possible, for the first time, to create convincing fake people at scale, and that this threatened the trust that conversation, markets and democracy depend on. He compared it to counterfeit money, which governments treat as a serious crime because it undermines everyone's confidence in the real thing, and called for creating or passing on counterfeit people to be outlawed, with tough penalties.

The phrase has stuck as a way to talk about undisclosed AI personas, even among people who don't share Dennett's proposed remedy. Oren Etzioni and Daniel S. Weld included it in their [October 2026 GeekWire glossary of AI-era language](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/etzioni-on-ai-the-colorful-and-cranky-language-of-the-ai-era-from-slop-to-slap/), calling it Dennett's last argument. They count Tilly Norwood, an AI actress unveiled at the Zurich Film Festival in September 2025, among them; they report that her makers said talent agents wanted to sign her. Whether a character openly presented as AI fits Dennett's definition is part of the argument. Fake accounts at scale are also what the [dead internet theory](/glossary/dead-internet-theory/) worries about.

## Examples

Invented examples of where the line falls:

:::example
**Counterfeit:** a "community member" account on a forum that has posted 400 enthusiastic product reviews in a month, signs off as "a mum of two from Leeds" and is run entirely by a marketing tool.
:::
:::example
**Not counterfeit:** a help-desk chat window that opens with "I'm an automated assistant. Type 'agent' to reach a person."
:::

A reply from a counterfeit person often reads like this, and so does plenty of human filler:

:::slop
What an incredibly insightful post! This truly resonates. Thank you for sharing your amazing perspective with the community. 🙌
:::
:::fine
We tried the same rota change in our clinic. It helped with handovers, but Friday cover got worse, so we went back to the old pattern for weekends.
:::

## The criticism

Dennett's essay drew pushback. In 3 Quarks Daily, Tim Sommers argued that the harms Dennett listed don't depend on AI being anything like a real person, that many of them were already happening, and that the "counterfeit" framing could distract from those concrete problems. At Crooked Timber, the philosopher Eric Schliesser read the essay as alarmist. Any ban would also have to be drawn carefully so it didn't catch disclosed assistants or accessibility tools.
