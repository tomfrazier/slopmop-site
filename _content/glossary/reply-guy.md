## What a reply guy is

A reply guy is someone who replies to other people's posts far more than anyone asked them to, in a way that's annoying, condescending, or overfamiliar. The classic reply guy corrects things that weren't wrong, explains the poster's own field to them, or turns up under every post from the same account with a comment that adds nothing.

[Wikipedia notes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reply_guy) that reply guys are often men, often with few followers of their own, and tend to target celebrities, large accounts and women. That pattern is why the phenomenon has been described as an example of benevolent sexism: the replies may be meant as helpful or friendly, and still treat the poster as someone who needs help they didn't ask for.

## Where reply guy came from

The phrase started out neutral. Around 2012 a social media manager for the Applebee's restaurant chain became known as "Applebee's Reply Guy" for answering comments on its Facebook page. By 2015 the meaning had shifted towards someone whose replies came across as rude, intrusive or mansplaining.

Some platform features have been described as aimed partly at reply guys. In 2020 Twitter let users limit who could reply to their tweets, which Vox linked in part to cutting down unpleasant reply-guy interactions. In 2023 an experimental feature in Mastodon's Android app reminded people when they were replying to a stranger, or to a post more than three months old; TechCrunch described it as tackling "the scourge of the 'reply guys'".

## Examples

A scientist posts that her team's paper on soil bacteria has been published. Two invented replies:

:::slop
Actually, it's worth pointing out that correlation doesn't imply causation. Have you considered that soil composition varies by region? Just something to think about. I did a module on microbiology at uni so I know a bit about this.
:::
:::fine
Congratulations. Does the method work on clay soils, or only the sandy sites in the study? We have a clay-heavy field trial starting in spring.
:::

The first reply answers a question nobody asked, assumes the author missed something basic, and ends on the replier's credentials. The second has read the paper, asks a specific question, and says why it matters to the person asking.
