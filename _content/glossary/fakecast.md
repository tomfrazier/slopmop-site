## What a fakecast is

A fakecast is a podcast with no people in it. Feed an AI tool some documents, press a button, and two synthetic hosts discuss them for you: they laugh at each other's jokes, say "right, exactly" a lot, and act thrilled to learn things they were handed a minute ago.

The audio is often impressive. The catch is the pretending. A podcast is worth listening to because of who is talking: what they know, what they've seen, where they disagree. A fakecast performs all of that without having any of it. The hosts can summarise your sources, but they have nothing of their own to add, and the chummy banter exists to make you forget that.

## Where the word fakecast came from

The format went mainstream in September 2024, when Google added [Audio Overviews to NotebookLM](https://blog.google/technology/ai/notebooklm-audio-overviews/). Google's own announcement promised that "with one click, two AI hosts start up a lively 'deep dive' discussion based on your sources."

Ole Reissmann put the word to work in July 2025 in [Rise of the Fakecast](https://olereissmann.com/rise-of-the-fakecast-the-uncanny-valley-of-ai-generated-podcasts/). Given an hour, his newsletter and three articles it mentioned, he had AI tools script and voice a 14-minute episode. His verdict: impressive at first, then "just superfluous summaries", with the hosts twice discussing layoffs without knowing what they were talking about. "It's not a podcast—it's a fakecast," he wrote, and asked for synthetic voices that are pleasant to listen to but obviously artificial. We haven't found who said "fakecast" first.

At its far end, a fakecast is a small case of what Daniel Dennett called [counterfeit people](/glossary/counterfeit-people/): software built to pass as human company.

## Examples

Two show-note blurbs for the same episode:

:::slop
In this electrifying deep dive, our hosts unpack the fascinating world of municipal bin collection, exploring the surprising insights that will change how you think about waste forever! 🎧✨
:::
:::fine
AI-generated audio summary of the council's 2026 waste report: two synthetic voices, about 9 minutes. Read the full report at the link below.
:::

The first sells a conversation that never happened. The second tells you what you're getting, and that it's a machine.

Slop Mop doesn't listen to audio. It reads text: your feeds on LinkedIn, X, Reddit, Facebook, Substack and Medium, the articles and websites you read, and email in Gmail and Outlook.
