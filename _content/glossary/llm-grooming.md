## What it means

LLM grooming describes an influence tactic aimed at machines. Rather than persuading people directly, an operation publishes huge volumes of articles pushing its narratives, in the hope that search engines index them, AI companies scrape them into training data, and chatbots end up repeating the claims to users as if they were ordinary information.

The pages don't need human readers for this to work. Volume, repetition and being findable by crawlers are what count.

The term is new and its central claim is disputed. Researchers agree that propaganda sites exist at scale and that chatbots sometimes cite them; they disagree about whether that's the result of a deliberate strategy to corrupt AI, and about how often it happens.

## Where it came from

The nonprofit American Sunlight Project named it in a report published in early 2025 about what it called the "Pravda network", a set of websites and social media accounts that aggregate pro-Russia content (unrelated to the newspaper Pravda). Writing in the Bulletin of the Atomic Scientists, the report's authors estimated the network's publishing rate at "at least 3.6 million pro-Russia articles" a year, and argued that the sites' poor design, broken scrolling and lack of search suggested they were "not primarily intended for human consumption."

In March 2025 NewsGuard published an audit of 10 leading chatbots. It tested them on false narratives linked to the network and reported that, taken together, they repeated those narratives 33% of the time.

## The criticism

In October 2025 a research note in the Harvard Kennedy School Misinformation Review challenged the idea. Its authors, Alyukov and colleagues, ran their own small-scale audit and found chatbots producing false claims far less often, with references to Pravda sites rare and mostly in answers that debunked them. They argued that when such sources do appear, it's usually because of "data voids": topics with little credible coverage, where a chatbot has few better sources to draw on. They also criticised the earlier audit for not publishing its full methods.

On that reading, the problem is gaps in good information more than successful manipulation. Both sides agree that low-quality sources reaching chatbot answers is a real risk; the dispute is over cause and scale.

## How Slop Mop sees it

This sits outside what Slop Mop judges. It scores how a post is written, not whether its claims are true or where they came from, so it isn't a tool for spotting propaganda.
