## What vibe revenue means

Cambridge Dictionary defines vibe revenue as "money or funding that an AI company is given because people are excited about the potential of AI, rather than because the company's products are already successful." In practice it often looks like this: money comes in because a product is new and exciting, then gets presented as if it will keep coming in. Customers sign up to try the latest AI tool, pay for a month or two, and drift away. In the meantime that early burst is annualised and quoted on a pitch deck as annual recurring revenue (ARR).

The revenue may be real. The "recurring" part is the question. A company can have impressive sign-ups and growth for a few months and still have very few customers who would notice if the product disappeared.

## Where vibe revenue came from

The phrase came into use in early 2025. On 28 February 2025 the entrepreneur Greg Isenberg wrote a post about AI companies raising large rounds on what he called "curiosity revenue" rather than sustainable ARR. He said "vibe revenue" came from a message a reader sent him about his earlier tweet, and he adopted it. His list of warning signs: high initial conversion, impressive short-term growth, poor retention after 3 to 6 months, little expansion within accounts, and customers quick to switch when something newer appears.

On 28 September 2025 [Fortune reported](https://fortune.com/2025/09/28/how-is-arr-calculated-startups-venture/) on founders stretching how they calculate ARR, quoting an unnamed venture capitalist: "The problem is that so much of this is essentially vibe revenue." The article's point was that the revenue exists, but much of it isn't recurring.

Cambridge Dictionary's blog added it to its [new words for 26 January 2026](https://dictionaryblog.cambridge.org/2026/01/26/new-words-26-january-2026/), alongside [bragawatts](/glossary/bragawatts/), and Oren Etzioni and Daniel S. Weld included both in [their October 2026 GeekWire glossary of AI-era language](https://www.geekwire.com/2026/etzioni-on-ai-the-colorful-and-cranky-language-of-the-ai-era-from-slop-to-slap/).

The term is informal and has no accounting definition. It's a judgment about how long revenue will last, and using it doesn't imply anyone has broken accounting rules.

### The numbers people blur

| Term | What it actually is |
|---|---|
| Booked revenue | Money from signed deals or completed sales |
| Recurring revenue | Revenue from subscriptions or contracts that renew |
| ARR | Recurring revenue expressed as a yearly figure; methods vary |
| Run-rate | A recent period's revenue multiplied out to a year |
| Pipeline | Deals that might close, weighted or not |
| Projection | A forecast |

Vibe revenue usually lives in the gap between run-rate and recurring revenue, with some pipeline mixed in.

## Examples

Both are invented.

:::slop
We just hit $5M ARR in 90 days 🚀 The demand is insane. This is what product-market fit feels like. We're only getting started.
:::
:::fine
We passed $5M annualised run-rate in October, based on September's revenue. 38% of customers from our first cohort are still paying after 4 months, and we want that above 60% before we call it product-market fit.
:::

The first claim may even be true. It just doesn't say how ARR was calculated or whether anyone stays. The second gives the method and the retention figure, including the part that isn't good yet.
