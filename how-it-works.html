How Slop Mop judges a post

Click the mop on any post and you get a breakdown: a score, a verdict, the Slopprint and three blue bars. Here's what each part means, what moves it, and what it can't tell you.

Reading the breakdown

Every part of the details panel, top to bottom. Pick one of three posts to see how the panel changes: one that folds away, one that's borderline, and one that reads fine. Try voting on them.

  1. The score, out of 100. Higher means more slop-like. It's worked out after the three counter-signals have taken their share off, so it's the final number. It never arrives alone: the signs behind it are always listed.
  2. The verdict. Looks fine, Possibly slop or Likely slop, each with a sentence saying why. When the model isn't confident, the verdict is Not sure and the post is left alone. Where the lines fall depends on your sensitivity setting (Mild, Moderate or Aggressive).
  3. The range bar. Where this post sits between fine and likely. In Hide mode a line marks where posts start folding away.
  4. Your vote. Not sure, No, Maybe or Probably. Not sure is where every post starts. A vote overrides the model's score in your feed and dims it in the panel.
  5. The Slopprint. Nine tells of slop, one per spoke. The further out a point sits, the more of that tell the model found. All nine, with examples.
  6. In its favour. Three counter-signals: sounds like a person, useful to readers and reader response. More blue means more evidence the post is worth reading. They can only lower the score, never raise it.
  7. Community votes and checks today. How other Slop Mop readers voted on this post, and how many of your 250 daily checks are left.

Nine tells of slop

The model asks nine separate questions about the writing, one per spoke of the Slopprint. The shape tells you what kind of slop a post is, not just how much. Six tells come from Graphite's research on AI tells, which compared human and model writing. The last three are feed habits we added ourselves.

One spike isn't enough

Plenty of people have one over-the-top habit. When only one tell stands out, the score is cut. Slop usually shows up as several tells at once, and that's what gets flagged.

Unsure answers count for less

The model says how sure it is about each tell, and shaky answers carry less weight. That calibration is what it's designed for. If it isn't sure enough about the post overall, the verdict is Not sure and the post is left alone.

The weights are private

Some tells count for more than others. The exact weights on the hosted server aren't published, so nobody can write a post that games them.

Scroll-stopper dramatic hook, one-line paragraphs, a moral

slopI got rejected 47 times.
Then everything changed.
Here's what I learned 👇
fineWe rebuilt our onboarding flow in March. Trial-to-paid went from 9% to 14%, and most of that came from cutting one form field.

Engagement bait asking for comments, reposts and follows

slopComment “GUIDE” and I'll DM you the template. ♻️ Repost to help your network.
fineIf you've tried this with a team under ten people, I'd like to hear what happened. A genuine question barely registers. Formula asks and giveaways keyed to comments are what count.

Hype words marketing vocabulary

slopThis game-changing framework will unlock unprecedented growth and elevate your brand.
fineThe framework cut our review cycle from five days to two.

Empty praise evaluation with no detail behind it

slopAn incredibly meaningful, truly transformative experience. This matters more than ever.
fineThe workshop got three of our five teams shipping behind feature flags within a week.

No-catch promises all benefit, no cost

slopScale your output without sacrificing quality, without burning out, and without hiring.
fineIt saves us about ten hours a week. Setup took a month and we lost two integrations.

“Not X, but Y” defining things by what they aren't

slopLeadership isn't a title. It's a mindset. It's not about being right, it's about being curious.
fineThe best managers I've had asked more questions than they answered in one-to-ones.

Flowery prose flourish instead of the plain claim

slopGrowth is a delicate dance between vision and humility, a tapestry woven from quiet moments.
fineWe grew because two customers referred six more.

Stiff phrasing stock transitions and hedges

slopFurthermore, this approach may provide additional benefits. Ultimately, it can support long-term outcomes.
fineIt helped. Not as much as we hoped, but enough to keep.

Too-tidy story an anecdote that exists to prove the moral

slopA junior employee once asked me a question I'll never forget. That day, I realised leadership is about listening.
fineLast Tuesday our intern found a bug in the billing export that three of us had missed for a month. We now review exports in pairs.

counter-signal 1 of 3

Sounds like a person

Does this read like one particular person, or like a generic professional voice that anyone could have written? The model reads the text only.

What raises it

  • Specific personal detail: names, places, dates or numbers from the writer's own life or work
  • Word choices and asides that are clearly theirs
  • Uneven rhythm: long sentences, short ones, fragments
  • Contractions and casual qualifiers (“pretty”, “sort of”)

What doesn't count on its own

  • “I”, “my” or “we”
  • Naming the writer's company
  • “I believe”, “in my experience” and similar framing

Anyone writing on someone else's behalf uses those too, so they don't prove there's a real voice.

Short barAs leaders, we must embrace change. I believe the future belongs to those who adapt. It's in the first person, but anyone could have written it.
Long barSpent Monday rewriting our pricing page for the third time (don't ask). The version that finally worked was the ugly one my co-founder sketched on a train to Leeds. Details, an aside and a rhythm that only this person would produce.

What it does to the score

It takes a little off the slop score. It's a nudge, not an exemption: a very personal post stuffed with engagement bait can still be flagged.

This is not the same as “was it written by AI?” Slop Mop asks that separately, and the answer can only soften the score of a post that reads as typed by a person. It never flags a post by itself.

counter-signal 2 of 3

Useful to readers

Could someone doing the job learn something from this post, or act on it? Who or what wrote it doesn't matter. Useful AI-assisted writing scores just as well as useful writing typed by hand.

What counts

  • Specific facts and numbers
  • Named, concrete examples
  • First-hand experience, with the details
  • A method or step a reader could follow

What doesn't count on its own

  • An opinion or position, however well argued
  • A framing, a manifesto or a call for change
  • Citing a well-known source or disclaimer
  • “More on this in my next post”

A thoughtful opinion piece can be worth reading. It just isn't what this bar measures.

Short barAI is changing everything. Businesses that don't adapt will be left behind. True enough, but there's nothing a reader didn't already know.
Long barWe moved support triage to a two-question form. First response dropped from 11 hours to 3. The one thing it broke was refunds, so those now skip the form. Numbers, a trade-off and something you could copy tomorrow.

Useful isn't the same as popular

A post nobody has commented on yet can score high here, and a viral post can score low. Popularity has its own bar, reader response. Usefulness is half of the shield: a genuinely useful post has a large share of its slop score taken off. Never all of it, so a useful post buried in bait can still be flagged.

counter-signal 3 of 3

Reader response

What readers actually did with the post: reactions, comments and reposts. This one isn't the model's opinion. It's counted from the numbers shown under the post.

Effort counts more than taps

A comment costs someone minutes, and a repost puts the post in front of their whole network. Both count for far more than a one-tap reaction.

Hollow engagement is discounted

Hundreds of reactions with almost no comments or reposts is what an engagement pod or a cheap hook looks like. Once a post has lots of reactions, a thin comment-and-repost rate earns it less credit.

The first responses move it most

The scale is logarithmic. A post's first comments and reposts move the bar a lot; the thousandth reaction barely moves it.

What it does to the score

Reader response is the other half of the shield, alongside usefulness. Together they can take a large share off a post's slop score, but never all of it. Posts are checked again as they pick up engagement, so a new post starts with an empty bar and can fill it later. Your own posts and drafts leave this bar out entirely.

How it adds up

From twelve answers to one score, in order.

  1. Nine tells. The model's answers on the Slopprint are weighted and combined. A single tell standing out on its own is cut; shaky answers count for less.
  2. Sounds like a person. Takes a little off.
  3. The shield. Half usefulness, half reader response. Takes a share off the score, up to a cap, so no post is cleared by the shield alone.
  4. Human-typed? If the post reads as typed by a person, the score is softened a little. This step can only lower the score; being AI-assisted never adds to it.
  5. Your threshold. The final score is compared with your sensitivity. Looks fine is left alone. In Highlight mode, the post's mop turns yellow for Possibly slop and red for Likely slop. In Hide mode, posts past your threshold fold away into a strip you can reopen.

The score is the weight of the tells, shielded by what's useful and human.
