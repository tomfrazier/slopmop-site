What Slop Mop is

Slop Mop is a browser extension that reads the posts in your feed before you reach them and judges the writing. Posts that read like slop fold into a one-line strip you can reopen with a click, or stay in place with their mop icon tinted, depending on the mode you pick. Every verdict shows the signs behind it.

Slop is writing that takes your attention and gives little back: the post that praises without saying what was good, the hook with nothing behind it, the request to comment “YES” to get a framework. A person or an AI can write it. Slop Mop is a bad-writing detector, so it catches AI slop as part of catching all slop. AI writing can be good and human writing can be bad, and Slop Mop judges the content, never the person who wrote it or the person reading it.

Nothing is deleted, reported, muted or blocked. Slop Mop only changes what you see in your own browser.

Slop Mop mops the slop out of your feed, so you read only the posts worth your time.

How it works

  1. It reads ahead. As you scroll, Slop Mop sends the text of each new post to its scoring service. Your name and the author's are never sent.
  2. It makes calibrated decisions. Each post is checked for nine signs of slop: Scroll-stopper, Engagement bait, Hype words, Empty praise, No-catch promises, “Not X, but Y”, Flowery prose, Stiff phrasing and Too-tidy story. They are weighed against three counter-signals: sounds like a person, useful to readers, and reader response.
  3. It scores the post. A score out of 100 and a verdict: Likely slop, Possibly slop, Looks fine or Not sure. When Slop Mop isn't sure, it leaves the post alone.
  4. It acts on your settings. Hide folds likely slop into a strip that one click reopens. Highlight hides nothing and tints the post's mop icon. Sensitivity is Mild, Moderate or Aggressive.
  5. It shows its working. Click the mop on any post to see the score, the strongest signs and the Slopprint, a chart of all nine signs.
  6. You get the last word. The method is probabilistic, so it will sometimes be wrong. Vote Not sure, No, Maybe or Probably on any post, and your vote overrides Slop Mop in your own feed.

Post text is scored, not kept: the server stores a one-way hash of the text and the scores. The one exception is a post you vote against, whose text is kept so the miss can be reviewed. How it works, in detail.

Who it's for

  • People who read a lot online and are tired of scrolling past hooks, bait and filler to find the posts worth reading.
  • LinkedIn users on desktop today, and readers of X, Reddit, Facebook, Substack, Medium, the open web and email as those arrive.
  • People who want a calmer feed without leaving the platform, muting people or blocking anyone.
  • Anyone who wants to see why something was filtered, and to overrule it.

Why now

Generative AI made plausible writing nearly free, and feeds reward volume. People post more to be seen, tools write it for them, and the posts worth your attention sit between the hooks and the bait. Content is cheap now. Attention is the scarce thing.

Two things made a fix practical in 2026. In September, Graphite published its “AI Tells” study of 100,000 articles, which found about 13,000 words and phrases that turn up at least twice as often in AI writing; six of Slop Mop's nine signs start there, and the other three are feed habits. And a new kind of AI model makes calibrated decisions: it answers a fixed question and says how sure it is. That is what lets Slop Mop judge every post as you scroll, and leave a post alone when the answer is shaky.

The first State of Slop report put a number on the problem: one LinkedIn post in five was slop, after LinkedIn's own filtering had done its work.

Questions and answers

About Slop Mop

What is Slop Mop?

Slop Mop is a browser extension that reads the posts in your feed before you reach them, judges the writing and folds away what reads like slop, so you read only the posts worth your time. Nothing is deleted: a folded post reopens with one click, and every verdict shows the signs behind it. It works on LinkedIn today, in desktop Chrome and other Chromium browsers.

Is Slop Mop an AI detector?

No. Slop Mop is a bad-writing detector, which makes it a superset of AI slop detection rather than an AI detector. AI writing can be good and human writing can be bad, so a post is never folded just because it reads as AI-written. Slop Mop asks whether a post is worth your attention.

How does Slop Mop decide what is slop?

Slop Mop makes calibrated decisions on nine signs of slop: Scroll-stopper, Engagement bait, Hype words, Empty praise, No-catch promises, “Not X, but Y”, Flowery prose, Stiff phrasing and Too-tidy story. It weighs them against three counter-signals: whether a post sounds like a person, whether it is useful to readers, and how readers responded. Each post gets a score out of 100 and a verdict: Likely slop, Possibly slop, Looks fine or Not sure.

Can Slop Mop be wrong?

Yes. The method is probabilistic, so some verdicts will be wrong, and Slop Mop leaves a post alone when it isn't sure. That's why every reader can vote Not sure, No, Maybe or Probably on any post. A vote overrides Slop Mop in that reader's own feed.

Does Slop Mop judge the person who wrote a post?

No. Slop Mop judges the content only. It never scores the person who wrote a post or the person reading it, and it never reports, mutes, blocks or deletes anything. It only changes what you see in your own browser.

Is Slop Mop free?

Yes. Slop Mop is free for one feed, with 250 checks a day. Slop Mop XL, coming soon, covers every feed, the sites you read and your inbox, for US$5.99 a month or US$59.99 a year.

Which sites and browsers does Slop Mop work on?

LinkedIn is live today, on desktop Chrome and other Chromium browsers such as Brave, Opera and Comet. X, Reddit, Facebook, Substack, Medium, websites and email (Gmail and Outlook) are coming soon, as are Microsoft Edge, Safari and Firefox.

What happens to the posts Slop Mop reads?

Your name and the author's name are never sent. Post text is scored, not kept: the server stores a one-way hash of the text and the scores. The one exception is a post you vote against, whose text is kept so the miss can be reviewed.
