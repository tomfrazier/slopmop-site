The nine tells of slop

Slop Mop asks nine questions about every post, one per tell. Each page shows what the tell looks like, when it's fine, how it's scored and how to keep it out of your own writing.

01 · dramatic hook, one-line paragraphs, a moral

Scroll-stopper

A dramatic hook, a drumroll of one-line paragraphs and a moral at the end, all wrapped around one ordinary idea.

02 · asking for comments, reposts and follows

Engagement bait

Formula requests for comments, reposts and follows, added to a post to buy reach rather than start a conversation.

03 · marketing vocabulary

Hype words

Marketing vocabulary such as game-changing, unlock, elevate and seamless, used where a number or a plain verb would say more.

04 · evaluation with no detail behind it

Empty praise

Big verdicts like incredible, powerful and transformative, with no detail to show what the writer actually saw.

05 · all benefit, no cost

No-catch promises

Claims that list every benefit and leave out the price, the effort, the limits and the people it won't work for.

06 · defining things by what they aren't

“Not X, but Y”

Defining things by what they aren't, usually against a claim nobody made, so a slogan can stand in for an argument.

07 · flourish instead of the plain claim

Flowery prose

Tapestries, symphonies, dances and journeys used where a plain sentence would say what happened.

08 · stock transitions and hedges

Stiff phrasing

Stock transitions and hedges such as furthermore, moreover and may potentially, bolted onto sentences that don't need them.

09 · an anecdote that exists to prove the moral

Too-tidy story

An anecdote with no date, no names and no loose ends, which exists only to deliver the moral in the last line.
