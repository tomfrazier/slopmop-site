Hype words

Marketing vocabulary such as game-changing, unlock, elevate and seamless, used where a number or a plain verb would say more.

marketing vocabulary

Hype words are the vocabulary of a product launch applied to everything: a reorganisation, a hiring tip, a lunch. Game-changing, unlock, elevate, supercharge, seamless, cutting-edge, unprecedented. Each one claims something is big without saying how big, or what changed.

What hype words look like

slop

Thrilled to announce our cutting-edge, AI-powered platform that will revolutionise how teams collaborate and unlock unprecedented productivity.

fine

Our new release lets two people edit the same invoice at once. Before, the second person got a locked file and had to wait.

slop

This seamless onboarding experience empowers new hires to hit the ground running and elevates your employer brand.

fine

New starters now get their laptop, logins and a named buddy on day one. Last year it took an average of 4 days to get all three.

slop

Delve into the dynamic landscape of sustainable finance and leverage robust insights to supercharge your strategy.

fine

Green bonds made up a bigger share of our client portfolios this year. This note explains which ones we bought, which we turned down, and why.

They fall into a few groups:

  • Size without a measure: game-changing, unprecedented, massive, revolutionary.
  • Motion without a destination: unlock, elevate, supercharge, empower, accelerate, transform.
  • Quality without evidence: seamless, robust, cutting-edge, world-class, best-in-class.
  • Setting the scene: landscape, realm, journey, ecosystem, "in the world of".
  • Verbs for doing ordinary things grandly: delve, leverage, harness, navigate (when nobody is steering anything).

Why it wastes your time

A hype word asks you to take a claim on trust. "Unprecedented growth" could mean 3% or 300%; you can't tell, so you either believe it or skip it. Neither helps you.

The words also crowd out the useful sentence. A post that says "This tool will supercharge your workflow" has used its space on the adjective, and the fact (what the tool does, what it replaced, what it cost) never makes it onto the page.

They have become a sign that nobody looked closely. Some of these words have grown more common in writing produced with AI help. One analysis of more than 15 million PubMed abstracts, published in Science Advances in July 2025, found sudden jumps in style words such as "delves", "showcasing" and "underscores" after chatbots became widely available. Marketing copy used most of the rest long before that. Either way, a reader who meets "elevate" and "seamless" in the first line has learned to expect filler, and often stops reading.

When it's fine

Many hype words have plain, technical meanings, and in those meanings they're the right choice.

  • Robust in statistics describes a method that isn't thrown off by outliers. A "robust estimator" is a specific thing.
  • Leverage in finance means borrowing to increase exposure, and a leverage ratio is a number you can check.
  • Seamless describes tubes, garments and steel pipe made without a joint.
  • Landscape is a painting, a page orientation and a view across a valley.
  • Unlock is what you do to a phone or a door.
  • Journey is a train trip from York to Edinburgh.
  • Unprecedented is accurate when there's genuinely no precedent, and a good writer will usually say which record was broken.

Sales pages and adverts also use enthusiastic language openly, and readers know what they're looking at. The tell becomes a problem when that vocabulary turns up in a post presented as advice or news, with no figures behind it.

How Slop Mop scores hype words

Hype words is one of the nine questions the model asks about every post, and one spoke on the Slopprint. The further out the point sits, the more of the tell the model found, so one "robust" in a technical post should sit much nearer the centre than a paragraph built from launch language.

The model rates how sure it is, and unsure answers count for less. When hype words is the only tell that stands out, the score is cut: an enthusiastic founder announcing real news shouldn't be folded away for one excited adjective. Hype words often appear alongside empty praise and no-catch promises, and several tells together is what gets flagged.

Three counter-signals (sounds like a person, useful to readers and reader response) can only lower the score. A post with real numbers and a method readers can copy gets a share taken off, even if it says "game-changing" once. Some tells count for more than others, and the weights aren't published. Your sensitivity setting decides the verdict, and your vote overrides it in your feed.

How to fix it in your own writing

  1. Circle every adjective and verb that claims size or quality. Game-changing, seamless, unlock, elevate and the rest.
  2. Ask "how would I know?" For each one, write down the fact that made you reach for it: a number, a before-and-after, a customer's words.
  3. Replace the word with the fact. If there's no fact, the claim probably shouldn't be there.
  4. Use the plainest verb that's true. "Use" for leverage, "look at" for delve, "help" or "let" for empower.
  5. Read it aloud to a colleague. If you'd be embarrassed to say "unlock unprecedented value" across a desk, don't post it.
slop

Our game-changing new dashboard empowers finance teams to unlock real-time insights and seamlessly elevate decision-making.

fine

The new dashboard shows cash in the bank as of this morning, refreshed every hour. Finance teams used to wait for a weekly export. It doesn't yet include card transactions; those arrive in December.

Where hype words show up

Launch announcements on LinkedIn are dense with them, and so are sponsored posts on Facebook and X, product write-ups on Medium, and the "About" and pricing pages of the websites you read. They also fill press releases, job adverts, conference abstracts, newsletters and, increasingly, cover letters and sales email.
