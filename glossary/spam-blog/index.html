Spam blog

A blog with no writer and no readers.

also splog, auto blog, autoblog · since 2005 · about ai and human content

on this page What it means Where it came from Examples How Slop Mop sees it

What it means

A spam blog looks like a blog from a distance: a name, a list of posts, dates, perhaps an author photo. Up close, nobody is writing it. The posts are copied from other sites, run through a rewording tool, stitched together from search results or generated automatically, and they exist for one of two reasons.

The first is links. A network of spam blogs can point thousands of links at a site someone wants to rank, to fool search engines into treating it as popular. The second is ads: every page that collects a stray visit from search earns a fraction of a penny, and a script can publish thousands of pages a day.

"Splog" is the short form. "Autoblog" describes the automated version, where software pulls in feeds and republishes them with no human involved.

Where it came from

Spam blogs became a visible problem as free blogging platforms and blog search engines grew in the mid-2000s. The word "splog" was popularised around August 2005, when the entrepreneur Mark Cuban, whose company ran the blog search engine IceRocket, used it publicly while complaining about the volume of fake blogs clogging its results.

The tactics have changed with the tools. Early splogs relied on copying and keyword stuffing; later ones used article spinning to disguise the copying; generative AI now produces passable text in bulk, which makes a fake blog harder to tell from a thin real one.

Examples

A post from an invented autoblog about running shoes:

slop

Best Running Shoes 2026 Best Running Shoes Reviews. Running shoes are shoes that are used for running. When choosing the best running shoes 2026, it is important to consider many factors such as comfort, price and running shoes quality. Click here to see the best running shoes deals today!

fine

I've run about 300 miles in these since March. The foam is still springy, but the outsole has worn through at the heel, which my last pair didn't do until 500. If you land hard on your heels, they probably won't last you a year.

The first is written for a search crawler: the keyword repeated, a definition nobody needed, and a link to click. The second could only come from someone who ran in the shoes.

How Slop Mop sees it

Spam blogs live on the open web, and Slop Mop is live only on LinkedIn today, with websites coming soon. The text inside them shares the habits it scores, filler, stiff phrasing and hype words, and none of the specific detail that earns a post credit for being useful to readers.

Glossary

Scraper site

A website that copies other sites' content, sometimes lightly reworded or run through AI, and republishes it to earn ad money.

Glossary

Article spinning

Rewriting an existing article with synonyms and shuffled phrasing so it passes as new, usually to game search rankings or hide plagiarism.

Glossary

Keyword stuffing

Cramming a page with repeated search terms, lists of place names or hidden text so it ranks, at the cost of readability.

Glossary

Made for AdSense

A website with no real value of its own, built only to draw visitors in and get them to click on ads.

Glossary

Content farm

A company or site that pumps out huge volumes of cheap articles or videos for search and ad revenue rather than for readers.
