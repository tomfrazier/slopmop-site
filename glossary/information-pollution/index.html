Information pollution

Litter, but for your attention.

also info pollution, infollution · since 2003 · about ai and human content

on this page What it means Where it came from Examples How Slop Mop sees it

What it means

Information pollution is the idea that the information we receive can be contaminated, the way air or water can. Irrelevant detail, repeated messages, unwanted notices, warnings nobody needs and filler all mix in with the material that matters, and the reader pays for sorting it out.

The comparison with industrial pollution is deliberate. Each bit of waste looks harmless on its own: one more line in an email, one more pop-up, one more sign. The damage is cumulative. People learn to skim and ignore, and then they miss the one message that was important.

It overlaps with information overload, which describes having too much to take in. Information pollution puts the emphasis on quality: some of what's arriving shouldn't have been sent at all.

Where it came from

The term gained traction in 2003 through the usability expert Jakob Nielsen. In an article dated 10 August 2003, he argued that "Excessive word count and worthless details are making it harder for people to extract useful information," and that "Saying less often communicates more." His opening example was a hotel lobby sign near JFK Airport that cycled through four messages to explain that the monitor beneath it showed flight times.

He described the effect on readers in terms of information foraging: polluting a page with useless material "is like packing the forest with cardboard rabbits: frustrated wolves are bound to hunt elsewhere."

A related coinage, "infollution", is credited to Paek-Jae Cho, who used it in a 2002 speech.

Examples

A building notice, two ways:

slop

Dear Valued Residents, We hope this message finds you well. As part of our ongoing commitment to maintaining the highest standards of service and ensuring the continued comfort and safety of all our residents, we would like to take this opportunity to inform you that there may be some temporary disruption to certain utility services in the near future.

fine

The water will be off on Thursday 6 November from 9am to 1pm while the main valve is replaced. Fill a kettle the night before.

The first notice runs to nearly 60 words and hasn't said what, when or for how long. The second says all three in 2 sentences.

How Slop Mop sees it

Information pollution is close to what Slop Mop was built for: text that takes attention and gives little back. The filler in the first notice is the kind of stiff phrasing it scores. It works on LinkedIn today; Slop Mop for Gmail and Outlook, where a lot of this lands, is coming soon.
