Shrimp Jesus

The patron saint of Facebook AI slop.

since 2024 · about ai content

What Shrimp Jesus means

Shrimp Jesus is the name people gave to a run of AI-generated images on Facebook that showed Jesus Christ built out of shrimp, or rising from the sea surrounded by them. The pictures were surreal, plainly machine-made and, for a while, everywhere. They collected thousands of reactions and comments, many of them a sincere "Amen".

The name now stands for something larger: the flood of cheap, strange, mass-produced AI images that pages post to farm engagement. In their October 2026 glossary of AI-era language for GeekWire, Oren Etzioni and Daniel Weld called Shrimp Jesus the specimen of AI slop: "Christ fused with crustaceans, posted to Facebook by the thousands."

Where Shrimp Jesus came from

According to Know Your Meme's account of the Facebook AI posts epidemic, AI-generated images flooded Facebook in late 2023 and early 2024, after text-to-image tools became widely available. Researchers and journalists identified hundreds of accounts posting them. The accounts tended to post dozens of times a day, were often linked to spam or scam websites, and favoured a few subjects: Jesus, shrimp, flight attendants, children and sea creatures.

404 Media catalogued a number of these accounts on 18 December 2023. A Stanford Internet Observatory study, in preprint in March 2024, argued that the accounts bought bot followers and sent people to content farms and low-quality sites. Its authors suggested that Facebook's push to show "unconnected content" from pages people didn't follow had helped them spread. On X, a 13 March 2024 post expressing alarm about the "Shrimp Jesus" images earned more than 57,000 likes in two weeks, one of many posts that spread the name.

Money explains much of the volume. In August 2024, Jason Koebler of 404 Media reported on creators, many in developing countries, who make images aimed at US audiences because those audiences attract higher advertising rates.

Examples

An invented post of the kind that made the name:

example

An image of a glowing figure in robes, his body made of pink shrimp, rising from a turquoise sea. Caption: "Why don't pictures like this ever trend? 🙏" Underneath, 9,000 reactions and a long column of comments reading "Amen".

The caption is often where the trick is. Two invented captions for a picture posted to a Facebook page:

slop

Made this with my own hands 🙏 Nobody liked it. Type AMEN if you think it's beautiful.

fine

Generated with an image tool for our church's Easter flyer. We went with the third version; the first two had six fingers.

The first claims handmade work and asks for a formula comment. The second says how it was made and why.
