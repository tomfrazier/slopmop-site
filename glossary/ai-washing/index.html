AI washing

Greenwashing, but the leaves are a chatbot.

since 2019 · about ai content

What AI washing means

AI washing is marketing that makes a product sound more AI-driven than it is. A spreadsheet macro becomes an "intelligent engine". A rules-based filter becomes "powered by machine learning". Sometimes there's no AI in the product at all, only the label.

The aim is the same as any inflated claim: win customers who want the latest thing, and help secure funding from investors. The cost lands on the buyer, who pays for a capability that isn't there, and on honest competitors, whose real work gets drowned out by louder claims.

The term has also stretched. In 2026 The Washington Post used it for bosses who blame layoffs on AI when the cuts may have more to do with ordinary cost-cutting.

Where the term AI washing came from

The name is modelled on greenwashing, where companies overstate their environmental credentials. According to Wikipedia, the AI Now Institute, a research institute at New York University, first defined AI washing in 2019. It has been compared to the dot-com bubble, when companies added ".com" to their names to lift their valuations.

Regulators have started to act on it. In March 2024 the US Securities and Exchange Commission imposed its first civil penalties on two companies for misleading statements about their use of AI, and in July 2024 it charged an executive at a supposed AI hiring startup with fraud. One consumer example Wikipedia cites is Coca-Cola's Y3000 drink from September 2023, said to be "co-created with human and artificial intelligence", which drew accusations of AI washing because there was no evidence of what, if anything, the AI had contributed.

Examples

Two invented descriptions of the same invoicing tool:

slop

Meet InvoiceIQ, the AI-powered finance platform that revolutionises how you get paid. Our intelligent engine learns your business and unlocks effortless cash flow.

fine

InvoiceIQ sends a reminder 3 days before an invoice is due and again 7 days after. You set the wording. It doesn't use AI; it runs on the dates you enter.

The first one makes an AI claim with nothing to check. The second says what the tool does, when, and what it doesn't do. A buyer can test every sentence.

A quick test for any "AI-powered" claim:

  1. What decision does the AI make that a fixed rule couldn't?
  2. What happens when it's wrong?
  3. Would the product description still make sense with the word "AI" deleted?

How Slop Mop sees it

Slop Mop can't inspect a product to see whether the AI is real. What it can see is the language AI washing usually comes wrapped in: hype words and no-catch promises with no cost or limit attached. Those are two of the tells it scores in posts on LinkedIn, X, Reddit, Facebook, Substack and Medium, on the product pages and articles you read, and in sales emails in Gmail and Outlook. AI snake oil

AI products sold on claims they don't and can't live up to, a term popularised by Princeton computer scientist Arvind Narayanan.

Vibe revenue

Money or funding an AI company gets from excitement about AI rather than proven success, often presented as durable recurring income.

Bragawatts

AI companies boasting about huge projects to attract excitement and investment, though many never get built.

The nine tells

Hype words

Marketing vocabulary such as game-changing, unlock, elevate and seamless, used where a number or a plain verb would say more.

The nine tells

No-catch promises

Claims that list every benefit and leave out the price, the effort, the limits and the people it won't work for.
