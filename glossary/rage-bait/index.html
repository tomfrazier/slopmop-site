Rage bait

Anger, farmed for engagement.

also ragebait, rage-baiting, rage farming · since 2002 · about ai and human content

on this page What rage bait means Where rage bait came from Examples How Slop Mop sees it

What rage bait means

Rage bait is content made to annoy, offend or outrage, because anger gets a response. An angry reader comments, quote-posts and shares to complain, and every one of those actions tells the platform the post is engaging. The poster collects the reach whether people agreed or not.

The bait can be an outrageous opinion, a deliberately wrong "fact", a staged video or a carefully cropped screenshot. What these share is intent: the anger is the point, and the topic is chosen because it reliably produces it.

Rage bait is related to clickbait, which trades on curiosity, and to the hot take, which is often provocative too, but a hot take can be sincere. Rage bait is built to provoke whether or not the writer believes it.

Where rage bait came from

Oxford University Press traces the phrase to a 2002 Usenet post, where it described one driver's reaction to being passed by another. It later became internet slang for content that provokes anger to drive traffic or engagement.

In December 2025 Oxford named rage bait its Word of the Year, after a public vote and a review of its language data, choosing it over "aura farming" and "biohack". It said usage of the term had tripled in the previous 12 months, and linked the choice to a year of debate about online anger, regulating online content and digital wellbeing.

Examples

Two posts on the same subject, both invented.

slop

Unpopular opinion: if you need a lunch break, you don't actually want to succeed.

I haven't taken one in 6 years. Winners eat at their desks.

Tell me I'm wrong 👇

fine

I used to skip lunch to get more done. Tracking my hours for a month in 2025 showed the afternoons I skipped it were my least productive. I now take 30 minutes away from my desk, and my output after 3pm is noticeably better.

The first post is designed to make most readers angry and to invite them to say so. The second makes a claim, gives the evidence behind it and doesn't ask for anything.

How Slop Mop sees it
