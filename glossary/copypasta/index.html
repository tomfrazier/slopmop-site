Copypasta

The internet's oldest reply: paste.

since 2006 · about human content

What a copypasta is

A copypasta is a block of text that people copy and paste from one place to another, over and over, until it becomes a running joke. The same paragraph turns up in comment threads, chat groups and forums, sometimes years apart. Regulars recognise it at once; newcomers often take it at face value and reply in earnest, which is part of the fun for whoever posted it.

Most copypastas are funny because of a mismatch: a furious rant in reply to something trivial, a mock-serious boast, a passage from a film script dropped into an unrelated discussion. Some are pasted to provoke, and a few have been used to push ideas or attack real people.

In programming the word has a second, plainer meaning: code that was copied from one place to another, often with the mistakes that come from not adapting it.

Where the word copypasta came from

The word comes from "copy and paste". Wikipedia traces its use to an anonymous 4chan thread in 2006, and Merriam-Webster records it appearing on Usenet and Urban Dictionary the same year.

Some copypastas have lasted for well over a decade. The "Navy Seal" text, a threatening boast from someone claiming absurd military credentials, is still pasted as an over-the-top reply to a mild insult. The whole script of the 2007 film Bee Movie became one in 2013, when people began posting it to Reddit and Tumblr.

The joke can turn serious. Wikipedia notes a 2024 case in the Philippines in which an actor filed a cyberlibel complaint against a creator who had posted a well-known copypasta about a celebrity behaving badly in a shop, with the actor's name swapped in.

AI has added a new strain. "Sloppypasta" is used for chatbot output pasted at someone without being read, which on this site we file under AI slap.

Examples

These are invented in the style of the form.

example

In a forum thread about which bin the pizza boxes go in, someone replies with four paragraphs of mock-legal outrage about their "constitutional right to recycle", then signs off with a list of imaginary qualifications. It's the 30th time that exact text has appeared on the forum.

example

In a group chat, every time someone says "quick question", a friend pastes the same 200-word monologue about how no question has ever been quick.

slop

Hi everyone, I do NOT give LinkedIn permission to use my photos, posts or data. Copy and paste this into your own post to protect yourself. Liking it is not enough!

The last one is the kind of copypasta that does harm. It's a chain letter with a legal-sounding claim that achieves nothing, and it asks every reader to repeat it.

How Slop Mop sees it

Most copypasta is an in-joke, and an in-joke is outside what Slop Mop judges. The chain-letter kind is a different matter: "copy and paste this" and "share to protect yourself" are engagement bait, one of the nine tells it scores. Chain posts like that circulate on Facebook, LinkedIn and X alike, and Slop Mop reads posts on all three, as well as Reddit, Substack and Medium. How it works lists the other tells.
