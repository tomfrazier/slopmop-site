Hustle porn

Bragging about the sleep you didn't get.

since 2010s · about human content

on this page What hustle porn means Where the term hustle porn came from Examples How Slop Mop sees it

What hustle porn means

Hustle porn is content that sells overwork as a virtue. The 4am alarm, the 100-hour week, the missed birthdays, the "while you were sleeping, I was building" caption: all presented as proof of commitment and the price of success. It's usually aimed at founders, freelancers and anyone with a side project, and it often ends by selling something, such as a course, a coaching programme or a newsletter.

The "porn" in the name is about the spectacle. Like other "-porn" coinages, such as outrage porn, it describes content made to be consumed for a feeling, here the feeling of ambition, more than for anything a reader could use. The hours are the point of the post; what they produced is often missing.

The usual complaint about hustle porn is that it makes exhaustion look like a strategy, hides the luck and money behind most success stories, and makes anyone who keeps normal hours feel they're failing.

Where the term hustle porn came from

The phrase circulated in startup and tech circles in the 2010s. It got wide attention in May 2019, when Reddit co-founder Alexis Ohanian criticised what he called "hustle porn" at The Wall Street Journal's Future of Everything Festival. Fox Business reported that he described it as people boasting about how many hours they work, and quoted him: "Y'all see it on Instagram and you certainly see it in the startup community, and it becomes really toxic." Ohanian said his view had changed after taking paternity leave in 2017.

Ohanian popularised the phrase; we haven't found a reliable source saying he coined it.

Examples

Both invented.

slop

Woke up at 3:45am.

Gym. Cold plunge. Two hours of deep work before my team was even awake.

I haven't taken a weekend off in 4 years.

My friends call it obsession.

I call it Tuesday.

Rest is for people who've already made it. 💯

fine

I worked 70-hour weeks for most of our first year. Some of it was necessary: we were three people and a launch date. A lot of it wasn't. When I dropped to 50 hours in year two, our release rate didn't change, and I stopped making the kind of pricing mistakes that cost us two customers in month eight.

The first post is all hours and no output. It also has a scroll-stopper layout and a moral at the end. The second gives the hours, says what they were for, admits what they cost, and reports what happened when they changed.

slop

Your 9-to-5 is someone else's dream. Quit it, grind for 18 months, and you'll never work for anyone again.

That one is a no-catch promise: all upside, with the risk, savings and luck left out.

How Slop Mop sees it

Slop Mop doesn't take a view on how many hours anyone should work. What it scores, on LinkedIn, X, Facebook and the other feeds it reads, are the habits hustle porn tends to arrive with: the drumroll hook, the too-tidy story, the promise with no cost attached. A frank post about long hours, with the numbers and the trade-offs, can read as perfectly fine.
