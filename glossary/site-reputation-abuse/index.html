What site reputation abuse means

Site reputation abuse is renting out a trusted website's good name in search. A news site, university or established brand has built up years of ranking signals. A third party pays, or partners, to put its own pages on that domain, usually in a subfolder or subdomain, and those pages rank far higher than they would on a site of their own.

The pages typically have nothing to do with the host's purpose: coupon codes on a newspaper, payday loan comparisons on a charity site, casino reviews on a university domain. The host gets a fee; the third party gets traffic; the reader gets a page whose main credential is the address it sits on.

SEO practitioners have long called the tactic parasite SEO, since the content feeds on a host it doesn't belong to.

Where site reputation abuse came from

Google named site reputation abuse as a spam policy on 5 March 2024, alongside its March 2024 core update, and said it would take effect from 5 May 2024 to give site owners time to prepare.

Its definition: "when third-party pages are published with little or no first-party oversight or involvement, where the purpose is to manipulate Search rankings by taking advantage of the first-party site's ranking signals." Google said the policy doesn't treat all third-party content as a violation. Advertorials and native advertising aimed at a publication's regular readers, of a kind that typically wouldn't confuse them, were given as examples of content that is acceptable.

Examples

example

A regional newspaper's site has a section at /deals/ that it doesn't edit. A marketing firm fills it with hundreds of pages like "Best online casinos 2026" and "Top 10 CBD gummies", none written by the paper's journalists. They rank well because of the newspaper's domain.

example

A university's website hosts a sponsored "student resources" subdomain that turns out to be essay-writing service reviews with affiliate links.

example

Not abuse under Google's description: a food magazine runs a sponsored recipe series with a kitchenware brand, edited by its own staff, labelled as sponsored and written for its usual readers.

The first two borrow a reputation they didn't earn. The third is ordinary advertising the host stands behind.

Doorway page

A page made only to rank for one search query, which then funnels the visitor somewhere else.

Scaled content abuse

Google's term for producing many pages, by AI, scraping or cheap writers, mainly to manipulate search rankings and not to help readers.

Glossary

Content farm

A company or site that pumps out huge volumes of cheap articles or videos for search and ad revenue rather than for readers.

Made for AdSense

A website with no real value of its own, built only to draw visitors in and get them to click on ads.

Cory Doctorow's term for platforms that serve users, then advertisers and sellers, then only themselves, getting worse at each step.
