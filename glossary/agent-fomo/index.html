Agent FOMO

Fear your agents are accomplishing nothing without you.

since 2026 · about ai content

on this page What agent FOMO means Where it came from Examples How Slop Mop sees agent FOMO

What agent FOMO means

Agent FOMO is the itch to check on your AI agents. You set a coding agent or research agent off on a task, walk away, and then keep wondering whether it has stalled waiting for permission, gone down the wrong path, or finished an hour ago and is sitting idle.

It flips the usual fear of missing out. The worry is that your software is missing out on work because you aren't there to approve the next step.

Most of it comes down to design. Many agents pause before steps that could cause damage, such as deleting files, spending money or sending a message, and wait for a person to say yes. That's sensible, but it means an agent's progress depends on how often someone looks at it. The more agents you run at once, the more places there are for one to be waiting.

It's a joke about modern work habits, and it isn't a measured psychological condition. If the checking starts to feel like anxiety, treat it like any other work stress.

Where it came from

The Wall Street Journal used "agent FOMO" in its reporting on AI agents and start-up work culture. Oren Etzioni and Daniel S. Weld included it in their October 2026 GeekWire glossary of AI-era language, as the Journal's name for the condition they dub agentsomnia: being unable to rest or switch off because your agents might be "stalled without your feedback".

The feeling has been named elsewhere too. At AI Engineer Europe 2026, Michael Richman, an engineering leader at Bitly, gave a talk on "FOMAT", fear of missing agent time: the problem that an agent can work on its own for a while, then sit waiting for a decision, clarification or permission that nobody is around to give.

The feeling grows with every tool that lets agents run for long stretches unattended, from vibe coding assistants to research agents, and with the apps built to let people check on them from their phones. Those apps tend to make the checking easier, which can make the habit stronger.

Examples

example

A developer leaves a coding agent refactoring a test suite and goes to lunch. She checks her phone four times during the meal. On the fourth check the agent has been waiting 40 minutes for her to approve a single command.

example

A manager says in a stand-up, "I kicked off three agents before this meeting and I've got Agent FOMO." Everyone knows what he means.

example

A sensible response: setting the agent to send a notification only when it is blocked or done, and deciding in advance which steps it may take without asking.
