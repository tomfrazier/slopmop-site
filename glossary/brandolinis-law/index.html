Brandolini’s law

Ten minutes to make it, three days to debunk it.

What Brandolini’s law means

Brandolini’s law, also called the bullshit asymmetry principle, is an internet adage about effort. In its usual form:

The amount of energy needed to refute bullshit is an order of magnitude bigger than that needed to produce it.

Making a false or empty claim is quick. Checking it means finding the original source, reading it, asking someone who knows, and writing a correction clear enough that people will read it. By the time that's done, the claim has travelled.

"Order of magnitude" means roughly ten times. The figure isn't a measurement. The adage is a rule of thumb, and its use is in naming the imbalance.

Where Brandolini’s law came from

Alberto Brandolini, an Italian programmer, formulated it publicly in January 2013. He said he was inspired by reading Daniel Kahneman's Thinking, Fast and Slow shortly before watching an Italian political talk show featuring former Prime Minister Silvio Berlusconi and the journalist Marco Travaglio.

The law is often cited in discussions of misinformation. A standard example is the false claim that the MMR vaccine causes autism: the 1998 paper behind it was retracted and its lead author lost his medical licence, yet decades of research and public education haven't made the claim go away. During the COVID-19 pandemic, the Radio-Canada disinformation journalist Jeff Yates described fact-checking a YouTube video full of medical claims: "It took this guy 15 minutes to make his video and it took me three days to fact-check."

It has picked up new relevance with generative AI. When producing plausible text costs almost nothing, and AI slop fills the gaps, the side doing the checking falls further behind.

Examples

example

A post claims a new law bans home printers from copying passports. Writing it took a minute. Checking it means finding the actual bill, reading the relevant clause, and confirming it doesn't say that. The correction gets a fraction of the reach.

The same imbalance shows up at work. An invented status report:

slop

Following extensive analysis, our findings indicate that customer satisfaction has improved significantly across key segments, driven by multiple strategic initiatives.

fine

Satisfaction scores rose from 7.1 to 7.6 between the March and June surveys. We can't tie it to any one change; the new returns policy launched in the same period.

The first takes seconds to write and an afternoon to verify, because it names no figure, period or cause. The second can be checked in a minute.
