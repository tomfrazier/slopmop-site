What liquid content means

Liquid content is the idea that a piece of writing no longer has to stay in one shape. With generative AI, the same article can be poured into a 30-second summary, a podcast, a quiz, a LinkedIn carousel or a version for a ten-year-old, depending on who's reading and how much time they have. The article works more like a tap you pour from.

For readers that can be handy. You get the report as a two-minute listen on the bus. For publishers it's an open question with a scary edge: if the reader only ever meets the reshaped version, what exactly are they paying for?

Where the term liquid content came from

The phrase circulates among people who build media and AI products. In March 2025 Farsight, the magazine of the Copenhagen Institute for Futures Studies, ran The Anything-to-Everything Future Is Here, which says AI "is enabling a world of 'liquid content'", where stories and images move freely between formats. The piece is built around an interview with Google DeepMind's Matthieu Lorrain and asks whether the notion of a "finished" piece will dissolve. Lorrain's answer, as Farsight reports it, is that audiences still see a finished state.

The tools already exist in small ways. Text-to-speech reads articles aloud, chatbots summarise them, and Google's NotebookLM turns your documents into a two-host audio show, which is where the fakecast comes from. We haven't found who used "liquid content" first.

The catch with liquid content

Most pours lose something. Nuance, caveats, the reporter's careful "probably", the one sentence that changes the meaning: these are the first things to go when a machine squeezes 3,000 words into a quote card. The result often reads like flowery prose with the facts boiled off. And when anyone can reshape anything instantly, you get more of everything, which feeds content shock.

There's an older cousin too. Article spinning reworded one article into many to game search engines. Liquid content can be the honest version of that, or the same trick with better tools.

Liquid content: common questions

What does liquid content mean?

The idea that AI can pour one piece of writing into any shape: a summary, a podcast, a quiz, a thread, whatever you want.

Is liquid content a good thing?

It depends on what survives the pour. A long report turned into a faithful five-minute summary is useful. A report turned into a chirpy carousel that drops the caveats is slop with a nicer font.

Who uses the term liquid content?

Mostly people in media, publishing and AI product work, talking about stories that adapt to each reader's time, device and format.

Where does the term liquid content come from?

The phrase circulates among people who build media and AI products. In March 2025 Farsight, the magazine of the Copenhagen Institute for Futures Studies, ran The Anything-to-Everything Future Is Here, which says AI "is enabling a world of 'liquid content'", where stories and images move freely between formats.

Fakecast

An AI-generated podcast in which synthetic hosts banter about your documents as if they were real people having a real chat.

Glossary

Content shock

The point where the amount of content being produced outstrips people's limited capacity to consume it.

Glossary

Article spinning

Rewriting an existing article with synonyms and shuffled phrasing so it passes as new, usually to game search rankings or hide plagiarism.

Glossary

AI slop

Low-quality content made in bulk, usually with generative AI. Merriam-Webster's word of the year for 2025.

The nine tells

Flowery prose

Tapestries, symphonies, dances and journeys used where a plain sentence would say what happened.
