What word salad means

Word salad is language that has the shape of sense without the sense. The words are real, the grammar may be fine, and at the end you can't say what was claimed.

The phrase has two lives. In psychiatry and neurology it is a clinical description of speech in which words come out in an incoherent sequence, a symptom seen in some people with schizophrenia, dementia or brain injury. In everyday use it is a put-down for an answer that dodges the question with a stream of abstract words, the sort heard from a nervous interviewee or a spokesperson with nothing to announce.

This page is about the everyday sense. The clinical sense describes a symptom that people don't choose, and it's no joke.

Where word salad came from

Merriam-Webster dates the first known use of the clinical sense to 1904. Its definition: "unintelligible, extremely disorganized speech or writing manifested as a symptom of a mental disorder (such as schizophrenia)". Linguists also use the term for strings of words that native speakers judge to be ungrammatical.

In everyday speech the term became a put-down for empty, incoherent answers, and it is now often applied to AI text that strings fluent phrases together without saying anything in particular. Chatbot output is rarely word salad in the clinical sense. Its sentences are grammatical and on topic. They just often don't commit to anything.

Examples

Two invented replies to "Why did the product launch slip by a month?":

slop

The timeline shift reflects a holistic recalibration of our go-to-market priorities, ensuring alignment across key workstreams as we continue to optimise for long-term value creation and stakeholder outcomes in a dynamic environment.

fine

The payments provider found a security issue in our checkout two weeks before launch. Fixing and retesting it takes about four weeks, so the new date is 3 March.

The first could explain any delay at any company, or none. Every phrase is abstract, and none of them answers "why". The second names the cause, the size of the delay and the new date.

A quick test for word salad: try to restate the passage in one plain sentence. If what comes out is "something changed and we're handling it", the original said nothing more.
