Parasocial

One-sided emotional relationships.

also para-social, parasocial relationship, parasocial interaction · since 1956 · about ai and human content

on this page What parasocial means Where parasocial came from Examples How Slop Mop sees it

What parasocial means

A parasocial relationship is a one-sided bond. You feel you know someone, a podcaster, a streamer, a novelist, a TV presenter, and they don't know you exist. You follow their life, take their side, feel let down when they change. All of the relationship happens on your end.

Cambridge Dictionary defines parasocial as "involving or relating to a connection that someone feels between themselves and a famous person they do not know, a character in a book, film, TV series, etc, or an artificial intelligence". That last part is new. A chatbot that remembers your name and asks how your week went can produce the same feeling, with the difference that it replies.

Parasocial feelings are common and usually harmless. Most people have a presenter, writer or character they feel fond of. The term describes a kind of relationship, and it isn't a diagnosis or a judgment. It becomes a concern when the one-sided bond starts crowding out two-sided ones or shaping big decisions, and for chatbots that question overlaps with AI addiction.

Where parasocial came from

The term dates to 1956, when the University of Chicago sociologists Donald Horton and Richard Wohl described television viewers forming "para-social" relationships with on-screen personalities. They noticed that TV brought faces into people's homes every week, and that viewers responded to presenters much as they would to friends or family.

For decades it stayed mostly an academic term. Social media, influencers and streaming moved it into everyday speech. In November 2025 Cambridge Dictionary named "parasocial" its Word of the Year, pointing to interest in the one-sided relationships people form with celebrities, influencers and AI chatbots. Oren Etzioni and Daniel S. Weld included it in their October 2026 GeekWire glossary of AI-era language, noting that "a seventy-year-old term about Ed Sullivan turns out to describe your relationship with ChatGPT."

Examples

These are invented.

example

A listener has heard a podcast host's voice for three hours a week for six years. When the host announces the show is ending, she feels something close to grief, though they've never spoken.

example

A creator's post reads: "Honestly, you all feel like family. I read every single comment." It goes to 400,000 followers. The warmth may be sincere, and it's also written to make each reader feel personally addressed.

example

Someone talks to a companion chatbot every evening. It recalls details from earlier chats and says it missed them. The bond feels mutual, and only one side of it has feelings.
