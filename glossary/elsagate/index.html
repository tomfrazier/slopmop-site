Elsagate

Nursery rhymes reverse-engineered from search terms.

since 2017 · about ai and human content

What Elsagate was

Elsagate is the name for a controversy over videos on YouTube and YouTube Kids that were presented as suitable for children but contained violent, sexual, scatological or frightening scenes. Many used popular characters, such as Elsa from Frozen, Spider-Man and Peppa Pig, without permission. The wider controversy also took in channels featuring real children, which raised concerns about possible abuse.

The videos were built for search and recommendation. Titles and descriptions piled up character names alongside phrases like "learn colors", "education" and "nursery rhymes", a form of keyword stuffing, and some were tagged in ways that got past YouTube's child-safety systems. They carried automatically placed adverts, which made them profitable for their makers and for the platform. At YouTube's scale they were hard to moderate.

Where the name Elsagate came from

The name combines Elsa with the "-gate" suffix used for scandals. Wikipedia says such channels existed from 2014, and press coverage built through 2016 and early 2017, including a BBC piece in March 2017 about disturbing videos "tricking children". The term itself was coined online in 2017; that summer it spread as a hashtag on Twitter, and a subreddit about it drew tens of thousands of users.

Public attention peaked in November 2017. The New York Times reported on 4 November on videos slipping past YouTube Kids' filters, and on 6 November the author James Bridle published an essay on Medium about videos made to scare and exploit children, noting how parodies, imitations and automated content had blended into one another. Later that month YouTube tightened its guidelines and removed channels, videos and comments. Wikipedia notes that similar videos, now often built around children's video games, were still being found on the platform in the 2020s.
