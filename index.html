Many posts too little worth reading

Generative AI made plausible writing nearly free, and feeds reward volume. People post more to be seen, tools write it for them, and the few posts worth your attention sit between the hooks, the bait and the tidy little parables.

  • 01AI made it cheap. A post that took an hour now takes a prompt.
  • 02Humans make it too. Broetry and engagement bait were here long before the models.
  • 03Feeds reward it. Reactions are easy to farm, so the formula spreads.
  • 04You pay in attention. Every post costs a few seconds to decide it wasn't worth it.

So Slop Mop reads ahead and does the deciding. Here's the kit it uses.

the nine tells

Nine tells of slop

The model asks nine separate questions about the writing. Six come from Graphite's research on AI tells; the last three are feed habits we added. One spike isn't enough: slop usually shows several at once.

  • Scroll-stopper

    A dramatic one-line opener, a gap, then one-line paragraphs walking to a moral. Built to stop the thumb, not to say anything.

    e.g. I got rejected 47 times. / Then everything changed.
  • Engagement bait

    Asks for the reaction instead of earning it: comment a keyword, repost to help your network, follow for part two.

    e.g. Comment “GUIDE” and I'll DM you the template.
  • Hype words

    Marketing vocabulary standing in for a claim. Big verbs, no numbers.

    e.g. This game-changing framework will unlock unprecedented growth.
  • Empty praise

    Calls something meaningful or profound and never says what it did.

    e.g. An incredibly meaningful, truly transformative experience.
  • No-catch promises

    All benefit, no cost. Real work has trade-offs; this writing never does.

    e.g. Scale your output without sacrificing quality or hiring.
  • “Not X, but Y”

    Defines things by what they aren't, over and over, so every line sounds like a revelation.

    e.g. Leadership isn't a title. It's a mindset.
  • Flowery prose

    Flourish in place of the plain claim.

    e.g. Growth is a delicate dance between vision and humility.
  • Stiff phrasing

    Stock transitions and hedges that make a sentence longer and say less.

    e.g. Furthermore, this approach may provide additional benefits.
  • Too-tidy story

    An anecdote that exists only to land exactly on its lesson.

    e.g. A junior employee once asked me a question I'll never forget.

three counter-signals

The value of humanity, included

Useful beats authentic. Three counter-signals sit beside every verdict, and they can only pull a score down. A useful post survives the mop however it was written.

Sounds like a personasked of the model

One particular person, not anyone

Specific detail from the writer's own life or work, phrasing that's clearly theirs, uneven rhythm. “I” and “my” alone don't count. It takes a little off the score.

Useful to readershalf the shield

Something you could use today

Facts, numbers, a named example, a step to follow. Who or what wrote it doesn't matter: useful AI-assisted writing scores as well as useful writing typed by hand.

Reader responsecounted, not asked

What readers actually did

Comments and reposts count for far more than one-tap reactions, and hundreds of reactions with no discussion is discounted as hollow. The other half of the shield.

The score is the weight of the tells, shielded by what's useful and human.

no judgment of people

Judges the writing. Never the writer.

Slop Mop has no opinion about you, and none about the person who wrote the post. It never learns who anyone is, keeps no copy of what they wrote, and tells nobody what it thought.

  • No names leave your browser. Not yours, not the author's. Only the post text and its public counts are sent to be judged.
  • Posts aren't stored. The server keeps a one-way hash so a post isn't judged twice. The words themselves are never kept.
  • No profiles, of anyone. No score is ever attached to a person, and no browsing history or LinkedIn profile data is collected.
  • Nothing is reported. No author is flagged, muted or told. A verdict only changes what you see, in your own browser.
  • Your votes are anonymous. They're tallied under a salted hash of your install, enough to dedupe, not enough to identify you.

faq

Questions? We've mopped up a few.

What is Slop Mop?

Slop Mop is a free browser extension that reads the posts in your feed before you reach them, judges the writing, and folds away what reads like slop. Nothing is deleted: a folded post reopens with one click, and every verdict shows the signs behind it.

Today it works on LinkedIn in desktop Chrome and Chromium browsers. X, Reddit, Facebook, Substack, Medium, email and the rest of the web are coming.

Is Slop Mop an AI detector?

No. Slop Mop is a bad-writing detector. AI-assisted writing can be excellent and human writing can be spectacular slop, so it asks a more useful question: is this worth your attention?

How does it decide whether a post is slop?

An AI model designed for calibrated decisions answers twelve questions about each post in parallel: nine tells of slop, two counter-signals (does it sound like a person, is it useful to readers) and how likely it is to be human-typed. Reader response, counted from reactions, comments and reposts, is the third counter-signal.

The answers become one score out of 100. Counter-signals can only lower it. How the score adds up.

Does it block, delete, mute or report posts?

No. Slop Mop only changes what you see, locally in your browser. Hide folds a post into a strip you can reopen; Highlight leaves it visible and tints its mop icon yellow or red. Nothing is sent to LinkedIn and no author is ever reported.

What happens when it gets one wrong?

Click the mop beside the post and vote: Not sure, No, Maybe or Probably. Your vote overrides the score in your feed, and everyone's votes feed into how the questions get tuned. It will be wrong sometimes. That's why it shows its working and gives you the last word.

Is it free?

Yes. Slop Mop is free to install and free to use on LinkedIn, up to 250 checks a day. Slop Mop XL, a paid subscription for every other feed, articles and email, is coming soon. It will renew automatically until you cancel, and you can cancel any time.

What leaves my browser?

The post text and its public engagement counts go to api.slopmop.lol to be judged. Your name and the author's name are never sent. The server keeps a one-way hash of the text, not the text. The privacy policy lists all five things it stores.

Is Slop Mop open source?

The original version of the extension and judging server is MIT licensed and public on GitHub. Everything about it, and the link to the code, is on the open-source page.
