How to see fewer suggested posts on Facebook

No switch turns suggested posts off, but Not interested, Hide, snooze and the Feeds tab cut them down. What each does, on app and desktop.

Facebook has no setting that switches suggested posts off. What you can do is tell it, post by post, what you don't want: Not interested on a post you'd rather not see, Hide in the app, and a 30-day snooze for a Page or group you're sick of. For a feed with no suggestions at all, open the Feeds tab, which shows only recent posts from people, Pages and groups you follow.

Why Facebook suggested posts fill your feed

Facebook ranks your main feed with machine learning that predicts what you're likely to find valuable, based on what you've watched, liked and commented on, plus signals such as how many reactions a post already has (Facebook Help). Posts from accounts you don't follow are part of that mix. In a 2024 study of AI-generated image spam, researchers Renee DiResta and Josh Goldstein worked through Meta's own Widely Viewed Content Reports and found that views of "unconnected posts" (from Pages people didn't follow) rose from 8% of Feed views in mid-2021 to 24% in the third quarter of 2023 (arXiv).

That has a practical consequence: every time you stop to stare at a strange post, even in disbelief, you're telling the ranking system you were interested. Feedback you give on purpose is the only way to correct it.

Before you start

  • Use the post's own menu. Nearly every control below lives in the three-dot menu at the top right of a post. Facebook calls it Options in its help pages.
  • Labels move. On 13 October 2026 Meta said it had updated that menu and was rolling out new feedback tools (Meta). If a label below doesn't match what you see, look for the nearest equivalent; the menu may be labelled differently on your account.
  • Ads are separate. Sponsored posts have their own ad preferences. This guide covers posts.

1. Tap Not interested on posts you don't want

This is the main tool, and it works on suggested posts and on posts from people you follow.

  1. Find a suggested post you don't want more of.
  2. Tap or click the three dots at the top right of the post.
  3. Choose Not interested.

Facebook says this tells it you'd like to see fewer posts like this one, and that it now replaces the old Hide post option with "stronger personalization" (Facebook Help). The opposite, Interested, asks for more like it (Facebook Help).

Meta's October 2026 announcement says that after you choose Interested or Not interested, Facebook may ask a follow-up question so you can say, in your own words, what you disliked (Meta). If it asks, be specific. "AI pictures of cabins" is more use than "boring".

2. In the app, tap Hide

On the Facebook app, some posts show a Hide control at the top right, next to the three dots. Tapping it removes the post, and Undo brings it back (Facebook Help). Facebook's desktop instructions only describe the three-dot menu, so on a computer use Not interested instead.

3. Snooze a Page or group that keeps turning up

If the same suggested Page or group keeps appearing, one post at a time is slow.

  1. Open the three dots on one of its posts.
  2. Choose Snooze [Name] for 30 days.

You won't see its posts in your feed for 30 days, and it isn't notified (Facebook Help). Snoozing is a good test: if you don't miss it in a month, you won't miss it at all.

4. Tell Your algorithm what you want

Meta's newest tool, Your algorithm, is a place to add and manage the topics you want in Feed and Reels, and Meta says you find it in a post's three-dot menu (Meta). It was announced on 13 October 2026 and is rolling out, so it may not be on your account yet. Its own help pages weren't available when we checked, so we can't give exact steps. If you have it, add the topics you actually come to Facebook for; the suggestions it shows you should lean towards them.

5. Use the Feeds tab when you want none

When you want a session with no suggestions, skip the main feed. The Feeds tab shows recent posts from the people, Pages and groups you follow, newest first, with no personal ranking (Facebook Help).

  • App: tap the menu at the top left, then Feeds.
  • Desktop: in the left-hand menu of your Home feed, click Feeds. If it isn't there, click See more first.

The catch: when you close and reopen the app, Facebook goes back to the ranked feed. A later guide in this series covers getting the most out of Feeds and Favorites.

What to mark first

You'll get the biggest change for the least effort by spending your Not interested taps on the posts that are built to make you stop. Those are the ones the ranking system learns most from, because they're the ones people pause on.

  • Unexplained images from Pages you've never heard of. Impossible cabins, carvings and cakes with captions such as "Made it with my own hands" were a staple of the AI image spam in the DiResta and Goldstein study (arXiv).
  • Posts that ask you to react, type a word or share. That's engagement bait, and Facebook already demotes it, so marking it reinforces a signal it uses.
  • Hooks with nothing behind them. "You won't believe what happened next" with no detail is a scroll-stopper.

This grandad carved this by hand and nobody noticed 😢 Can I get a well done?

Our village hall is open again from Saturday 1 November. The car park is still closed, so use the school one on Mill Lane.

The first is a suggested post that wants a reaction. The second is from a group you joined and tells you something you can use.

If that doesn't work

  • Give it a few days. Feedback changes ranking over time. A handful of taps won't clear a feed that took months to drift.
  • Stop engaging with the junk, even to complain. A comment saying "this is fake" is still a comment.
  • Check what you follow. Some posts that look suggested are from groups you joined years ago or from friends' activity, such as comments on public posts (Facebook Help). Those need unfollow or snooze, covered in a later guide.
