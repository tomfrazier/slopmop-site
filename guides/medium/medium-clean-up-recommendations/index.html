How to clean up your Medium recommendations

Fix a Medium feed full of filler with show less like this, topic muting, reading history and the Refine recommendations page.

Medium builds your For you feed from what you follow and what you read, so the fastest fixes are to tell it what you don't want and to remove the reads that misled it. Use show less like this on bad stories, mute whole topics you never want, and prune your reading history on the Refine recommendations page. Expect the feed to change over a few days of use, not instantly.

Before you clean up your Medium recommendations

You need to be signed in. Everything below works in a desktop browser, and most of it works in the Medium app too. Where the app and the website differ, the steps say so. Medium changes its interface from time to time, so a button may be labelled or placed slightly differently from what's described here.

It helps to know where recommendations come from. According to Medium's help page on the homepage, For you shows top stories based on your personalised recommendations, which are shaped by the topics, writers and publications you follow. Your reading history feeds into it as well. A single click on a clickbait title can bring a week of similar titles, which is why the reading history step matters more than people expect.

1. Use show less like this on the stories you don't want

This is the quickest signal and the one to use every time you scroll past something that wasted your attention.

  1. On your homepage, find the story preview you want less of.
  2. Look beside the preview for the show less control. Medium's help page describes it as a minus button near the story preview on desktop; Medium's May 2026 announcement calls it a thumbs-down button. You may see either, depending on your version.
  3. Click it. The story disappears from your feed and Medium records that you'd like fewer stories like it.

Use it on the exact stories that annoy you, rather than on stories you're simply not in the mood for. If you thumb down every good piece you're too busy to read, the feed has no way to tell those apart from filler.

2. Mute a topic you never want to see

Since May 2026, Medium lets you mute topics as well as writers and publications. This is the stronger tool when a whole subject keeps flooding in, say crypto get-rich stories or "10 AI tools" pieces under a Productivity tag.

  1. Press the thumbs-down button on a story tagged with the topic you're tired of.
  2. Choose the topic from the list of tags that appears and mute it.
  3. Alternatively, open a story, use its three-dot menu, and mute one of its topics from there.

Medium's writer newsletter says you can also mute a topic from the topic's own page, or by hovering over the topic button at the bottom of a story on desktop. Medium says muting one topic shifts around 10% of the stories in an average feed from that topic to other recommendations, so a couple of well-chosen mutes can make a visible difference.

Be careful with broad topics. Muting "Technology" because of one flood of AI listicles will also remove the good engineering write-ups you came for. Mute the narrowest topic that covers the problem.

3. Open the Refine recommendations page

Most of the remaining controls live on one page.

  1. On desktop, click your profile picture in the top right corner.
  2. Choose Settings, then Refine recommendations. In some versions the page is linked directly from the profile menu.
  3. In the app, open your profile, then Settings, and look for the same option. The wording in the app may differ slightly.

The page has four tabs: Suggestions, Following, Reading history and Muted. The next three steps use them.

4. Prune your reading history

This is where most bad feeds are fixed.

  1. Open the Reading history tab.
  2. Scroll through the list and look for stories you opened by accident, rage-read or regretted: the sensational headline, the article that turned out to be an advert, the listicle you clicked to see how bad it was.
  3. Remove each one from your history. Medium's 2021 announcement of this page says removing a story lowers the chance of being recommended similar stories.

If your feed is beyond saving, clear the whole history. Medium describes this as resetting your recommendations altogether, and the feed then rebuilds from what you follow and what you read next.

5. Check what you follow

The Following tab lists the writers, publications and topics you follow. Follows made in a burst on the day you joined are often the root of a feed you don't recognise. Unfollow anything you'd no longer choose today. Following is the main positive signal Medium uses, so a short, deliberate list does more than a long one.

6. Review the Muted tab

The Muted tab shows the writers and publications you've muted. It's worth a look if a favourite writer seems to have vanished; you may have muted them by mistake. You can unmute from here.

What to expect afterwards

Changes don't all land at once. A topic mute removes stories after the feed refreshes. Reading history changes and show less signals adjust what's recommended next, so give it a few days of normal reading.

Keep a light touch going forward. Each time you open a story and it turns out to be filler, back out and use show less on it from the feed. Each time you finish a story that taught you something, follow the writer. Those two habits matter more than any single clean-up.

If that doesn't work

Some readers report that show less like this has little visible effect on its own, especially when the same story keeps reappearing. When that happens:

  • Mute the topic rather than the story, as in step 2.
  • Mute the writer or publication if one source is responsible for most of the filler. Medium lets you do this from the three-dot menu on a story preview.
  • Switch to the Following feed in the homepage sidebar for a while. It shows stories only from writers and publications you follow, newest first, with no recommendations mixed in.

None of these controls judge the writing itself. They remove sources and subjects.
