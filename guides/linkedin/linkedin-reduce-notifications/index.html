How to reduce LinkedIn notifications

Turn off notification types at the source, pick in-app, push or email for each, and stop newsletter alerts you never asked for.

The fastest fix is on the Notifications tab itself: click the three dots next to any notification you don't want and choose Show less like this, or Change notification preferences to switch that type off. For a full clear-out, go to Settings & Privacy, then Notifications, and work through each category, choosing in-app, push or email for every type you keep.

Before you reduce LinkedIn notifications

LinkedIn can reach you three ways, and each is set separately (LinkedIn Help):

  • In-app: the badge on the Notifications tab, on the website and in the app.
  • Push: pop-ups on your phone. These only work while in-app notifications for that type are on.
  • Email: sent to your primary address, with a frequency you can set for some types.

Turning off push alerts won't stop the emails, and unsubscribing from an email won't clear the badge. Decide which channel you want for each type before you start flicking switches.

A useful rule of thumb: keep anything a person sent you directly (messages, invitations, comments on your posts, mentions) and be ruthless with anything about other people's activity.

1. Triage from the Notifications tab

Spend five minutes on the tab itself. Each notification you act on tells LinkedIn what to send less of.

On desktop:

  1. Click the Notifications icon at the top of the LinkedIn homepage.
  2. Click the More icon (three dots) next to a notification you don't want.
  3. Choose one of:
    • Show less like this to reduce similar notifications.
    • Change notification preferences to switch that type Off.
    • Delete notification to remove just that one.

In the app, tap the Notifications icon in the navigation bar, then the three dots next to the notification. The options are the same, and you can also swipe left on a notification to delete it (LinkedIn Help).

LinkedIn notes that the options vary by notification type, so not every one offers all three.

2. Reduce LinkedIn notifications by category in Settings

This is where the bulk of the noise goes.

On desktop:

  1. Click your profile picture (the Me icon), then Settings & Privacy.
  2. Click Notifications in the left rail.
  3. Under Notifications you receive, click a category.
  4. Click each type and choose whether you want it in-app, as a push notification, by email, or not at all.

In the app, tap your profile picture, then Settings & Privacy, then Notifications, and the steps are the same (LinkedIn Help). On the mobile website, LinkedIn's help says the Settings icon sits at the upper right after you tap your profile photo.

The category names have changed over the years; current help pages mention Connecting with others and Posting and commenting among them (LinkedIn Help). Yours may be labelled differently. If you only use LinkedIn for work news, the types usually worth switching off are updates about other people's activity, news and recommended articles, and offers and tips from LinkedIn itself (LinkedIn Help).

3. Set email frequency

Inside the same settings, some email types let you pick how often they arrive as well as whether (LinkedIn Help). Where offered, a weekly digest is usually enough for anything that isn't a message or an invitation.

For a single email you never want again, use the Unsubscribe link in its footer. That's often quicker than finding the matching toggle, and it acts on the type of email you're holding.

4. Stop newsletter alerts

LinkedIn newsletters send push, in-app and email notifications for each new edition. A few rules from LinkedIn's newsletter FAQ save confusion (LinkedIn Help):

  • To leave one newsletter, open its page and click Subscribed near the top, or click the More icon on a new-edition notification and choose Unsubscribe.
  • To see all your subscriptions, click My Network, then Newsletters under Manage my network on the left.
  • The Unsubscribe link at the bottom of a newsletter email stops emails from every newsletter you subscribe to. You stay subscribed on LinkedIn, so the in-app alerts keep coming.
  • Invitations to subscribe are sent automatically when you follow someone who has a newsletter. To stop those, click the More icon on the invitation notification and choose Turn off.

5. Mute noisy message threads

Group chats and recruiter threads can generate more alerts than everything else combined. You can mute a single conversation without leaving it (LinkedIn Help).

On desktop:

  1. Click the Messaging icon at the top of the homepage.
  2. Open the conversation.
  3. Click the More icon in its upper-right corner and choose Mute.

In the app, tap the Messaging icon, swipe left on the conversation, tap More, then Mute. A muted icon appears next to it in your message list. Repeat the steps and choose Unmute to undo it.

What to keep switched on

Some notifications earn their keep even when you're cutting hard:

  • Messages and connection invitations, unless you check LinkedIn daily anyway.
  • Comments and mentions on your own posts, if you post. Conversations under a post are hard to rejoin once they've gone cold.
  • Job alerts you set up yourself, if you're looking.

If that doesn't work

  • A type you turned off keeps arriving. Check it isn't arriving by a different channel. A type switched off for push can still arrive by email.
  • Notifications stopped altogether. LinkedIn's troubleshooting page suggests resetting two settings: under Connecting with others, open Updates from your network, and under Posting and commenting, open Comments and reactions. Switch each toggle off and on again, or on and off again if it was off. Push only works when in-app notifications are on (LinkedIn Help).
  • You want a type back. Click the Notifications icon, then Manage notifications in the left pane, find the type and switch it On (LinkedIn Help).
