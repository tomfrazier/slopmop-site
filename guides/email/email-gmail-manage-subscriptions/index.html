How to unsubscribe from newsletters in Gmail with Manage subscriptions

Gmail's Manage subscriptions page lists your mailing lists in one place. How to use it, what unsubscribing does, and when to block instead.

6 min read · steps checked 18 october 2026

Gmail has a page called Manage subscriptions that lists the senders you're on mailing lists for, with how many emails each one sent recently and an Unsubscribe button beside each. On a computer it's under More in the left-hand menu; in the Android and iPhone apps it's in the Menu at the top left. Unsubscribing there removes you from all of that sender's active lists, though it can take a few days to stop.

Before you start

  • It may not be on your account yet. Google's help page says the feature is "gradually rolling out and may not be available to you yet". If you can't find it, skip to "If that doesn't work" below.
  • Unsubscribing and blocking do different things. Unsubscribing asks the sender to stop. Blocking leaves you on the list and sends everything they mail to Spam. Google's own advice is to unsubscribe from newsletters you don't want, and keep blocking for senders you don't want to hear from at all.
  • Decide what you're keeping first. Receipts, bank statements, security alerts and anything your job depends on should never go in the cull. Most of those don't come through mailing lists anyway, but a quick look saves you hunting for a password reset next month.

Use Gmail Manage subscriptions on a computer

  1. Open Gmail in your browser.
  2. In the left-hand menu, click More, then Manage subscriptions.
  3. Look down the list. Each sender shows how many emails they've sent recently. Click a sender's name or picture to see all their emails before you decide.
  4. To leave, click Unsubscribe on the right, next to the email count.
  5. If a pop-up appears, some senders want you to finish on their website. Choose Go to website to do that, or Block if you'd rather not visit their site.

Use Gmail Manage subscriptions on Android, iPhone or iPad

  1. Open the Gmail app.
  2. Tap Menu at the top left, then Manage subscriptions.
  3. Tap a sender's name to review their emails, or tap Unsubscribe next to the sender to leave.
  4. If the sender wants you on their website, you'll get the same choice: Go to website or Block.

The steps are the same on both phone platforms, according to Google's help page. Your menu may sit in a slightly different place if your app is out of date.

Unsubscribe from a single email

If you're already reading a newsletter you want gone, you don't need the list.

  1. Open the email.
  2. Click Unsubscribe next to the sender's name.
  3. In the pop-up, click Unsubscribe (or Go to website if that's what's offered).

Google notes that this route may only take you off one of the sender's lists. A shop that sends a weekly offer, a monthly digest and "new arrivals" might be three lists. Manage subscriptions removes you from all the active ones at once, so it's the better route for senders you want out of your life entirely.

What happens afterwards

  • The emails don't stop at once. Senders can take a few days to process the request. Google says you may keep getting messages in the meantime.
  • New mail from that sender goes to Spam. Once you've unsubscribed in Manage subscriptions, Gmail sends any new emails from them to your Spam folder with a banner explaining why. If you change your mind, open one and click Report not spam.
  • Old emails stay put. Unsubscribing doesn't delete anything. If you want the backlog gone, open the sender from the list, select their emails and delete or archive them.

Why the button is there at all

Since 1 February 2024, Google has required anyone sending more than 5,000 messages a day to Gmail accounts to support one-click unsubscribe on marketing and subscribed messages, as well as a visible unsubscribe link in the body. That rule is why large senders mostly behave when you click. Small senders below that volume may not offer the button, so you'll need the link at the bottom of their email instead.

How to choose what to cut

Gmail gathers the list; the judgement is yours. A few questions that make it quicker:

  • When did you last open one? If you can't remember, that's your answer.
  • Is it written for you or for the open rate? A subject line built from hype words and urgency ("Last chance!", "You won't believe…") usually means the inside is a sales page.
  • Does it ever tell you something you couldn't get elsewhere? A local council update, a trade body's notice or a writer you'd pay for earns its place. A brand's fifth discount email of the week doesn't.
  • Does it promise without a cost? Newsletters that are all upside, every week, with no trade-offs mentioned, are running on no-catch promises.

An example of the difference:

slop

Subject: 🚀 Don't miss out! Your exclusive offer ends TONIGHT
Hi there! We've handpicked game-changing deals just for you. Unlock savings like never before!

fine

Subject: Price change from 1 November, and how to keep the old rate
Your plan goes from £8 to £9 a month on 1 November. If you switch to annual before then, you keep £96 a year.

The second one is still marketing. It's worth keeping because it tells you something with a date and a number on it.

If that doesn't work

  • No Manage subscriptions in the menu. The rollout hasn't reached you. Use the Unsubscribe button inside each email instead, or the link at the bottom of the message.
  • No Unsubscribe button next to the sender's name. Gmail only shows it when the sender supports it. Look for an unsubscribe link at the bottom of the email.
  • The email looks suspicious. Don't click its unsubscribe link. Google's phishing guidance is to avoid clicking links in messages from untrustworthy or unknown senders. Block the sender or report the message as spam instead.
  • They keep emailing after a week. Block them. In an open message, click More next to Reply, then Block with the sender's name. Their future emails go to Spam. You can review and unblock senders in Settings, See all settings, Filters and blocked addresses.
