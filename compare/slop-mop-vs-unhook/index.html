Slop Mop vs Unhook

Unhook switches off whole parts of YouTube. Slop Mop reads LinkedIn posts one at a time and folds the low-value ones.

on this page At a glance What each one does Where they differ When to use Unhook When to use Slop Mop Using both

Unhook and Slop Mop both make a site quieter, but they work on different sites and at different sizes. Unhook switches off whole sections of YouTube. Slop Mop goes through a LinkedIn feed one post at a time.

What each one does

Unhook is a browser extension that removes distractions from YouTube. From its popup you toggle what to hide: the homepage feed, the recommended sidebar, Shorts, end-screen suggestions, comments, the trending tab, autoplay and a long list of smaller parts of the page (Chrome Web Store). It runs on Chrome, Firefox and Edge, works on video embeds, and also works on mobile YouTube through Firefox on Android. It's free, and the developer accepts donations (Unhook).

Slop Mop is a Chrome and Chromium extension that reads each LinkedIn post as you scroll. An AI model designed for calibrated decisions answers twelve questions about the writing: nine tells of slop, such as engagement bait and hype words, and three counter-signals, such as whether the post sounds like a person. Each post gets a score out of 100. Hide mode folds likely slop into a one-line strip you can reopen; Highlight mode marks it without hiding anything. LinkedIn is live today. X, Reddit, Facebook, Substack, Medium, websites and email are coming soon. YouTube isn't on that list.

Where they differ

Site. This is the big one. Unhook is for YouTube only. Slop Mop is for LinkedIn only today. If your problem is on YouTube, Slop Mop can't help, and if it's on LinkedIn, Unhook can't.

Sections versus posts. Unhook works on the layout. It decides nothing about an individual video; it removes the place recommendations would appear. Slop Mop works on content. It leaves LinkedIn's layout alone and makes a call about each post: fold it, mark it, or leave it.

Kind of distraction. YouTube's pull is mostly the next thing: the sidebar, autoplay, Shorts. Unhook cuts those off, which goes straight at the habit. LinkedIn's problem, for many readers, is the quality of what's in front of them: scroll-stopper hooks, bait and filler between useful posts. Slop Mop is built for that.

Control. Unhook's controls are plain switches, and what you see is exactly what you chose. That's easier to trust than any model. Slop Mop gives you more to tune (two modes, three sensitivity levels, a vote on every post) because it's making judgement calls that can be wrong. It shows its reasons when you click the mop icon, and says Not sure and leaves a post alone when it isn't confident.

Privacy. Unhook hides parts of the page in your browser. Slop Mop has to send post text (never your name or the author's) to its scoring service; the server keeps a one-way hash of the text and the scores, not the text itself, except for posts where you vote against the score.

Browsers and price. Unhook covers Chrome, Firefox and Edge, for free. Slop Mop covers Chrome and Chromium browsers such as Brave and Opera; Edge, Safari and Firefox are coming soon. It's free for one feed with 250 checks a day, and Slop Mop XL, coming soon, will be US$5.99 a month or US$59.99 a year.

When to use Unhook

  • You go to YouTube for one video and come back an hour later.
  • Shorts, autoplay or the recommended sidebar are what keep you watching.
  • You want to keep subscriptions and search but lose the homepage feed.
  • You'd like the same setup on Firefox or Edge, or on Android through Firefox.

When to use Slop Mop

  • You read LinkedIn for work and want the posts worth reading to stand out.
  • You want each post judged on its writing, including posts from people you follow.
  • You'd like to see why something was folded, and to overrule it.

Using both

There's nothing to choose between here if you use both sites: Unhook on YouTube, Slop Mop on LinkedIn. They don't overlap or interfere.

If you'd rather remove a social feed entirely, the way Unhook removes YouTube's homepage, Slop Mop vs News Feed Eradicator covers that approach. If Facebook is the site you want to tame with rules and switches, see Slop Mop vs Social Fixer.
