Slop Mop vs browser reading modes and AI summarisers

Reading modes and summarisers tidy or shorten what you've opened; Slop Mop screens a feed first and folds what isn't worth opening.

Reading modes, summarisers and Slop Mop all promise less time wasted on text, so they can look like rivals. They act at different moments. Slop Mop works in the feed, before you've opened anything, and decides what's worth your attention. Reading modes and summarisers work after you've picked something, and change how it's presented.

Reading modes, AI summarisers and Slop Mop: what each one does

Browser reading modes strip a page down to its text. Chrome's version, according to Google's help page, opens in a full-screen view or the side panel, lets you change the typeface, font size, colour theme and spacing, and on a computer can read the text aloud. It isn't available on every website. Other browsers have their own reading views.

AI summarisers condense an article, thread or document into a few lines, so you can decide whether to read the whole thing or skip it. They come built into browsers and AI assistants, and as extensions. Prices range from free to paid.

Slop Mop reads each post in your feeds on LinkedIn, X, Reddit, Facebook, Substack and Medium before you reach it, and judges the writing. It also checks the articles and websites you open, and in Gmail and Outlook it reads what the sender wrote. An AI model designed for calibrated decisions rates nine tells of slop and three counter-signals (sounds like a person, useful to readers, reader response), then gives a score out of 100. Likely slop folds into a one-line strip you can reopen; Highlight mode marks it instead. How it works goes through each question.

Where they differ

One page or a whole feed. A reading mode cleans up one page at a time, the one you've opened. Slop Mop screens every post in the feed as you scroll, so you don't spend clicks on what isn't worth it.

Shortening slop keeps the slop. A summariser condenses whatever you give it. Summarise a post that's all flowery prose and empty praise, and you get a shorter version of nothing, which can be useful in its own way: it shows you quickly that there was little there. Slop Mop takes the other route and folds the post, leaving the posts worth reading exactly as their authors wrote them.

In a world that never stops moving, true leaders pause. They listen. They lead with heart. Because at the end of the day, it's people who make the difference. 💡

example

A summary of that post: "Good leaders listen and value people." Shorter, and still nothing you didn't already know.

On request or automatic. Reading modes and summarisers act when you ask, one item at a time. Slop Mop works automatically on each new post, which saves the step of deciding to check.

Whose words you read. A reading mode keeps the author's text and changes only the layout. A summary is in the tool's own words, and can miss or misstate details, so it's worth checking anything that matters against the original. Slop Mop doesn't rewrite anything. It folds or marks posts, and the ones you read are the originals.

Where they work. Reading modes and summarisers work on most articles and pages, and come free with most browsers. Slop Mop works on six social networks, the articles and websites you read, and Gmail and Outlook, in Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari and Chromium browsers. On an article, Slop Mop tells you whether the writing is worth your time but leaves the layout alone; a reading mode is still the tool for making a page easier on the eye. The free plan covers one feed you pick; websites and the rest come with Slop Mop XL.

Privacy. A built-in reading mode reformats the page in your browser. Many summarisers send text to an AI service, so check each one's terms. Slop Mop sends the text of each new post (never your name or the author's) to its scoring service, which keeps a one-way hash of the text and the scores, not the text.

When to use a reading mode or AI summariser

  • You've already found a long article worth reading and want it cleaner, larger or read aloud.
  • You want a quick sense of a long document before deciding to read it.
  • You want something free that comes with your browser and sends nothing anywhere (a built-in reading mode).

When to use Slop Mop

  • Your time goes on deciding, post after post, that something wasn't worth it.
  • You want the good posts left exactly as their authors wrote them.
  • You read feeds, articles or newsletters and want the screening done before you click.

Using both

They're steps in the same task. Slop Mop decides what's worth reading in the feed; when a post links to a long article, a reading mode makes that article easier to read, and a summariser can tell you whether it's worth the full twenty minutes. Add Slop Mop to your browser and it folds the empty posts before you spend a click on them.
