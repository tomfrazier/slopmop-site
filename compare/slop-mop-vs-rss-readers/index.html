Slop Mop vs RSS and feed readers

Feed readers let you leave the algorithm for hand-picked sources; Slop Mop keeps you on the platform and judges each post's writing.

A feed reader is the classic answer to a noisy feed: stop letting a platform decide what you see, and subscribe to the sources you trust instead. It's a good answer for blogs, news sites and newsletters. It can't do much about a LinkedIn, X or Facebook home feed, which doesn't come out as RSS, and that's where Slop Mop does most of its work.

RSS readers and Slop Mop: what each one does

RSS and feed readers, such as Feedly, collect new articles from sites, blogs and newsletters you subscribe to and show them in one list, usually newest first and outside any platform's algorithm. Some add AI features on top. Feedly, for example, has a Feedly AI menu that includes Mute Filters, which hide articles containing a keyword you pick; as of October 2026 those need a Pro+ or Enterprise plan. Most readers have a free tier, with paid plans for extras.

Slop Mop is a browser extension that reads posts in your feeds on LinkedIn, X, Reddit, Facebook, Substack and Medium before you reach them, and checks the articles you open and the email in Gmail and Outlook. It rates nine tells of slop, from hype words to flowery prose, and three counter-signals, then gives a score out of 100. Likely slop folds into a one-line strip you can reopen, or, in Highlight mode, stays visible with a coloured mop icon.

Where they differ

Leaving versus staying. A feed reader replaces the platform's feed with one you build. Slop Mop leaves you on the platform and cleans up what it shows. If you've had enough of algorithmic feeds, a reader gets you out. If you need to be on LinkedIn for work, hiring or keeping up with contacts, a reader can't stand in for it.

What they can see. A feed reader only sees sources that publish a feed. Your LinkedIn, X and Facebook home feeds aren't among them, so a reader has nothing to offer there. Slop Mop works directly in those feeds.

How they filter. Feed readers filter mostly by source: you chose to subscribe, so everything from that source arrives. Some add keyword filters. Few judge individual articles on how well they're written. Slop Mop judges every post on its writing, including counter-signals such as whether it's useful to readers, and shows why. Click the mop for the score and the strongest signs, and vote if you disagree; your vote overrides the model in your own feed.

Curation is the reader's strength. A well-chosen list of sources often has little slop to begin with, because you picked writers who don't do it. That's a quieter result than any filter can give, at the cost of the time it takes to find and add sources, and of missing whatever you didn't subscribe to.

Coverage. Both now reach long-form writing, in different ways. A feed reader gathers posts from the blogs and newsletters you chose, on any device including your phone. Slop Mop works where the writing already is: Substack and Medium, the articles and websites you read, and newsletters in Gmail and Outlook, as well as the social feeds, in Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari and Chromium browsers on desktop. It doesn't gather or reorder anything, and there's no phone app, so for a calm, chronological reading list the reader is still the better tool. Slop Mop's free plan covers one feed; the rest need Slop Mop XL.

Privacy. A reader fetches public feeds. Slop Mop sends the text of each new post (never your name or the author's) to its scoring service, which stores a one-way hash and the scores, not the text.

When to use an RSS reader

  • You'd rather follow a hand-picked set of blogs, sites and newsletters than any social feed.
  • You want a chronological list with no ranking algorithm.
  • You want your newsletters out of your inbox and in one place.
  • You do a lot of your reading on a phone.

When to use Slop Mop

  • You still scroll LinkedIn, X, Reddit or Facebook, for work or for people, and want the slop folded.
  • You read Substack or Medium in the browser and want padded posts flagged before you commit to them.
  • You want each post judged on its writing, with the reasons shown.
  • You don't want to rebuild your reading from scratch.

Using both

They fit together. Move the blogs and newsletters you value into a reader, where you control the list. Keep LinkedIn, X or Reddit for what only they have, and let Slop Mop handle the posts there. If your reader's keyword filters are doing the heavy lifting, Slop Mop vs keyword filters explains what words can and can't catch. Once you've found a long article worth reading, reading modes and summarisers are the next step. Add Slop Mop to your browser and it folds the slop in the feeds you still scroll.
