Slop Mop vs DeSlop

Two slop filters: DeSlop hunts signs of AI writing on your device, Slop Mop judges whether a post is worth reading.

What DeSlop and Slop Mop each do

DeSlop and Slop Mop are the closest pair on this site. Both are browser extensions, both work on the LinkedIn feed, and both read posts as they appear and quietly get the bad ones out of your way.

DeSlop, made by Pineido, reads the posts on your screen, scores each one for signs of AI writing, and hides, collapses or dims the ones that score as slop. Its Chrome Web Store listing names the habits it goes after: "I'm thrilled to announce" openers, one-sentence-per-line "broetry" and "Agree?" bait (habits we cover under broetry and engagement bait). It has Lenient, Balanced and Strict sensitivity, a "Why hidden" panel that lists the signals that fired, and one-click "Show anyway" and "Always show this person" buttons. By default it leaves your first-degree connections and people you follow alone (you can switch that off). You can train it with "Add to slop", tune individual signals, filter AI-drafted messages and turn on a focus mode. As of October 2026 every feature is free, with an optional US$4.99 one-time contribution that adds no extra features.

Slop Mop sends the text of each new post to its scoring service, where an AI model designed for calibrated decisions answers twelve questions about it: nine tells of slop and three counter-signals (sounds like a person, useful to readers, reader response). The post gets a score out of 100 and a verdict. In Hide mode likely slop folds into a one-line strip you can reopen; in Highlight mode nothing is hidden and the post is marked instead. Sensitivity is Mild, Moderate or Aggressive. Beyond LinkedIn, it does the same on X, Reddit, Facebook, Substack and Medium, on the articles you read, and in Gmail and Outlook. How it works explains the score.

Slop Mop vs DeSlop: where they differ

The question each one asks. DeSlop's headline signal is "signs of AI writing", and its listing is careful to say it flags writing, never people. Slop Mop isn't an AI detector at all. It asks whether the writing is worth your time, so a useful post drafted with AI can pass and a hand-typed post full of bait can be folded. If you want AI-looking posts gone as a category, DeSlop's framing matches that wish more directly.

slop

Most people will scroll past this. The ones who don't will change their year. Comment "READY" and I'll send you my 5-step system 👇

fine

Our support queue dropped from 400 to 260 tickets a week after we rewrote the three help articles people opened most. The old ones answered the wrong question.

Either post could have been written with or without AI. Slop Mop's nine tells are aimed at the gap between those two posts.

Privacy and where it runs. This is DeSlop's clearest advantage. Its listing says it runs in your browser and that nothing about what you read leaves your device; the only network request it can make is to check an optional supporter key. Slop Mop sends post text to its server for scoring. It never sends your name or the author's, and it keeps a one-way hash of the text and the scores rather than the text itself. The exception: when you vote against a score, that post's text is kept so the miss can be reviewed. If keeping everything on your machine matters most, DeSlop wins here.

Price. DeSlop is free with no limits. That's a clear point to DeSlop. Slop Mop is free for one feed with 250 checks a day, and Slop Mop XL costs US$5.99 a month or US$59.99 a year for every feed, the sites you read, Gmail and Outlook, and a higher limit.

How it explains itself. Both show their working. DeSlop lists the signals that fired. Slop Mop shows the score, the strongest signs and the Slopprint, a chart of all nine tells, so you can see what kind of slop a post is as well as how much.

How you overrule it. DeSlop lets you show a post, whitelist a person and teach it with "Add to slop". Slop Mop lets you reopen any folded post and vote on it; your vote overrides the model in your own feed. Slop Mop has no "always show this person" switch, because it judges each post on its own.

Reach. DeSlop is built for LinkedIn. Slop Mop works on LinkedIn, X, Reddit, Facebook, Substack and Medium, the articles and sites you read, and Gmail and Outlook, in Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari and other Chromium browsers. If LinkedIn is the only feed you read, this makes no difference; if the same bait follows you to X or Reddit, it does.

When to use DeSlop

  • AI-written posts are the thing that bothers you, and you'd rather see a clumsy human post than a polished AI one.
  • You want no feed data leaving your browser.
  • You want every feature free, with no daily cap.
  • You like protecting your own network from filtering by default.
  • LinkedIn is the only feed you want cleaned.

When to use Slop Mop

  • You're bothered by hooks, bait, hype and filler whoever wrote them, and you don't mind good AI-assisted posts staying in your feed.
  • You want a per-post breakdown of which tells fired.
  • You want a Highlight mode that marks posts without hiding anything.
  • You want the same judgement on X, Reddit, Facebook, Substack, Medium, the web or your inbox.
  • You're fine with post text (never names) being scored on a server, under the limits described in the privacy policy.

Using both

Running two feed filters on the same page is possible but rarely worth it: they'll compete to hide the same posts, and you won't know which one acted. If you want to compare them, try each for a week on its own.

If your real interest is spotting AI authorship rather than cleaning a feed, the Pangram comparison and the wider look at AI detectors cover that. If you'd like to write your own filter rules, see Slop Mop vs Cringe Guard. To try the other half of the comparison, add Slop Mop to your browser and give it the same week.
