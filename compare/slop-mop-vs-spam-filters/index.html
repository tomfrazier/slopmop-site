Slop Mop vs spam filters

Spam filters catch junk, scams and phishing at the server; Slop Mop judges legitimate mail and posts on their writing. One doesn't replace the other.

It's tempting to think of slop as a new kind of spam, which makes a spam filter look like the obvious comparison. The two overlap less than the nickname suggests. Spam is mail you didn't ask for, much of it bulk or fraudulent. Slop is usually posted by someone you chose to follow, through the normal channels, and the only harm is the minute it takes to realise there's nothing in it.

Spam filters and Slop Mop: what each one does

Spam filters sit on your email provider's servers and move unwanted or dangerous messages (bulk mail, scams, phishing) out of your inbox before you see them. They learn from what people report: Gmail's help pages say that as you report more spam, Gmail identifies similar emails as spam more efficiently. If it gets one wrong, you can mark it Not spam, add the sender to your contacts, or make a filter. Spam filtering comes free with nearly every email service and works on every device you read mail on.

Slop Mop judges the writing of what does reach you. In Gmail and Outlook, through the Slop Mop for Gmail and Slop Mop for Outlook add-ons, it checks what the sender wrote; in your browser it reads posts on LinkedIn, X, Reddit, Facebook, Substack and Medium, and the articles you open. An AI model designed for calibrated decisions rates nine tells, from engagement bait to too-tidy stories, and three counter-signals, and gives each email or post a score out of 100, with the reasons. On feeds, likely slop folds into a one-line strip you can reopen, or, in Highlight mode, stays visible with a coloured mop icon. How it works covers the questions in detail.

Where Slop Mop and spam filters differ

What counts as unwanted. A spam filter is looking for messages that shouldn't have reached you at all. A post from a former colleague announcing that they've "never been more humbled" isn't spam by any definition: it's from a real account you follow, sent in the ordinary way. Nor is the newsletter that takes four paragraphs to reach its one link, or the colleague's update that never gets to a decision. Slop Mop is built for exactly that kind of message. It ignores who sent something and looks at what's written.

slop

Humbled and honoured to share that I've been named a Top Voice. None of this would be possible without YOU. Drop a 🙌 if you're ready to grow in 2027!

fine

We were named a Top Voice for posts on payroll compliance. The two that did best were the checklist for contractors in Germany and the one on holiday accrual errors.

slop

Hi there! I hope this email finds you well. I wanted to reach out to share some truly exciting news about how our innovative solution can transform the way your team works.

fine

Hi Tom, our invoice tool now exports straight to Xero. Your plan includes it at no extra cost; the setting is under Billing, then Integrations.

Safety. This is where a spam filter does something Slop Mop doesn't attempt. Spam filters are a security tool: they catch phishing and fraud. Slop Mop doesn't check links, senders or attachments and isn't a defence against scams. It stays out of the Spam folder altogether: spam, along with private, legal, health and transactional mail, is never read unless you check a message by hand.

Where they run. Spam filtering happens on the server for every message, so it's on everywhere, including your phone, without any setup. Slop Mop runs on desktop, in Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari and Chromium browsers, and in Gmail and Outlook through its add-ons. Before an email is checked, personal details are replaced with placeholders in your mailbox, and only what the sender wrote is sent, never attachments or quoted earlier messages. For feed posts it sends the text (never your name or the author's), and the scoring service keeps a one-way hash and the scores, not the text.

What happens to the item. A spam filter moves mail to a separate Spam folder. Slop Mop never deletes, reports, mutes or blocks anything, and it doesn't move mail between folders. On feeds it folds the post in place, and one click opens it.

How they stack. Slop Mop for Gmail and Outlook sits on top of your spam filter, judging the legitimate mail that gets through. It doesn't replace the filter, and it costs money where the filter doesn't: email is part of Slop Mop XL, at US$5.99 a month or US$59.99 a year. The free plan covers one social feed.

When to rely on your spam filter

Always. It's already switched on, it's free, and it handles junk and scams better than any reading tool could. Gmail's guide to reporting spam shows how to help it learn. If spam is getting through, report it so the filter learns, and use your provider's block and unsubscribe options for senders you no longer want.

When to use Slop Mop

  • Your problem is email and posts from real people and companies that take time and give nothing back: templated cold outreach, padded newsletters, workslop.
  • You want to see why something was judged low-value, and overrule it when you disagree.
  • You read Gmail or Outlook, or your feeds, on a computer.

Using both

They answer different questions, so there's nothing to choose between. The spam filter decides whether a message should reach you at all; Slop Mop decides whether something that legitimately reached you is worth reading.

If your inbox problem is newsletters and low-priority mail more than spam, email triage tools such as SaneBox are the closer comparison. If you're wondering whether Slop Mop works out who, or what, wrote a post, see Slop Mop vs AI detectors. Add Slop Mop for Gmail and Outlook, part of Slop Mop XL, and it flags the legitimate mail that isn't worth your time.
