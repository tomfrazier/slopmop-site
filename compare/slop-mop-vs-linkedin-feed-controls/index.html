Slop Mop vs LinkedIn's own feed controls

LinkedIn's controls act after you've read a post and shape its algorithm over time; Slop Mop judges each post first and shows why.

on this page At a glance What each one does Where they differ When LinkedIn feed controls are enough When to add Slop Mop to LinkedIn feed controls Using both

LinkedIn already gives you ways to shape your feed, and they cost nothing. Before installing anything, it's fair to ask what they do and where they stop. The short version: LinkedIn's controls are feedback you give after reading a post, which its algorithm uses over time. Slop Mop is a judgement on each post before you read it, with the reasons shown.

What each one does

LinkedIn's own controls sit in the menu on each post and in your settings. According to LinkedIn Help, as of October 2026 you can:

  • choose I don't want to see this post and say why;
  • unfollow or mute people and companies whose posts you no longer want;
  • report inappropriate or offensive content;
  • follow new sources to add them to your feed;
  • sort the feed by most relevant or most recent updates, on desktop only.

LinkedIn says your activity on the site affects what its feed shows you. These controls work on the website and in the apps, and there's nothing to install.

Slop Mop is a browser extension for Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari and Chromium browsers. It reads each post in your LinkedIn feed before you reach it, rates nine tells of slop (such as scroll-stopper hooks and engagement bait) and three counter-signals, and gives a score out of 100. In Hide mode, likely slop folds into a one-line strip you can reopen; in Highlight mode, its mop icon turns yellow or red. How it works explains the scoring. It does the same on X, Reddit, Facebook, Substack and Medium, so the same bar applies across your feeds.

Where they differ

Before or after. With LinkedIn's controls, you've already read the post by the time you flag it. That's fine for the odd one, but slop is common enough that flagging each post becomes a chore. Slop Mop reads ahead, so the posts it judges as likely slop are folded before you get to them.

One post or the algorithm. "I don't want to see this post" removes that post and tells LinkedIn something about your taste. What it does to future ranking isn't shown. Slop Mop doesn't touch LinkedIn's ranking at all. It acts on the posts LinkedIn has already chosen to show you, one at a time, in your browser.

Reasons. LinkedIn doesn't explain how your feedback changes what you see. Slop Mop shows its working on every post: click the mop for the score, the strongest signs and the Slopprint, a chart of all nine tells. If you disagree, vote, and your vote overrides the model in your own feed.

Devices. This is where LinkedIn's controls are ahead. They work everywhere you use LinkedIn, including the mobile apps. Slop Mop works where its extension runs: Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari and Chromium browsers such as Brave and Opera, on a computer.

Reporting. LinkedIn's report option is for content that breaks its rules. Slop Mop never reports, deletes, mutes or blocks anything; it only folds or marks posts in your own view. Nobody is told.

Privacy. LinkedIn's controls stay inside LinkedIn. Slop Mop sends the text of each new post to its scoring service, never your name or the author's. The server keeps a one-way hash of the text and the scores, except for posts you vote against, whose text is kept so the miss can be reviewed.

When LinkedIn feed controls are enough

  • You only want to dismiss the occasional post.
  • A particular person or company keeps posting things you don't want: mute or unfollow them.
  • Something breaks LinkedIn's rules: report it.
  • You'd like the newest posts first: sort by most recent on desktop.
  • You mostly read LinkedIn on your phone.

When to add Slop Mop to LinkedIn feed controls

  • You read LinkedIn on a computer and spend too long scrolling past hooks, bait and filler.
  • You want to keep seeing a contact's good posts while their weaker ones fold away.
  • You want to see why each call was made, and overrule it.

Slop Mop is free for one feed with 250 checks a day. Slop Mop XL, at US$5.99 a month, adds every feed, the sites you read, Gmail and Outlook, and a higher daily limit.

Using both

They don't conflict. Keep using LinkedIn's controls for people and topics you've decided against, and for anything that needs reporting. Slop Mop then handles the per-post judgement in between. Sorting by most recent and running Slop Mop together gives you a chronological feed with the slop folded.

Muting is a decision about a whole person, which is a different trade-off; Slop Mop vs unfollowing and muting covers it. If you've had enough of the feed altogether, feed blockers remove it entirely.

If that sounds like your feed, add Slop Mop to your browser and it folds the slop before you reach it. For a full walk-through of LinkedIn's settings, see Slop Mop for LinkedIn.
