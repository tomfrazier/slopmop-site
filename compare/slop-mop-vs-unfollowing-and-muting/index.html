Slop Mop vs unfollowing and muting people

Muting removes everything a person posts, good or bad; Slop Mop judges each post on its writing, so the good ones still get through.

Muting is the bluntest tool for a better feed and often the right one. If someone only ever posts things you don't want, muting them fixes it in two clicks. The trouble is that most people's feeds don't divide that neatly. The former manager whose posts you skip three times out of four also writes the one post a month you'd hate to miss.

Unfollowing, muting and Slop Mop: what each one does

Unfollowing and muting remove all of a person's or company's posts from your feed without disconnecting you from them. Every major social platform offers it, including X, Reddit, Facebook and Substack. On LinkedIn, the menu on any post lets you unfollow or mute the person or company who posted it, and you stay connected. It's free, needs nothing installed, and works on every device you use LinkedIn on.

Slop Mop reads each post in your feed on LinkedIn, X, Reddit, Facebook, Substack or Medium and judges the writing, whoever wrote it. It rates nine tells of slop and three counter-signals, gives a score out of 100, and folds or marks the post depending on your settings. It never mutes, blocks, reports or deletes anything. How it works explains the scoring.

Where Slop Mop differs from unfollowing and muting

People versus posts. Muting is a decision about a person. Slop Mop makes a decision about a piece of writing. That means you can keep someone in your feed and still skip the weaker posts. Here are two posts from the same imaginary contact, a week apart:

slop

I was rejected by 30 investors. Then a stranger on a train changed everything.

Here's what he taught me about resilience 👇

fine

We closed our seed round on 3 October. 30 investors passed; the most common reason was that we hadn't priced the enterprise tier. We've now published it.

Muting this person loses both. Slop Mop is built to fold the first and leave the second. The two posts even share a fact, which is the point: the difference lies in the writing.

Judgement. Muting makes no judgement and can't be wrong about a post, because it never looks at one. Slop Mop does judge, and sometimes it'll call a post differently from how you would. You can click the mop on any post to see the score and the strongest signs, and vote to overrule it in your own feed.

Undoing. A mute lasts until you remember to go back and lift it, and in the meantime you don't see what you're missing. A post folded by Slop Mop stays in place as a one-line strip, and one click opens it. In Highlight mode, nothing is folded at all; the post's mop icon just turns yellow or red.

Devices and reach. Both work on every major network now, but muting still has the edge on devices: it applies in the phone apps as well as on the web, because the platform does it. Slop Mop works on LinkedIn, X, Reddit, Facebook, Substack and Medium in desktop browsers (Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari and Chromium browsers), so a post it folds on your laptop still shows in the app on your phone. The free plan covers one feed; Slop Mop XL, at US$5.99 a month, covers them all. The same habit turns up everywhere: the X account you follow for one good thread a month, the subreddit regular whose comments are hit and miss, the Facebook friend who shares one bait post for every photo of their garden.

Fairness. Muting someone is a judgement of them, even if they never know. Slop Mop judges writing, not people, and it doesn't care whether AI helped write a post. A contact who uses an AI tool to write something useful still gets through. The same contact posting a hook and a request for comments by hand doesn't.

Privacy. Muting happens inside the platform. Slop Mop sends the text of each new post, never your name or the author's, to its scoring service, which keeps a one-way hash of the text and the scores rather than the post.

When to unfollow or mute

  • One person or company consistently posts things you don't want, whatever the writing is like.
  • The issue is the subject they post about, and that won't change.
  • You want the change to apply everywhere, including the mobile app.
  • You don't want to install anything.

When to use Slop Mop

  • The people in your feed are worth keeping, but a share of their posts aren't.
  • You've muted so many people that your feed feels thin.
  • You'd rather the call was made on each post, with the reasons shown.
  • You follow the same people on several networks and want one approach that works on all of them.

Using both

Most people will end up with both. Mute the handful of sources you've given up on; let Slop Mop handle everyone else, post by post. If what you want to avoid is a topic rather than a person, keyword filters do that more directly. The other options LinkedIn builds in, such as sorting and "I don't want to see this post", are covered in Slop Mop vs LinkedIn's own feed controls, and Slop Mop for Facebook covers the controls Facebook offers. Add Slop Mop to your browser and it folds the weak posts from people you'd rather keep.
