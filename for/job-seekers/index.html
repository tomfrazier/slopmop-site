Slop Mop for job seekers: a LinkedIn feed filter, plus Reddit and email

For people looking for work: mark the career-advice bait on LinkedIn, Reddit and email, and keep job posts and useful advice in view.

What your feed looks like

When you're looking for work, the internet changes around you. LinkedIn starts showing you more career advice, more "I'm hiring" posts and more people selling help with the search. Reddit's career and industry subreddits fill your feed with interview stories and CV reviews. Your inbox gets job alerts, recruiter messages and newsletters from coaches. Search for "how to answer weaknesses question" and you land on pages that take 1,500 words to say "be honest".

Some of it is useful. A lot of it is written to make you anxious enough to comment, follow or buy. The common types:

  • CV and interview "secrets" offered in exchange for commenting a keyword.
  • Courses and coaching that promise interviews within weeks, with no mention of the cost or who it didn't work for.
  • Stories of someone who sent 300 applications, got nothing, then changed one thing and got five offers. These turn up on LinkedIn and Reddit alike.
  • Motivational posts about rejection that end with a call to like if you agree.
  • Recruiter emails that praise your "impressive background" without naming anything in it.

What you need is narrower and less exciting: actual job posts with a salary and a location, hiring managers saying what their team is building, people in your field describing how their company's interview process works, and the occasional honest post about a search that went badly.

Examples

slop

I applied to 312 jobs and heard nothing. Then I changed ONE line on my CV. 5 interviews in 2 weeks. 🤯 Comment "CV" and I'll send you the exact template. 👇

fine

Our interview loop for product designers is a 30-minute chat with the recruiter, a portfolio review with two designers, and a final with the head of design. No take-home. We aim to give feedback within five working days.

slop

Land your dream job in 30 days. No experience? No problem. No network? No problem. My method works for everyone, in any industry, at any level.

fine

The career sessions I run are £60 for an hour. They work best for people moving between similar roles. If you're changing field entirely, a course or a contact in that field will probably help you more.

The first slop example stacks a scroll-stopper on top of engagement bait. The second is a no-catch promise: all benefit, no cost, no limit. The fine versions tell you something you can check or act on, including the limits.

How Slop Mop helps job seekers

Slop Mop is a browser extension for Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari and Chromium browsers such as Brave and Opera, on desktop. It reads each post before you reach it and scores the writing against nine tells of slop and three counter-signals. One of the counter-signals is "useful to readers": specific facts, named examples, a method someone could follow. A job post with a salary band scores well on that, however plain it is. How it works covers the rest.

Where a search happens:

  • LinkedIn. It reads the posts in your feed and marks or folds the bait. LinkedIn lets you hide a post or unfollow its author, which is worth doing for accounts that only ever post bait; Slop Mop handles the rest one post at a time.
  • Reddit. It reads posts and comments in your feed, so a thread of interview stories shows the first-hand ones and folds the recycled ones.
  • Websites. It reads the career-advice articles you open and marks the padded ones.
  • Email. Slop Mop for Gmail and Outlook checks what the sender wrote in recruiter messages and coaching newsletters. Personal details are replaced with placeholders before anything is checked, and attachments and quoted earlier messages are never sent.

It judges the writing, not who wrote it, and it isn't an AI detector. A recruiter who used AI to draft a clear, specific job ad isn't penalised for it. It never deletes, reports, mutes or blocks anything.

Setting up a LinkedIn feed filter for job seekers

Start with Highlight mode on Mild sensitivity.

This is the gentlest setup, and it suits a search. Job ads are often written in hype-heavy language ("fast-paced", "rockstar", "exciting opportunity"), but a badly written ad can still be a good job. In Highlight mode nothing is hidden. Each post's mop turns yellow for Possibly slop or red for Likely slop, and you decide what to read. Mild flags only the clearest cases, so you'll mostly see the keyword-bait and the miracle-method posts marked.

Once you know what the colours mean in your feed, two changes are worth trying:

  • Move to Moderate if the career-advice bait is still getting through unmarked.
  • Switch to Hide if you find yourself never opening the red posts. Folded posts sit behind a one-line strip, and one click reopens them.

If Slop Mop marks a real job post, click its mop and vote No. Your vote overrides the score in your own feed. Voting isn't available for email.

Choosing a plan:

  • Free covers the one feed you pick, with 250 checks a day. For most searches that's LinkedIn; if your field hires through Reddit communities, pick Reddit instead.
  • Slop Mop XL is US$5.99 a month or US$59.99 a year. It covers every feed, the sites you read, Gmail and Outlook, with a higher daily limit, on up to five browsers. During a search, it's worth it if you're reading LinkedIn and Reddit daily and your inbox is full of alerts. If money is tight, the free plan on your main feed does most of the work.

If you post too

Plenty of job-seekers post an "open to work" update or a write-up of a project. Those posts are read by recruiters and hiring managers who scroll past many of them a day.

  • Lead with the facts: the role you want, where, and one thing you've done that proves you can do it. Skip the dramatic opening line; the scroll-stopper page shows the pattern.
  • Avoid asking people to "like for reach". A clear request ("If you know a team in Bristol hiring data engineers, I'd like an introduction") works better and isn't engagement bait.
  • Describe what you did in plain words. "Cut month-end close from 9 days to 6" says more than "drove transformative process improvements". See hype words.
  • If you used AI to help draft it, or a cover letter, read it back for stock phrases. The stiff phrasing page lists the ones that give it away.

A long search on a noisy feed is tiring. If that's where you are, the AI fatigue entry might be familiar reading. Add Slop Mop to your browser and it marks the career-advice bait so the job posts are easier to find.
